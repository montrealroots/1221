## 1. Degree requirements

- **Residency.** B27 p.21: "Three years in residence is the time required for this work". B27 p.194 sets "six semesters in the three-year track M.F.A. program" and says "The School does not offer a part-time or less-than-half-time status."
- **Award.** The degree goes to students who "complete with distinction any of the programs of study outlined and who exhibit excellence in their professional practice" (p.21). They are "recommended by a majority vote of the faculty" (p.151).
- **Courses.** A fixed "Plan of Study: Acting / Required Sequence" for each year (pp.24–25). Year Three includes DRAM 743b Professional Preparation and Audition Workshop, DRAM 473b Taming the Cyclops (on-camera) and DRAM 793b Actor Showcase. Theater history (DRAM 873a/b), anti-racist practice (DRAM 863b) and the Critical Response Process course (DRAM 4b) are graduation requirements (pp.26, 144–145). **Credit counts: none are printed (unknown).** Grading (p.151): "Except where noted, courses at the School are offered on a Pass/Fail basis."
- **Production work.** Casting is assigned each year "as determined by the chair" (p.25). "Once cast in a role, the student is required to fulfill that casting obligation" (p.146). Failing to be available as an understudy "may be grounds for dismissal" (p.146). Work-study is "a minimum of 200 work-study hours, 150 assigned and 50 elective" (p.147).
- **Thesis.** None is named for Acting (unknown whether anything counts as one). Year Three includes "a semester-long solo verbatim project and development of self-generated performance material" (p.23).
- **Attendance.** Attendance at all classes, calls and productions "is expected and mandatory" (p.24). "Unexcused absences and/or persistent unexcused lateness are behaviors subject to disciplinary action" (p.149). The work periods are term time: "all students are expected to be in attendance" (p.150).
- **Standing.** The steps are a notice of academic concern, then academic warning, then dismissal. "Probation" appears only in passing (p.194) and is never defined.
  - A student "may be issued a notice of academic concern, be placed on academic warning, or be dismissed at any time despite having achieved passing grades in all coursework" (p.151).
  - "If the academic concern is not remediated within one semester, the issue may be escalated to formal academic warning." Also: "Failure to remediate the academic warning within the defined time frame may lead to dismissal" (p.154).
  - Dismissal can come "whether or not they are on academic warning", and "Once dismissed, a student is not eligible for readmission" (p.157).
  - SAP (p.194): a student may be disqualified for "two Failures in any one semester", up to "a total of seven or more courses". Pace must be "at least 75 percent of the cumulative attempted credits".
  - Incompletes are due "within one month of the end of the term". Otherwise the grade becomes a "Permanent Incomplete which does not confer credit" (p.152).
- **Graduation.** "May 24 M University Commencement" (B27 p.6, for 2027). B26 p.6 gives May 18, 2026. Candidates "must be present at the Commencement exercises unless excused for urgent reasons" (p.162). The Registrar (REG) says "Most students' degrees are conferred in May or December" and "Professional School students reach out to their school registrar". **Drama's exact conferral date is unknown.** No December completion is described for Acting.

## 2. Early graduation, acceleration, advanced standing, transfer credit

- **The general rule.** B27 p.21: "on rare occasions the faculty of David Geffen School of Drama may reduce the residency requirement, but in no case below a minimum of two years." B26 has the same wording. **No criteria, procedure or Acting instance is published (unknown).**
- **No transfer credit.** "The David Geffen School of Drama does not accept transfer credits" (p.194). This sentence is new in B27.
- **Individual variation.** "a program may choose to vary specific requirements on an individual basis with the approval of the dean" (p.148).
- **Advanced standing exists elsewhere in the School, not in Acting.**
  - Technical interns who later enroll in an M.F.A. are "sometimes receiving credit toward the degree for requirements already completed" (p.123).
  - Special students "may matriculate as second-year students" (p.22), but special-student status exists only in four other programs.
  - "There is no fellow status affiliated with the Acting program" (p.22).
- **Maximum time.** "students must complete the degree requirements within nine semesters" (p.194).
- **Finding.** The only route to a shorter degree is the faculty's rare reduction, never below two years. No application route or precedent was found for Acting. The fixed sequence puts DRAM 793b in "their final term" (p.33). The one recent change to program length went the other way. PL20: "the three-year MFA program is being extended temporarily to a fourth year", and the extra year "is mandatory for the incoming class of first-year students, who will now graduate in 2024."

## 3. Actor Showcase

- **Course.** DRAM 793b, in Year Three, spring term. B27 p.33: "In their final term, students choose and rehearse scenes, which are presented to agents, managers, casting directors, and other members of the industry in New York and Los Angeles." Faculty assist with scene selection "with input from Ellen Novack and an industry guest". "Paul Mullins directs the showcase performance." B26 p.34 has the same text.
- **Eligibility.** A student with an outstanding Incomplete "must satisfy the course requirements by the fourth week of their final semester and before course DRAM 793b, Actor Showcase, begins" (p.26). The same rule governs taking part in Commencement.
- **Preparation.**
  - DRAM 743b: "Students receive sides to prepare, work with a reader" (p.33).
  - DRAM 473b: filmed monologues are "posted on the School website at the end of the final semester, so casting directors, representatives, and other professionals can experience our students' work on camera". The course includes "meetings with some of the leading professional casting directors, agents, managers, entertainment lawyers" (p.33).
- **2026 (SC26).**
  - New York: "Monday, April 27 at 5PM and 7:30PM / Tuesday, April 28 at 1PM", at "The Robert W. Wilson MCC Theater Space / Frankel Theater".
  - Los Angeles: "Tuesday, May 5 at 1PM, 4:30PM and 7PM", at "Garry Marshall Theatre", Burbank.
  - Industry guests register by email.
- **2025 (SC25).** New York on April 28–29 at "La MaMa Experimental Theatre Club"; Los Angeles on May 6. The New York venue changed for 2026.
- **Calendar.** Derived from B26 p.6: the 2026 showcases fell after "Spring-term classes end" (April 24) and before Commencement (May 18).
- **Afterward.** **Follow-up meetings or signings are not described in any official source (unknown).**
- **Recent changes.**
  - PL20: "neither the School nor Yale Rep will produce a season of plays in 2020–21". The program was given a temporary fourth year.
  - The group photo on SC24 is named "2024DGSD4thYearGroup", which fits a four-year class of 2024.
  - **The 2020 and 2021 showcase formats are unverified.** No official page was found, and a paywalled Hollywood Reporter article (HTTP 402) was not read.

## 4. Professional work while enrolled

- **Acting outside the School.** B27 p.23: "During the academic year, and due in part to the highly interdependent and collaborative nature of the School, permission to act in projects outside the School is rarely given." WEB has older, stronger wording: "strongly discouraged, and permission to do so is rarely given."
- **Outside employment (B27 p.148; rewritten since B26 p.145).**
  - "the School discourages students from pursuing outside paid or unpaid professional work while school is in session."
  - "In rare situations, professionally meaningful, short-term outside work that is communicated to the program chair and dean with timeliness and transparency is approved."
  - "Only students in good standing may proceed with requests for outside work."
  - Steps: consult the chair, then a written proposal (dates, missed classes, assignments, "any visa considerations"), then work-study clearance, then the chair's recommendation, then the dean's written decision.
  - "Accepting and performing outside work without required approval is regarded as a conflict of interest and may lead to academic warning or dismissal."
  - Approved outside work ranks last (7th) among scheduling priorities, and Yale Rep calls rank first (p.142).
  - "A student is not normally granted a leave of absence to take on a professional commitment" (p.158).
- **Summer.** The policy covers work "while school is in session". **There is no explicit rule on summer outside work (unknown).** Students can still be held for recess work: recess pay applies "Whenever a student is required to work during all or part of a School recess, including summer recess" (p.150).
- **Unions.** "Through work at Yale Repertory Theatre, those students who are not members of Actors' Equity will attain membership to the union upon graduation" (p.24). **No rule was found on joining Equity or SAG-AFTRA while enrolled. SAG-AFTRA is not mentioned.**
- **Representation.** Agents and managers are part of the final year (DRAM 473b, DRAM 793b). **No rule was found on seeking or signing with an agent before graduation (unknown).**

## 5. Leaving the program

- **Leave of absence (pp.157–159).**
  - Approved by the chair and the dean, "for one, two, or three terms".
  - Personal leave needs "satisfactory completion of at least one term of study" and is "normally … not approved after the tenth day of a term".
  - On leave, a student "may not, however, fulfill any other degree requirements".
  - To return, the student must "notify the registrar in writing of the intention to return at least eight weeks prior to the end of the approved leave". A student who does not return and gets no extension "is automatically dismissed from the School."
  - International students "must consult with OISS regarding their visa status."
  - "Any approved leave of absence cannot exceed three semesters" (p.194).
- **Not registering.** A student who neither takes leave nor registers "is considered to have withdrawn from the School" (p.159).
- **Withdrawal (p.160).** "Normally, a student or intern who has chosen to withdraw is eligible to apply for readmission. A student or intern who is asked to withdraw by the faculty or is dismissed by the School is not eligible to apply for readmission."
- **Describing incomplete training.** **No rule or norm was found (unknown).** The registrar keeps "academic transcripts of current and former students" (p.162). The misrepresentation clause ("all forms of misrepresentation in academic and professional work", p.155) governs enrolled students.

## 6. F-1 students

- **Outside work also needs OISS.** B27 p.148: "International students who are approved for outside employment, paid or unpaid, must consult with the Office of International Students and Scholars (OISS) to receive proper work authorization before beginning work."
- **On campus.**
  - ONC: on-campus work is work "on the premises of Yale University or for which you receive a Yale paycheck". It allows "up to 20 hours per week" in session and "full-time … during holiday and vacation periods", but "per Yale policy students may not work more than 19 hours (combined for all jobs)".
  - P3600: "Adherence to the 19-hour limit is strictly enforced."
  - **How the cap applies to required work-study and Yale Rep calls is not addressed (unknown).**
- **CPT.**
  - CPT: "At Yale, CPT is available only in the two circumstances below". These are training "required of all students in your degree program", or credit through a course "listed in your school's bulletin and be specifically designed to award academic credit for an employment or practical training experience".
  - However: "OISS is currently processing CPT only when the training is required of all students in the program for degree completion."
  - CPTA has no drama section, so drama falls under "All Other Professional School Students".
  - **No such Acting course was found in B27. CPT for an Acting student is unknown and appears unavailable.**
- **Volunteer and unpaid work (VOL).** "Students should not accept a volunteer opportunity without first checking with OISS. In many cases volunteering will violate U.S. labor laws and potentially be seen as a violation of F or J immigration status." Also: "Any unauthorized (i.e. illegal) employment - even for one day - … poses a grave threat to your ability to remain in or return to the United States."
- **Self-employment while studying.** **OISS is silent (unknown).**
- **OPT.**
  - OPTW: "Pre-Completion OPT is for off-campus employment before graduation." Any such work would still need School approval.
  - OPTE: "Students are eligible for 12 months of OPT per degree level" and "OPT must be used directly after graduation". "Your employment does not need to be paid while on OPT."
  - OPTW: with a May Commencement, "the earliest you can apply for post-completion OPT is the end of February", and "The latest you can apply for OPT is 60 days after your program end date."
  - OPTO: "Your job must be directly related to your academic field of study". "Employment must be a minimum of 20 hours per week, paid or unpaid". The unemployment limit is "a total of 90 days".
  - Acting work, OPTO: "if you are a musician or performing artist, you can have gigs. Keep a list of all gigs, their dates, and their duration." OPTO also allows 1099 work-for-hire, a "Self-employed business owner" business "related to your degree program", and unpaid work "where this practice does not violate any labor laws."

## What a game may assume, labeled as an assumption

- **Assumption:** early graduation is effectively unavailable. At most it is a rare faculty decision, never below two years and never through transfer credit.
- **Assumption:** the Showcase, in late April and early May of the third year, is the main contact with agents. Meetings or offers after it are invented.
- **Assumption:** signing with an agent before graduation is not forbidden. Any audition trip or booking during term needs outside-work approval.
- **Assumption:** summer outside work needs no approval unless the student is held for a School or Yale Rep assignment.
- **Assumption:** a student who leaves lists the program and the years attended and claims no degree. No Yale rule says this.
- **Assumption:** an F-1 actor takes no paid outside acting work during the degree. Gigs count under OPT after graduation.
- **Assumption:** under F-1, required work-study and Yale Rep calls fit within the 19-hour cap.
