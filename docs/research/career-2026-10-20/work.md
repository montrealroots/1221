# Early acting work for a Yale MFA actor: authorization, unions, pay, payment, tax

Read on 2026-10-20 (all sources). Bracketed tags like [S9] point to the source list at the end, which gives URL, title, date shown and a short exact quotation. "Index only" means sagaftra.org refused direct reading (HTTP 403 bot check, on every page and PDF tried); the text was seen only in the search engine's copy of the page, so it is **not verified by reading**.

## 1. Work authorization

**US citizens and permanent residents.** No immigration limit on acting work. None of the sources below restricts them; the limits that apply are the school's and the unions'.

**The school's limit (applies to every student, citizen or not).** The Drama school "discourages students from pursuing outside paid or unpaid professional work while school is in session"; short outside work is approved only "in rare situations", after a written proposal to the program chair and the dean's written decision [S9]. For actors: "permission to act in projects outside the School is rarely given" [S9]. Unapproved outside work "may lead to academic warning or dismissal" [S9].

**F-1 during the program.**
- On-campus work: up to 20 hours a week while school is in session, full time when it is not [S1, S4].
- CPT: must be "an integral part of an established curriculum", authorized by the school's DSO, part-time (20 hours or less) "the only option during the academic year" at Yale [S1, S7]. Twelve months or more of full-time CPT ends OPT eligibility [S1, S7]. **No source says the Acting MFA offers CPT for outside acting.** Unknown.
- Unpaid acting: Yale OISS defines employment as work done "with the expectation that there will be compensation, either financial or other" and says to check with OISS before volunteering [S8]. The bulletin requires OISS work authorization for approved outside work "paid or unpaid" [S9]. So at Yale an unpaid outside role needs authorization. **Whether a deferred-pay SAG-AFTRA student film counts as employment: no source addresses it.** Unknown.
- Self-employment during the program: no source authorizes it; the regulation's routes are on-campus work, CPT, pre-completion OPT and economic-hardship work [S1]. Treating it as not authorized is an inference, not a quoted rule.

**F-1 after completion (OPT).**
- Length: "12 months of practical training", per degree level [S1, S2].
- Start: not before the date on the EAD [S1, S2]; all of it must be finished "within a 14-month period following the completion of study" [S1].
- Filing: up to 90 days before the program end date and, per USCIS, "no later than 60 days after you complete your degree" [S2]. **Sources disagree:** the eCFR as of 2026-10-19 reads "no later than 30 days" [S1]. That is the text of DHS's duration-of-status final rule (published 2026-07-17, effective 2026-09-15) [S10], which a federal court postponed on 2026-09-14; NAFSA, one of the plaintiffs, reports it "remains blocked nationwide" pending a First Circuit appeal [S11]. Under the blocked rule, the grace period after study or OPT drops from 60 to 30 days, and OPT also needs an extension of stay [S10]. The rule's status could change at any time.
- Hours and unemployment: OPT work must be at least 20 hours a week, "paid or unpaid" [S3, S6]; no more than 90 days of unemployment in total [S1, S3, S6].
- Gigs, 1099 and self-employment on OPT: SEVP's 2010 guidance (still linked by ICE) allows performing artists to work for "multiple short term employers (gigs)", keeping a list of each gig's dates; "1099 employment"; self-employment with business licences; and unpaid work of at least 20 hours a week [S5]. Yale repeats all of these [S6]. The job must relate to the degree [S6].
- Tax side effect: F-1 students who are nonresident aliens (generally fewer than 5 calendar years in the US) are exempt from Social Security and Medicare tax on on-campus and practical-training wages [S14].

**Performing-artist visas (brief).** O-1B needs "distinction" in the arts or "extraordinary achievement" in film/TV, a petition by a US employer or agent, and a peer or union consultation (union plus management for film/TV) [S12]. P-3 covers culturally unique performance [S13]. P-1 (groups) was not researched.

## 2. Union entry

**SAG-AFTRA.**
- Eligibility (index only): one day as a principal or speaking performer under a SAG-AFTRA contract, three background days, or a sibling union. The same index text says "Ultra-low budget, student and short films do not qualify one for membership" [S20]. **Sources disagree:** SAGindie (run by SAG-AFTRA) says Student, Short Project and Ultra Low Budget work can lead to membership "via an employment verification request". Moderate Low and Low Budget productions file Taft-Hartley reports, and Micro-Budget work does not count [S22].
- Taft-Hartley: a signatory producer may hire a non-member, filing a report [S22]. By federal law a union-security clause can require membership "on or after the thirtieth day" of employment [S24]. SAG-AFTRA's handbook (index only): after 30 days from the first covered principal job, the actor must join before the next union job [S23].
- Fees (index only): national initiation fee $3,121, annual base dues $246.14, work dues 1.575% of covered earnings up to $1,000,000 [S20]. Another index copy of the same page shows $3,060 and $241.32. The 2026 figures are exactly 2% above those, which fits a yearly 2% rise, but that is an inference. Semi-annual billing in May and November is secondary-sourced.
- Joining while enrolled: no SAG-AFTRA source restricts students. Unknown rather than permitted.

**Actors' Equity.**
- Routes: an Equity contract (valid during its term); **Open Access**, for anyone "paid to work as an actor or stage manager in theatre in the United States"; or a Four A's sibling union after a year of membership [S25, S26]. The EMC program was "phased out" in favor of Open Access (announced 2023) [S28]. "Membership candidate" and "eligible performer" no longer exist as routes on Equity's pages.
- Students: "Students who have participated ... on Equity/URTA contracts as part of a degree-granting theatre program are also eligible ... whether or not the work was paid" [S26]. Yale: students who are not already members "will attain membership to the union upon graduation" through Yale Rep work [S9]. Citizenship is no bar [S26]. Any non-union theatre job must be finished first [S26].
- Fees: initiation fee $1,800, payable over three years, with $600 down; basic dues $176 a year ($88 each May and November); working dues 2.5% of Equity-contract gross [S27].

## 4. How actors are paid; tax

- **W-2, not 1099, for union work:** "Union actors are not independent contractors; they are employees of the production," and producers use payroll companies for withholding [S22]. The employer withholds federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare, and pays its own share [S44]. Non-union classification follows the IRS common-law control test [S45]. Non-union gigs paid on a 1099 are common in practice, but no source here quantifies that.
- **Timing:** commercial session fees are due "not later than 15 working days after the day or days of employment", with set late-payment damages [S35]. **TV/Theatrical principal pay timing: not verified** (the contract text is on sagaftra.org, which was blocked). As a legal floor, NY Labor Law requires non-manual employees to be paid "not less frequently than semi-monthly" [S40]. Equity pay timing was not found.
- **Agent:** NY caps a theatrical employment agency's fee at "ten per cent of the compensation" [S41]. SAG-AFTRA (index only): an agent generally needs "scale +10" to take commission on session work [S42]. **Checks sent care of the agent, and how fast the agent must forward them: no source found.**
- **Federal:** wages have income tax, Social Security and Medicare withheld [S44]. Self-employment income instead owes 15.3% SE tax (12.4% + 2.9%) once net earnings reach $400, with the employer-equivalent half deductible [S43]. F-1 nonresident aliens are FICA-exempt on qualifying wages [S14].
- **New York State:** employers withhold for "nonresidents being paid wages for services performed within the state" [S46]. Nonresidents owe NY tax on NY-source income, "which includes earnings from work performed in New York State" [S47].
- **New York City:** withholding applies to "New York City residents even when services are performed outside New York City" [S46]. "Nonresidents of New York City are not liable for New York City personal income tax" [S47]. Rates were not captured.
- **Connecticut resident working in NY:** CT-1040 Schedule 2 gives a "Credit for income taxes paid to qualifying jurisdictions" and requires a copy of the other state's return [S48]. The credit's size was not researched.

## What a game may assume (each one an assumption)

- ASSUMPTION: A Yale acting student takes no outside professional work in term. Rare approved exceptions need the chair, the dean and, for F-1, OISS.
- ASSUMPTION: An F-1 student's outside paid or unpaid acting before graduation requires CPT; the game treats it as unavailable unless it models CPT.
- ASSUMPTION: The actor joins Equity at graduation (per the bulletin) and pays $600 down.
- ASSUMPTION: A non-member's first SAG-AFTRA principal job opens a 30-day window, then joining costs about $3,121 plus half a year's base dues (about $123).
- ASSUMPTION: Post-completion OPT starts the day the EAD allows, lasts 12 months, and fails after 90 unemployed days. Each gig counts as employment only for its dates, at 20+ hours a week. Keep the 60-day filing window and 60-day grace period while the court order stands.
- ASSUMPTION: Union pay arrives as a W-2 paycheck about 1–3 weeks after work, sent care of the agent, with 10% commission taken by the agent. Timing and routing are not sourced, except the commercials' 15 working days.
- ASSUMPTION: A NYC resident pays federal, NY State and NYC tax. A New Haven resident pays federal and NY State tax on NY work, and CT tax less a credit for NY tax.
- ASSUMPTION: Showcases and 29-hour readings pay expenses only, and neither earns union membership.
- ASSUMPTION: Off-Broadway pay sits between the LORT D and LORT B minimums. This is a placeholder, not a published figure.
