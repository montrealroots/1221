## 1. Getting representation

- **Showcase.** Yale's course list includes "DRAM 793b - Actor Showcase". The 2026 showcase ran in New York on "Monday, April 27" and "Tuesday, April 28" at MCC Theater, and in Burbank on "Tuesday, May 5" (S31). Yale doesn't say who attends or how many actors sign, so both are unknown.
- **Union status.** Students "who are not members of Actors' Equity will attain membership upon graduation" (S32). Equity's rule then applies: "Equity members may only sign with franchised agents" (S27).
- **No referrals from the unions or the ATA.**
  - SAG-AFTRA: "We do not make connections or recommendations to acting schools, teachers, agents, managers or casting directors" (S7).
  - Equity: "Equity staff cannot recommend particular agents to members" (S27).
  - ATA: "contact individual agencies directly" (S13).
- **Submissions.** Policies vary by agency:
  - Bloc: "Use only our online submission form."
  - Avalon: "submit a headshot, resume, and reel (if applicable) via mail or email".
  - Eris: "Industry referrals preferred." (S34)

  None of the three gives a response time. The claim that large agencies are "referral-only" comes from Backstage directory listings, which blocked me, so it is **unverified**.
- **Agent vs manager.** SAG-AFTRA: "agents are the only entities authorized to negotiate and service employment contracts, while managers primarily focus on career direction". It also says "SAG-AFTRA does not franchise managers… such fees are subject to State law" (S4).
  - **New York:** a theatrical employment agency "does not include the business of managing… where such business only incidentally involves the seeking of employment therefor" (GBL §171). DCWP: "If your primary business is managing artists, you do not need an Employment Agency License" (S22).
  - **California:** the Act's strictures "apply to managers as well as agents", and the Labor Commissioner has "discretion to apply the doctrine of severability" (S26). An unlicensed person may "act in conjunction with, and at the request of, a licensed talent agency in the negotiation of an employment contract" (§1700.44(d)).
  - **Manager fees:** I found no statutory cap. "10–15%" appears only in a SAG-AFTRA article [snippet], so it is **unverified**.

## 2. Agreements

**Union-approved forms.**
- SAG-AFTRA: "Franchised agents are required to use one of the standard SAG or AFTRA Exclusive Agency Contracts or a Union approved contract" (S8 [snippet]).
- Equity's regulations "provide two forms of standard agency contracts: Exclusive Management and Specific Engagement contracts" (S27).

**SAG-AFTRA and the ATA.**
- ATA: "The SAG Agency Regulations expired in 2002, but ATA agencies continue to represent union actors under ATA state-approved agency contracts" (S13).
- SAG-AFTRA: an agency that joins the ATA is "no longer franchised under legacy SAG's agency regulations [16(g)]". It stays franchised only under 12-C, and "GSAs are not valid for any area covered by the 12-C agency regulations" (S10 [snippet]).
- I found **no** report of a new SAG-AFTRA/ATA franchise agreement, and none on whether talks are under way.

**Commission.**

| Rule | Src |
|---|---|
| SAG-AFTRA: "No franchised agent may charge a rate of commission higher than 10%. In some cases, an agent must negotiate your fee above the minimum scale… 'scale plus 10%' in order to collect commission on a job." Commission is paid only "when and if you receive compensation". "Agents may not charge up-front fees of any kind." | S3 |
| SAG-AFTRA: "for performers who are significantly above scale, the agent may, instead, commission 10% of your gross". Per diem, mileage and reimbursements are non-commissionable. | S12 [snippet] |
| Equity: "Tier I: For weekly contractual salaries of $525 or less, a 5% commission". "Tier II: …greater than $525, a 10% commission… except that the rehearsal period at minimum salary is commissionable at 5%" | S28 |
| Equity: "Commission shall not be applicable on the Equity minimum portion of out-of-town expense money or per diem". On Special Production, Cabaret, Business Theatre and SF Bay Area contracts, "Any commission that would invade minimum salary… shall be paid by the Producer" | S28 |
| Equity: "Dues, taxes, and commission are deducted from sick pay and vacation pay." | S27 |
| NY, class "C" (theatrical): "the gross fee shall not exceed, for a single engagement, ten per cent of the compensation payable to the applicant". The cap is 20% for orchestras, opera and concert. | §185(8) |
| California: no statutory percentage. An agency must "file with the Labor Commissioner a schedule of fees". | §1700.24 |

SAG-AFTRA's commission chart rows for **New York** were blocked. Whether scale TV/theatrical work is commissionable in NY is therefore **unknown**.

**Exclusivity and fields.**
- Equity's form: "The Agent shall be the Actor's sole and exclusive agent in the legitimate theatre industry. This contract is limited to the legitimate theatre industry" (S30).
- Equity commission covers "any employment contract entered into during the term… even if you secured your own employment" (S27).
- SAG-AFTRA signs separate TV/Theatrical and Commercials contracts. "If both Contracts are signed… the TV/TH rules for termination will apply" (S2).
- Agencies take submissions by department, for example "Commercial department only" and "Theatrical / musical theatre department" (S34).
- I found no voiceover-specific contract rule.

**Duration.**
- Equity, Rule A: no contract "shall be for a term longer than three years". The initial Exclusive Management contract "shall not specify a term longer than one year", unless an earlier agency contract with the same agent exists. No renewal "except during the last one-third of the term" (S30 §§R–S).
  - **The two Equity texts conflict.** The 2008 form in the same PDF says "The initial contract between Actor and Agent shall not be for more than eighteen months."
- SAG-AFTRA under 12-C: "no initial contract… may be longer than eighteen (18) months". The maximum is "three (3) years", and a 3-year renewal is allowed "after representing a performer for not less than one year" (S11 [snippet]).

**Termination.**
- **SAG-AFTRA TV/Theatrical** (S2):
  - The actor may terminate "if after the initial 120 days… you have not received any bona fide TV/Theatrical offer".
  - If an offer did come, "you must wait at least 151 days".
  - From then on, and on renewals, the trigger is "if during the immediately preceding 91 days you have had less than 10 days of TV/Theatrical work".
- **SAG-AFTRA Commercials** (S2): the same structure, with the trigger "less than $4000 from commercials (including residuals)" in 91 days.
- Either SAG-AFTRA contract may be ended "at any time" by mutual agreement (S2).
- **Equity** (S29):
  - The actor may terminate "If during any ninety (90) day period the Agent fails to secure… a bona fide offer of employment… for at least four weeks at the Actor's customary salary".
  - The 90 days are extended while the actor is unavailable, employed or incapacitated. "You may not terminate if you are currently employed" (S27).
  - The actor may also terminate if a sub-agent named on the contract leaves the agency.
- **So 90 and 91 days are two unions' different rules.** Equity counts offers. SAG-AFTRA counts days worked or dollars earned.

**Commission after termination.**
- Equity: termination does not remove commission on contracts "entered into by the Actor prior to the effective date". It may extend through "any modifications, extensions, renewal or substitutions" of them (S29, S30 §Q).
- Equity tail: the agent communicates an offer in writing and the actor declines. If the actor accepts it after the term, "within 60 days after the date upon which the Agent gives such written information", the commission is owed (S30, form ¶4).
- SAG-AFTRA:
  - Union rules bar commission on work secured after the relationship ends. A GSA may claim renewals "whether negotiated during the term of the contract or after" (S9 [snippet]).
  - Wrongful termination: the agent "may be entitled to collect commissions… until its term ends" (S8 [snippet]).
  - Surrender notices: contracts are "terminated except that the agency may retain and collect any commissions earned under said contracts prior to the surrender" (S10 [snippet]).

## 3. Payment flow

- **Employer of record:** "This may be a production company or a payroll company" (S6).
- **Producer deadline:** "Payment is due on Thursday… for work performed through the previous Saturday". Late payment is "$10 per day (excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays) to a maximum of $200" (S5).
  - The page cites "General Provisions, Section 31.B" but never names the contract. Treating it as TV/Theatrical is my **inference**.
- **Agent to actor, SAG-AFTRA:** checks "received in trust" go out "on a (3) three, (5) five, or (7) seven day cycle". That is 3 days for TV/TH, 5 for commercials and 7 for out-of-state checks (S1).
- **Actor to agent, when paid directly:** "12-C Agency Regulations… interpret this reasonable requirement to be (10) ten business days" (S1).
- **Equity:** the agent collects only with "prior written authority from the actor", and the money is "promptly paid over" (S30 §P).
  - The agent "may deduct… any commission payable" and debts owed.
  - Client money is kept in "a separate account… 'Client's Account' or 'Trust Account'".
  - No day count is given.
- **California:** the agency must "immediately deposit that amount in a trust fund account". The money, "less the licensee's commission, shall be disbursed to the artist within 30 days after receipt" (S24).
  - It may be held longer only to offset a debt "then due and owing", or during a Labor Commissioner controversy.
  - For a willful delay, the Commissioner may award "reasonable attorney's fees" and interest "at the rate of 10 percent per annum".
- **New York law is silent** on trust accounts and payout deadlines in GBL §§171–174 and 185–187 as read (§187 checked, S20). What it does require:
  - fees only "in accordance with the terms of a written contract" (§185(1));
  - excess fees returned "within seven days after receiving a demand" (§186);
  - no deposit or advance fee (§185(3), per S22).
- **Gross:** NY caps the "gross fee". SAG-AFTRA allows "10% of your gross" above scale [snippet]. Whether commission is taken before or after tax withholding is **unstated**.

## 4. Licensing (New York)

- **Licence.** "No person shall open, keep, maintain, own, operate or carry on any employment agency unless such person shall have first procured a license" (§172). In NYC it is "issued by the commissioner of consumer affairs of such city", today DCWP (§§171–172). The ATA: talent agencies "are licensed under the Employment Agency Law of Article 11" (S14).
- **Application.** It must come with "samples or accurate facsimiles of each and every form" applicants sign, and "such forms must be approved by the commissioner before a license may be issued" (§173). DCWP also requires (S21):
  - a contract sample "for each class of employment", Class C (theatrical) included;
  - the Theatrical Self-Certification.
- **Licence terms** (S21):
  - "License Period: 2 years". It expires "May 1 in even years".
  - The fee is "$500" for 1–4 placement employees or "$700" for 5 or more; smaller amounts are prorated.
  - The bond is "$5,000 for all other employment agency applicants". The $10,000 bond is only for modeling agencies or for recruiting household workers from abroad. A search summary had wrongly said $10,000 for theatrical agencies.
- **Contracts and records.** Each contract must include §§185–186 and the "Gross commission or fees the artist will pay to my agency" (S23). Contracts and receipts are kept "for three years" (S22).
- **The union franchise is a second layer.** Equity's franchise requires a "State license" (S27).
- **California, for comparison:**
  - a licence "from the Labor Commissioner" (§1700.5);
  - contract forms approved by the Commissioner (§1700.23);
  - fee schedule filed (§1700.24).

## 5. Who does what

| Task | Who |
|---|---|
| Release a breakdown | Casting: "Casting directors determine who can receive their projects". Actors see one only if casting tells Breakdown Services "to release the Breakdown on Actors Access" (S33) |
| Receive breakdowns | "Licensed talent agencies… Personal Managers in Los Angeles and New York can also subscribe". Commercial breakdowns "can be released to licensed talent agencies only" (S33) |
| Submit | The agent or manager; the actor only on released breakdowns (S33) |
| Negotiate | The agent (S4). ATA: agents "procure and negotiate employment" (S13) |
| Convey an offer | Equity's form requires an offer "communicated by the Agent to the Actor in reasonable detail and in writing" (S30) |
| Employ and pay | The producer or payroll company (S6) |
| Career direction | The manager (S4) |
| Find work | The actor: "obtaining employment… is my own responsibility" (S7) |

Who issues the booking or deal memo is **not stated** in any source I read.

## What a game may assume (assumptions, not rules)

1. **Assumption:** agents, managers and casting directors attend the Yale showcase. The sources show only the dates and venues.
2. **Assumption:** most unsolicited submissions get no reply, and any reply takes weeks. No source gives a response time.
3. **Assumption:** large agencies sign by referral only (unverified listings).
4. **Assumption:** a meeting comes before signing, and a first contract runs one exclusive year. One year is within every limit found.
5. **Assumption:** legit, TV/film and commercial representation may sit with different agencies. Only the separate SAG-AFTRA contracts are verified.
6. **Assumption:** the casting director conveys the producer's offer to the agent, and the agent conveys it to the actor.
7. **Assumption:** commission is 10% of gross before tax withholding.
8. **Assumption:** NY agencies pay out on SAG-AFTRA's 3-day or 5-day cycle even for non-union work. NY law sets no deadline.
9. **Assumption:** managers take 10–15% and do not negotiate alone.
10. **Assumption:** a Yale graduate is an Equity member (sourced, S32) but not yet a SAG-AFTRA member (unsourced).
