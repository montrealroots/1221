# Projection, Scale And The Scene: What The Sources Say, 2026-10-16

What the stage change rests on. The confidence is SLATE's reading of these sources, and the sizes the engine gives
them are SLATE's own.

- **Being heard isn't pushing.** Patsy Rodenburg's three "circles of energy": the second is presence and connection,
  and the third is energy pushed outward, the voice too loud and the chest up, which she warns against. Practice on
  stage voice holds that pushing and shouting never help audibility in a large space: resonance and support carry.
  Sources: [Rodenburg, TED speaker page](https://www.ted.com/speakers/patsy_rodenburg);
  [Casting Frontier, "Teachings of master vocal coach Patsy Rodenburg"](https://castingfrontier.com/blog/teachings-of-master-vocal-coach-patsy-rodenburg/).
  Confidence: medium. The principle is widely taught; her book *The Actor Speaks* wasn't read here.
- **Clarity comes from intention.** Kristin Linklater, as quoted in a Portuguese study of vocal projection: clarity of
  understanding comes from clear and specific intentions. Cicely Berry's *Voice and the Actor* treats the voice as the
  expression of inner life, freed rather than forced. Source:
  [Universidade de Évora, "A projeção vocal no teatro"](https://rdpc.uevora.pt/bitstream/10174/27907/3/A%20proje%c3%a7%c3%a3o%20vocal%20%20no%20Teatro-%20estudo%20e%20tend%c3%aancias%20atuais.docx).
  Confidence: medium.
- **Restraint carries in a theater.** The Moscow Art Theatre's Chekhov emphasized subtext, the meaning under the lines,
  and is credited with intimate ensemble playing: a large stage on which what wasn't said carried. Sources:
  [Britannica, Moscow Art Theatre](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/393468);
  [Wikipedia, Stanislavski's system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislavski%27s_system). Confidence: high.

**What SLATE takes from them.**
- **The house.** It asks the voice and the body to reach it, whatever the scale chosen. It's read on the take's
  execution, as a microphone's demand is.
- **Scale.** It's a choice the scene's circumstances argue for. What a scene hides and how private it is argue for
  restraint. Speaking to many, and conflict brought out loud, argue for size.
