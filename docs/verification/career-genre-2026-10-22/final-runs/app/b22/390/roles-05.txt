Genres
Show Less

Each of the 149 genres keeps two figures of yours: Familiarity, from studying it, and Work In It, from rehearsals, nights on stage, shoot days and self-tapes in it. Press Study It beside a genre, under Genres on your Player Profile, to spend 90 minutes reading a play of its kind the game has, or watching a film of the kind where it has none. The better of the two is how well you know the genre. Each play, part, class scene and professional role says how much of each genre it asks, and another genre counts only one step along the same line: a kind of the genre, or the broader genre of a kind. Knowing a work’s genres is part of its preparation in a taped brief, a self-tape, an audition, a shoot day and a night on stage, every point up to 90% showing in the work; casting reads it in how well you suit a part; and a scene or a part’s lines in work of its kind are prepared up to 17% faster. Loving a genre makes a day of its work a better day; it doesn’t make the work any better.

Where To Find It
Player Profile, Genres.
When You Can Use It
From the guide’s chapter “You As An Actor”.
Show Me Where
What To Press
On the Player Profile, Genres lists every genre with what It Asks For, your Familiarity and your Work In It, and Study It and Open Its Brief.
Find narrows the list by name, and Show picks All, Loved, Studied Or Worked In or With Requirements; the button under the list shows more.
Press Study It, or Study The Genre inside an open brief.
Press a genre’s name for its look: its conventions, what it asks of an actor, how its scenes are played, how to study it, whether you love it, and your latest takes in it.
What You Need First
Awake, out of hospital, free for the time, and nothing on the calendar wanting you inside it.

Time
90 minutes
Energy

What Happens
STRAIGHT AWAY
The time passes, energy goes down, and the genre’s familiarity rises.
A toast names the genre, the play or film you spent the time with, and the familiarity reached, of 100; the Familiarity column moves with it.
LATER ON
The next take of a brief in the genre uses the new figure.
The next step on a scene, or the next lines learned, from a play of the kind goes faster by it.
READING THE RESULT
It Asks For names each technique with your level against mastery, or says the research didn’t establish one: “Not established: the research holds it with low confidence”, or “No more than any self-tape asks”.
A genre’s look gives Familiarity, Work In It and Preparation: how much faster the better of the two makes a scene or a part from a play of its kind, where a brief’s steps speed up with material you’ve met before instead. It gives what the research found the genre asks, and how sure it is.
Knowing The Genre, inside a brief, is the better of the first two against the 90% that completes it. Every brief is in one genre.
The Genre Asks, inside a brief, names each of its genre’s concerns: what it asks, what a self-tape in it is judged more on and calls on, and which part of preparation answers it. A tape held back most by an ability one of those concerns asks for says so: “Your … held back … by … points: …’s … asks for it.”, as when your Composure held back Consistency by 4.2 points and Nordic Noir’s Feeling Held In asks for it.
“You love it” on a genre’s look means a day of its work is a better day: loving it doesn’t make the work faster or limit it.

What To Do Next
Press Open Its Brief beside the genre, and prepare it (Preparing A Brief).
Study it once a day until its brief’s Knowing The Genre is complete.
If You Can’t
“There’s no such genre.”
“Not from a hospital bed.”, “You’re asleep.”, “You’re at … until ….” or “… starts at …: there isn’t the time before it.”
Locked, on a guided life, until “You As An Actor” has been read.
MISTAKES, AND PUTTING THEM RIGHT
Studying one genre for hours in a day: each study the same day adds half the one before. One a day, over a few days, goes further.
Playing every genre’s scene the same way: two scenes can stage the same features and still ask different things, since each genre’s register judges the take its own way. A way of playing it that leans where the genre does puts more of the take on what that genre asks.
Studying on past 90% for a take: past that it adds to a take no more, though it still speeds a scene or a part from a play of the kind.
Expecting a brief’s steps to go faster in a genre you know: they speed up with the material’s features you’ve met before, and the genre is its own part of the take.
Studying a genre the work isn’t in: a genre that isn’t one of the work’s, a kind of one or the broader genre of one adds nothing to it, however well you know it.
Taping cold to learn a genre: a take taped with nothing prepared counts 15% as much toward your work in it as a prepared take.
What Decides It
Familiarity
Each study adds 8% of what’s left to 100; the same genre again the same day adds half the one before (estimated)
Work In It
A take in it, a rehearsal or a night of a play of its kind, or a rehearsal or shoot day of a production of its kind, adds 10% of what’s left, shared among the work’s genres by their share of it; a take by how far it was prepared, 15% of that taped cold; a take that’s the same again adds half (estimated)
Knowing The Genre
The better of Familiarity and Work In It, for each genre the work asks, by how much of it the work asks: a play says it, and a part or a class scene that asks otherwise says its own, as a professional project and its roles do; a genre named beside one of its own kinds counts as that kind, and the order genres are named in changes nothing. It counts toward the interpretation of a taped brief, a self-tape, an audition, a shoot day and a night on stage: with none, 75% of it stands, and every point to 90% adds its share, complete there (estimated)
Related Genres
A kind of a genre the work asks counts at 65%: Psychological Horror known counts toward Horror. The broader genre of a kind it asks counts at 40%: Comedy known counts toward Farce. One step only: Nordic Noir doesn’t count toward Drama, two steps away, and two kinds of one genre don’t count toward each other. The best related genre counts once, beside the genre itself, so studying the genre itself always helps. Any other genre counts for nothing, however well you know it (estimated)
Faster Preparation
Up to 17% with every genre of the work known to 100, each counting for its share as Knowing The Genre does: a scene for class from a play of the kind, a part’s lines in one, and a professional part’s lines on its rehearsal days. A brief’s own steps speed up with material you’ve met before instead
How Its Scenes Are Played
Every genre has one or two concerns, 13 in all, such as Feeling Held In, Comic Rhythm or Caught Live. Every take of a brief written for it is judged more on what they lean toward and calls more on the abilities they ask, as strongly as a reading does. It shifts the balance and adds nothing: a take fully prepared at full ability comes to 100 in every genre
What It Asks For
41 genres ask a technique of an actor, where the research established it: their brief stages it, and a take of it calls for it. A night of a play of the kind, or a class scene from one, calls for its own material’s techniques instead
A Worked Example

A genre never studied, its brief open, Knowing The Genre at 0% of the 90% that completes it.

Press Study It.
Press it again the same evening.

What comes of it. The first study takes familiarity to 8.0, and the second the same day only to 11.7. Studied once a day from the start instead, it passes 90% on day 28, at 90.3: the brief’s Knowing The Genre is complete, and the genre’s look says it makes a scene or a part from a play of only its kind 15% quicker to prepare.
