Auditions And Callbacks
Show Less

An audition is in person, in New York or Hudson or New Haven at a set time, or a self-tape due by an evening. Prepare the sides first with Break It Down, Learn The Lines and Rehearse It On Your Feet, the way you prepare a practice brief. Then press Tape It, or be there when the clock stops for the audition and press Go.

Casting decides from how the audition went, how well the role suits you and how many other actors it saw. Some roles call you back for a second audition first.

Where To Find It
Career, Opportunities, In Progress.
When You Can Use It
From the guide’s chapter “Finding Work”, once a casting office has answered a submission.
Show Me Where
What To Press
Open Career, then Opportunities, and find the role under In Progress.
Press Break It Down, Learn The Lines and Rehearse It On Your Feet, each as many times as it helps. Each says its time.
For a self-tape, press Tape It before it’s due. For an audition in person, press Go when the clock stops for it.
If it clashes with something, press Ask For Another Time at least a day ahead.
What You Need First
Free for each step’s time, and awake. Rehearsing on your feet needs some of the lines learned and the energy for it.
For a self-tape, an hour free before it’s due.
For a new time, a day’s notice, asked once.
TIME AND MONEY
Time
30 minutes to 45 minutes a step; an hour for a self-tape; an audition in person 30 minutes, a chemistry read an hour and a screen test five hours, with the trip each way (estimated)
Money
None
What Happens
STRAIGHT AWAY
A step moves its part of the preparation on and the clock with it. A self-tape is judged at once and kept on your work record.
LATER ON
A day after a first audition casting decides whether to call you back, and three days after the last whether to offer you the role. A notice says which.
A lead in screen work, and a supporting part in a romance, has a chemistry read after the callback: the sides played in person with the actors already cast, judged above all on how you listen and answer them. A feature’s lead has a screen test after that: the part filmed on set in costume and makeup, take after take. Casting decides after each whether you go on. Either brings a hold on the part’s dates until it’s cast: Offers And Booked Work says what a hold asks of you.
READING THE RESULT
The band says how good the audition was. Casting’s chance reads the band, the fit and the field together, and even the best audition doesn’t make it certain.
Why It Went This Way, under a settled role, shows each part of it.
How It Connects
CHANGES LATER
The Calendar: An audition in person goes on your calendar with its trip, and stops the clock when it comes.
COUNTS TOWARD
Offers And Booked Work: Casting reads your audition’s band, with the role’s fit and the field, when it decides whether to offer you the role.
WHAT LEADS HERE
Genres: Knowing the project’s genres is part of an audition’s and a self-tape’s preparation, complete at 90%, and half of how well casting thinks you suit the role.
Finding Work And Submitting: A submission puts the casting office’s answer on the clock, and the answer sets an audition.
What To Do Next
Wait for casting’s word, then answer an offer under Offers.
If You Can’t
There are no sides yet: casting sends them with an audition.
You’ve done all this step can do.
There’s less than the hour a self-tape takes before it’s due.
You’ve already asked once, and casting is keeping the time it gave.
Casting has no other time clear of your calendar in the days it’s seeing actors.
MISTAKES, AND PUTTING THEM RIGHT
Missing an audition. Casting moves on without you, and the role lapses.
What Decides It
Your Audition
Your abilities and techniques, the sides’ preparation, how well you know the project’s genres and the day’s condition, judged as every piece of work is
The Role’s Fit
How well you know its genres, each by its share as Genres explains, and its techniques against yours
The Field
How many other actors that kind of work draws, estimated
A Worked Example

A self-tape asked for by Thursday at six, with the sides not yet prepared.

Press Break It Down, Learn The Lines and Rehearse It On Your Feet until they can do no more.
Press Tape It.

What comes of it. An hour on, the tape is judged and shown with its band, and the casting office decides on the Friday after it’s due.
