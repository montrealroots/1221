Finding Work And Submitting
Show Less

The Opportunities tab lists the roles you can be seen for. Their projects, companies and casting offices are invented for the game, except a few parts drawn from real productions, which say so. New roles go up each Monday morning and take submissions for seven days.

Press Submit Yourself on an open role to send your headshots and résumé, and a reel where casting asks for one. Some casting offices take actors only through an agent, and their roles reach you only while one represents you.

Where To Find It
Career, Opportunities, Open Calls.
When You Can Use It
From the guide’s chapter “Finding Work”.
Show Me Where
What To Press
Open Career, then the Opportunities tab.
Under Open Calls, read a role: the project, the part, who it’s written for, what it asks, its dates and place, its pay and whether you can be seen for it. Each part is written for an actor, an actress or either, and you’re posted only the ones written for you or for either.
Press Submit Yourself. If it’s grayed out, the line under it says why.
What You Need First
Headshots and a résumé, made on the Portfolio tab, and a reel where the casting office asks for one.
A role that takes your own submission, before its window closes.
The role’s playing ages, and Equity membership for an Equity open call.
TIME AND MONEY
Time
None: a submission is sent at once
Money
None
What Happens
STRAIGHT AWAY
The role moves to In Progress, with what you sent and when each was made.
LATER ON
Two days on, the casting office answers with an audition in person or a self-tape, and a notice says when and where.
READING THE RESULT
Open Calls are roles you can submit yourself for. Through An Agent shows the roles an agent brought, which only the agent submits.
Each role says it’s fictional, or, for a part drawn from a real production, that the part is the game’s own: no real production is casting.
A part comes round again only once the work it was cast for is over, as a new casting of the part, and its card says what came of it the last time. A part you’ve been booked for doesn’t come round again.
How It Connects
COMES FIRST
Headshots: A submission starts with your headshots: without them it can’t be sent.
CHANGES LATER
Auditions And Callbacks: A submission puts the casting office’s answer on the clock, and the answer sets an audition.
WHAT LEADS HERE
Productions And People: A role’s Open Its Page opens its project here, and the project’s page sends you back to answer it.
What To Do Next
Prepare the sides once the casting office answers.
If You Can’t
You have no headshots, and every submission starts with one.
The casting office takes submissions only from agents.
It’s an open call for Equity members, and you aren’t one yet.
Submissions closed.
MISTAKES, AND PUTTING THEM RIGHT
Waiting to make headshots until a role you want is posted. A role’s window is a week; make them first.
What Decides It
Materials
Your current headshots, résumé and reel, as What’s Missing reads them
Eligibility
The role’s playing ages, an open call’s union, and the days you’re already booked
A Worked Example

A graduate with headshots and a résumé, and a student film posted on Monday.

Open Career, then Opportunities.
Press Submit Yourself under the student film.

What comes of it. The role moves to In Progress with your headshots and résumé listed as sent, and two days on a notice says when the director wants to see you.
