# What The Second Material Area Kept, And Why

A sample of the 374 sources kept in brief 15's second pass, from all six files, each with its words and its reason, so
the owner can judge the reasons as well as the rewrites. Every kept source's reason is in
`docs/text/second-pass/material-b.json`.

The standard for this material (`../prompts/material-b.md`): the game's own narration is plain, vivid, specific English
for an actor; a staging note or an objective is a direct instruction; a character's line keeps the character's voice. A
source was kept where it already did all three.

## Whole Briefs

1. **One More School Night** (`second-a.ts` [brief-apocalyptic-2]). Where And When: "A kitchen in a small university
   town, at nine on a Thursday night, over takeout noodles. For three weeks the observatory has watched something coming
   that can’t be stopped, and at eight tomorrow morning the government will say so on every channel." The objective:
   "Give him one more ordinary school night, and keep her promise never to lie to him." **Kept because** every fact is one
   an actor can play (the noodles, the soldiers at the gate, eight tomorrow), the objective sets the promise against the
   secret in one line, and its one reason ("He gives her almost nothing, and the scene is her listening for what his
   silence is asking.") is already a direction in the scene's terms.
2. **Last Thursday’s Refill** (`second-b.ts` [brief-drama-2]). "Last Thursday she stayed open late to fill an old woman’s
   heart prescription, and this morning she found the error in her own log: the wrong strength." The timing reason:
   "She has to let his thanks run as long as she can bear, and the scene turns on the moment she cuts in." **Kept
   because** the error is given with its date, its strength and its report to the state board, exactly, and the timing
   reason tells the actor where the scene turns.
3. **Terms For The Widow** (`second-c.ts` [brief-gangster-2]). The beats: "He offers condolences and the usual envelope;
   she thanks him and lets it sit on the table at her side." / "She names her terms like a bookkeeper, a sum for each
   year until the boys are grown, and he’s amused in spite of himself." **Kept because** each beat is a full sentence
   with a physical action (the envelope left sitting) and a tone the actor can find, and nothing in it is the game
   describing itself.
4. **The Housekeeper’s Keys** (`second-d.ts` [brief-melodrama-2]). The vocal reason: "The scene climbs from a cold
   reading of the will to open fury, then falls almost to a whisper, and the voice has to carry the whole range."
   **Kept because** it gives the voice's whole journey in one sentence, at melodrama's own size, and the physical reason
   already keeps every gesture on her side of the frame.
5. **Proof Of Employer** (`second-e.ts` [brief-social_realism-2]). "He works forty hours a week at a distribution
   warehouse, and since the rent went up in May the money runs out around the twentieth." **Kept because** it states
   the social fact the scene rests on plainly, with its figures, and without comment, which is the genre's own register.
6. **The Left Sleeve** (`second-f.ts` [brief-zombie-2]). "Her gloves, the clipboard and the thermometer’s screen are on
   her side of the folding table; she checks him by sight across it and never touches him." **Kept because** the
   no-contact staging is already one plain line inside the circumstances, and reads as the nurse's procedure as well as
   the tape's rule.

## Readings

7. **Less Than The Roof** (`second-a.ts` [roof]): "Under the danger she sees the size of his trouble clearly, and the laugh
   in the fifth beat is where she decides what to do." It rests on: "His debt is less than the new roof cost, and she
   opens the safe herself." **Kept because** it points the actor to the exact moment the choice is made and rests it on
   two facts the beats state.
8. **The Song She Swore Off** (`second-b.ts` [song]): "The broken string gives her the excuse to sing the one song that’s
   really hers, and the scene is her deciding to." **Kept because** it turns an accident into a decision the actor can
   play, in one sentence.
9. **Her Own Monster** (`second-c.ts` [made]): "She made the creature up, and the horror of the scene is a young woman
   facing something she invented." **Kept because** it names the horror plainly and specifically, and rests on the
   brief's own fact (she made it up three summers ago).
10. **The Script Is The Kindness** (`second-d.ts` [procedure]): "She holds to the procedure because it protects him, and
    her warmth lives inside the same words said the same way." **Kept because** it gives a procedural scene an emotional
    spine without inventing anything, and the label says the idea in five words.
11. **Speaking Past The Enemy** (`second-e.ts` [enemy]): "Every line is pitched to the admiral listening too, so her crews
    hear a defiance the enemy can’t object to." **Kept because** it's a playable double address, specific to the shared
    channel the brief sets up.
12. **The Spring In Her Fingers** (`second-f.ts` [decision]): "The scene is one decision, and she weighs the truth against
    a life while he talks." **Kept because** it compresses the scene to the one choice the beats build to, and the label
    is the prop that holds it.

## Shapes Kept On Purpose

13. **The arc after the beats** (`why.beats`, every brief), for example The Safe Behind The Ring's "A slow start in the
    half dark, a memory used to hold him, a fight she wins by not finishing it, then a laugh and a debt paid in work."
    **Kept because** it recaps, in one line of phrases, beats the player has just read numbered above it, the way a script
    reader sums up a scene's shape; each was read for clarity, and the one that was a riddle (The Sweep Bus's "the chase
    called off by the driver chasing her") was rewritten.
14. **Pages and rooms as beats** (A Score Without Words, Lots One To Forty, Entries For The Twelfth Of November): "The
    dots: short, separate sounds, tried out like steps on ice." / "Nine o’clock: a sensible entry about the children’s
    lessons and the draft along the corridor." **Kept because** the piece is those pages, rooms or hours, so naming each
    by its heading is how the performer meets it.
15. **The trailer's sections** (Trash Day: The Trailer): "The opening: a building, a season, and a schedule that changed
    everything." **Kept because** the comedy is the trailer's own swollen structure, and the beats are its sections.
