# Material-A: A Sample Of What Was Kept

Every kept source has its own reason in `docs/text/second-pass/material-a.json`. These are a sample, across the files,
with the words kept and the reason, so the owner can judge the reasons as well as the rewrites.

## Batch 1

- **`briefs-a.ts` [brief-absurdist], Proof Of Arrival.** Relationship: "Strangers across a counter. He has never
  refused anyone, and he has never registered anyone either." Obstacle: "Each rule the clerk explains needs the thing
  the last rule forbids, and he means every word kindly." *Reason:* it sets out the witness rule's loop in plain,
  playable facts (one window, closing at five, a clerk who means every word kindly), and each beat says what Margit
  does.
- **`briefs-a.ts` [whisper], Not A Sound.** "The silence is where the comedy comes from: she fights the whole scene at
  a whisper, and the strain of staying quiet is the joke." It rests on: "The speeches have started on the other side of
  a thin door." *Reason:* one plain, playable rule for the scene, resting on the speeches beyond a thin door.
- **`briefs-a.ts` [brief-art_house], Oranges.** Beats: "He talks; she peels, and the peel comes off in one piece." /
  "He stops and waits. She offers him a segment." / "Her one full sentence: she asks him to leave the table where it
  is." *Reason:* a scene of silence and one long take, built from exact, unshowy facts an actor can play. The segment
  is the orange's, so the engine-speak count's hit on it stays.
- **`plays.ts` #THESIS_PLAYS.** "Hedda Gabler", "Henrik Ibsen", "Hedda Tesman", "Judge Brack" and the rest. *Reason:*
  the plays' real titles, authors and characters; a title is also matched by exact text against the casting a save
  holds (`school.ts`, `p.title === c.play`), so it's a key.

## Batch 2

- **`briefs-b.ts` [brief-experimental], The Glass Of Water.** Circumstances: "The exchange is six lines long. It’s played
  four times in one take. Your lines never change; the reader changes one of theirs each time, chosen in advance and
  unknown to you." *Reason:* it states its rules exactly, so an experiment with no story is still playable. The "take"
  is the tape's own, a single unbroken one.
- **`briefs-b.ts` [still], All In The Folding.** "He gives nothing away, and the whole scene is played in the folding of
  the shirts." It rests on: "Fear makes a source a risk, and he keeps folding after he understands." *Reason:* one
  physical action carries the whole reading, resting on the scene's own rule about fear.
- **`briefs-b.ts` [brief-costume_drama], Quarter Day.** Obstacle: "The custom of the house forbids her to sit until
  invited, or to name a sum first, and Mrs. Ashworth knows it and offers a glowing reference in place of the money."
  *Reason:* 1847's rules for a governess are set out plainly and the scene is made from them.

## Batch 3

- **`briefs-c.ts` [brief-giallo], The Dry Umbrella.** Circumstances: "One detail stays with her: the umbrella the figure
  carried was closed, and dry, in the rain. It rained again this morning, and the inspector’s coat is still wet."
  *Reason:* the scene turns on one clue an actor can see and hold, and ends with that umbrella in the room.
- **`briefs-c.ts` [brief-family], The Treehouse Treaty.** "On the tape the treehouse is a cramped corner, and the
  ladder is never seen." *Reason:* a set description a self-tape can do, kept as a description because it says what
  the frame holds, not a safety instruction.
- **`briefs-c.ts` [nobody], Nobody Waiting.** "The comedy is a woman holding off the fact that no one at home will
  miss her, and the third beat lets it in." *Reason:* it says exactly where the comedy and the grief meet, resting on
  the panel's "no record of a home".

## Batch 4

- **`briefs-d.ts` [brief-monster_movie], The Pump House.** Circumstances: "On the tape she kneels alone on a bare floor,
  with her brother off camera at her shoulder and the water only in her eyes." *Reason:* a monster scene built on one
  rule (it hunts by sound) and a whisper, with a set description a tape can do; kept as a description because it says
  what the frame holds.
- **`briefs-d.ts` [brief-period_drama], The Gown For Tonight.** Obstacle: "A maid may not accuse a tradeswoman of
  error, may not mention her mistress’s debts, and may not go home without the gown." *Reason:* 1911's rules for a
  lady's maid set out plainly, so the contest inside good manners is playable.

## Batch 5

- **`briefs-e.ts` [brief-silent_film], The Telegram For Miss Avery.** Physical: "The scene has no spoken lines: every
  thought is mime and gesture, played to the lens through the face and hands." *Reason:* it says plainly how a silent
  scene works and gives each beat one clear piece of business (the face that falls so far it's funny, the hatband).
- **`briefs-e.ts` [right], She’s Right.** It rests on: "The brief never says who sent the message, only that it came
  from her number." *Reason:* here "the brief never says" is the point, the one open fact the reading stands on.

## Batch 6

- **`briefs-f.ts` [brief-true_crime], The Register At The Starlite.** Circumstances (first sentence): "The case is
  invented for this scene: no real crime, person or place stands behind it." *Reason:* a true-crime brief says plainly
  that nothing real stands behind it, before a word of the scene, as Truth in the text asks.
- **`briefs-f.ts` [scale], Something Enormous.** "The scene is the size of what she sees. She plays to the eyeline of a
  performer in a creature suit on a miniature set, the way the genre’s actors do, and the wonder carries the argument."
  *Reason:* it says how the genre's actors actually work, as a playable note.

## Batch 7

- **`scenes-house.ts` [henry5-4-3], Henry V before Agincourt.** Westmorland's tactic, its reason: "He wishes for the
  men “That do no work today.”" *Reason:* the quotation is the play's, exact, and the game's sentence around it says
  plainly what he does with it.
- **`scenes-greek.ts` [bacchae-451], The Bacchae.** Dionysus's tactic, its reason: "Murray’s stage direction has him
  gentle and unafraid, and each answer turns the question back." *Reason:* a translator's stage direction is named as
  his, not passed off as the game's or the play's.
- **`scenes-classical.ts` [measure-2-4], Measure for Measure.** A reason for an answer the text doesn't support: "She
  would wear the marks of keen whips as rubies first." *Reason:* it answers "Bargain for better terms" with the play's
  own image, said plainly, and reads whole as the teacher's note.
