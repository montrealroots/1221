The Money line in Your Affairs on Home shows your primary checking account and your net worth (what you have, less what you owe), with what you owe when there’s anything. Your money is kept in the currency of the country you started in. Press Your Affairs, the panel’s title, to see what you’re worth, and from there each account and each debt. Your credit score decides whether a bank gives you a card, an overdraft or a loan, at what rate where the bank prices by it, and how high a card’s limit is and whether it’s raised. It rises with each card payment made on time, and slowly with no card at all while nothing is late or in collections. It falls when you use a card past 30% of its limit, pay late, miss a loan payment, apply for a card, leave a medical debt unpaid for a year, or have a debt charged off to collections. You see the score only when a life is made, as a number in the United States and Canada and as a word elsewhere. After that, you can tell it from what banks will do for you. Your accounts and your card are at your bank. Read “Your Bank” next to open them. The Details Your net worth is what you have in the bank, in cash and in a pension from before, less what you owe on cards and loans, in unpaid bills, medical bills and collections.
