The Money line in Your Affairs on Home shows your current account and your net worth (what you have, less what you owe), and how much of it is owed. Your money is kept in the currency of the country you started in. Press Your Affairs, the panel’s title, to open what you’re worth, and from there each account and each debt. Your credit record decides whether a bank gives you a card, an overdraft or a loan, at what rate where the bank prices by it, and how high the card’s limit is and whether it’s raised. It rises with each card payment made on time, and slowly with no card at all while nothing is late or in collections. It falls with a card used past 30% of its limit, a late payment, a missed loan payment, applying for a card, a medical debt left unpaid a year, and a debt charged off to collections. You see its figure only when a life is made, as a number in the United States and Canada and a word elsewhere. In play, you read it in what the banks do. The accounts and the card are your bank’s: the next chapter, “Your Bank”, opens them. The Details Your net worth is what’s in the bank and in cash, and a pension from before, less what you owe on the card, loans, what went unpaid, medical bills and collections.
