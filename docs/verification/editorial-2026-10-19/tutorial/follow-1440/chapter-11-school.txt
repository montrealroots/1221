You begin as a first-year in the MFA in Acting at the David Geffen School of Drama at Yale University, in New Haven. You’re one of the sixteen actors admitted this year, among the strongest of them but not ahead of everyone. You arrive on the first day of the fall term for registration and orientation, and classes begin a week later. The school is where you train, get cast and earn your degree. Open it with the Yale button on the School line in Your Affairs on Home, or from School in the menu. The School line shows your year, your standing, your place in the class and what’s next. The screen’s tabs are Standing And Craft, Classes, Coursework, Casting, Shifts And Pay, and Terms And Award. Classes run from nine to three on class days. Be there, in New Haven and awake, and your craft grows, more when you come rested. Your fifteen classmates grow too, each at their own pace, and you’re cast beside and against them. If you’re unwell, press Call In Sick on Classes before the day’s first commitment begins (on a class day, at the latest when the clock stops at the classes at nine). The day’s classes, labs, rehearsal calls and work-study shift are excused, and a partner rehearsal you miss while ill isn’t held against you. A day in hospital is excused on its own. Staying away when you’re well is an absence. Your craft, on Standing And Craft, is the 17 techniques the school trains and four of your abilities: Emotional Depth, Memory, Physical Expression and Vocal Expression, each kept in one place. A technique is a skill a class trains, and an ability is something about you, kept on your Player Profile. Composure, beside your craft, eases the nerves that add stress before every performance, and every performance grows it. Each course sets work at its meetings: a scene, a song, a movement score. On Coursework, prepare each piece a step at a time, answer its questions at the table, and choose how you’ll play it. Where the text supports more than one answer at the table, each is right. Choosing how to play a scene isn’t the same as rehearsing it: the choice pays off once you’ve rehearsed it on your feet. A scene pairs you with a classmate. Pick an evening they’re free and press Ask with their name, such as Ask Zoe. A rehearsal kept builds your scene together and their trust in you. If they won’t come or don’t turn up, Run It With A Reader works your side of it with someone feeding you their lines. It’s slower, and it leaves your partner as they were. Shown Today, on each piece, is its readiness now: what it would come to if it were shown today, which parts are short, and the steps that would answer them. When a piece is shown, your own work is judged, and so is the scene together, with what your partner brought, which also moves the bond between you. Your teacher’s note says what held it back most. The note is the game’s, written as your course’s teachers would say it. If too many of a term’s pieces are coming out Unready, a notice, A Word On Your Coursework, says so during the term, with the day the faculty review it. That’s a warning, not the review itself: the review comes at the end of the term’s classes. At the review, the faculty look at your attendance at classes, calls and assigned shifts, your growth against the class, your work on stage and your coursework. Coursework This Term, on Standing And Craft, counts the term the way the review will. Fall short on any of them and you drop a step: from good standing to academic concern, from concern to academic warning, and from warning to dismissal. Meet them all and you’re back in good standing. From your first spring, the chair casts you each term in one of the School’s productions, and once a year, in good standing, you’re at Yale Rep too, most often as an understudy. Casting shows your part and Why This Cast. Between rehearsal calls, Learn The Part, Catch Up The Blocking and Work On The Note work on your part. Missing two calls in a term, labs and rehearsals together, takes you out of the casting pool until a good review. A missed performance, or not being in New Haven when you’re called to go on, is a grave breach: it puts you on academic warning at once, and if you’re already on warning, it dismisses you. Work It Through, on a scene or a part, names the problems its forecast shows. Pick one, try a way at it and see the figure before and after, then keep the change or put it back. Every student works: six hours on the Saturdays of class weeks, and at least 50 more hours of elective evenings front of house at Yale Rep during the term, which you sign up for with Take An Elective Shift on Shifts And Pay. Hours are paid on Fridays. The Yale Cabaret is the students’ own theater: Join A Cabaret Show, on Casting, puts you in a show, one a term. In the spring of your first and second years, Join The Summer Company puts you in the Yale Summer Cabaret for the summer, with a weekly stipend. Plays To Read, on Coursework, are plays first staged since 2000. Read It with the Haas Arts Library’s copy, and once it’s read, Research It. Tuition is paid for every student by the tuition scholarship. Yale’s help with living costs goes by need, and your family’s means rule it out, so the Yale Health plan, billed each term, and your living costs come from your bank. Next, “You As An Actor” opens your Player Profile: your abilities, your practice and the genres you work in. The Details The craft, in the order the School screen lists it: Accents And Dialects, Audition Technique, Character Work, Classical Text, Clown And Mask, Cold Reading, Comedy, Dance, Emotional Depth, Improvisation, Intimacy, Memory, On Camera, Physical Expression, Scene Study, Script Analysis, Singing, Speech, Stage Combat, Vocal Expression and Voice-Over. Acting, the row under them, counts scene study twice, with character work and emotional depth, and it’s what a Yale Rep part asks most. A part is cast on the techniques its play needs: a voice and speech that carry and the body, and the classics and a fight where the play asks for them. The work you do grows what it trains. The first fall has Drama 50, three weeks of afternoon labs, and the first spring the New Play Lab, three weeks workshopping the first-year playwrights’ new plays. The last spring opens with your own solo piece, You Are The Creator. Each is a call, like a rehearsal. Work-study comes to 150 hours a year. The productions: the Carlotta Festival as a first-year; a directors’ thesis or the Langston Hughes Festival in a fall after that, and a thesis or the Carlotta Festival in the second spring; a Shakespeare Repertory Project in your last spring. On call at Yale Rep, the Rep calls three hours before each curtain, at five for an evening and at eleven for a Sunday matinee. Be in New Haven then, and the evening is yours unless you’re needed, when you’re told and the performance goes on your calendar. Every understudy plays one run-through for the director. The chair weighs every student the same way for each play: how well you fit it, what a night of it would come to from your abilities and the techniques it asks, its kind of play’s demands among them; a fifth of that by how much of yourself you gave in your last run; and a lead counting against your next lead and a small part for it. The best fit takes the biggest part written for them. Why This Cast, on Casting, shows how each student stood. Each night is judged on the techniques and abilities the part asks, the lines, blocking and notes your calls and your own work have made, and how rested you come. A performance holds the clock for its play’s running time and tires you by its workload: how long it plays, how much of it your part is on stage, and what it asks of your body and voice, less after an interval. Your stamina carries the late scenes and spares up to half the energy it costs, and The Evening’s Workload, under A Night Now, says it before the curtain. A night comes out off, solid, strong or remarkable, at LAMDA’s pass, merit and distinction, and the director gives a note at the end of the run. Join A Cabaret Show puts you in a show at least three weeks off, its week clear of your days off, casting calls, nights on call, labs and work-study shifts. It isn’t open on academic warning, on a leave, or cast in two productions at once. Join The Summer Company needs good standing, no leave, and your name in by April 30; the summer is three shows. A day called in sick, in hospital or kept off by an injury excuses a Cabaret or Summer Cabaret call. Miss a Summer Cabaret performance otherwise and the company goes on without you. At the review, a piece missed without an excuse counts as due and unready, and a course with half its pieces due not shown isn’t passed. The standings come from the Yale Bulletin. The Bulletin has the faculty evaluate students regularly; one review a term, and its floors and its steps, are estimated. After the program’s six terms, in good standing or on academic concern with every course passed, you’re awarded the MFA. If the last term ends on academic warning, the faculty don’t recommend it. A course not passed leaves 30 days to ask to repeat the year or for an additional term, where either is open to you, before the degree is withheld, and an Incomplete still open holds the degree until it’s made up. Work It Through’s problems are a part of the scene that isn’t secure, a turn not worked, stamina going late, or an ability holding it back. A change to how a part of the scene is played is kept or put back, and what you keep carries into every later rehearsal and performance. Between a production’s calls the director names one of these too, somewhere in the play (what you want, listening, pacing, the physical life, a change, a relationship), with ways at it: Work On The Note one way, and the next call says whether it was answered. On the School screen, the school’s name, each course and each technique open where they stand: the school into this year’s courses and the town, a course into the techniques it trains and the next day it meets, and a technique into where you stand in it against the class.
