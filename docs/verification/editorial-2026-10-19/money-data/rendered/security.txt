Chase Freedom Unlimited ending 9931, expires July 20, 2029 
Lock It Damaged Lost Stolen Report It Report It And Rush The New One 
Sources And Estimates
The bank lists no fee for a new card, so the game charges nothing for one. A rushed one costs $15.00 more. Cards last 4 years here. That’s estimated: the bank doesn’t say.
Once you report a card lost or stolen, Chase declines anything new on it and sends a replacement with a new number. For a credit card, the account itself stays open and keeps its age on your credit record.
Chase watches cards around the clock and may text, send an app notification, email or call to check that unusual activity was you. Fraud alerts are automatic: you don’t need to sign up. Chase can decline a payment it believes may be fraud, but it doesn’t say it holds a payment while it waits for your reply.
The law on the debit card: If you report a lost or stolen debit card within two business days of finding out, you pay at most $50 of what was taken before you told the bank. After two business days, the most you pay rises to $500. And if a payment you didn’t make is on a statement and you don’t report it within 60 days of the statement being sent, you can be made to pay all of what’s taken after those 60 days. The two days start when you find out; the 60 days start when the statement is sent. This is Regulation E, 12 CFR 1005.6(b), under the Electronic Fund Transfer Act.
The law on the credit card: If someone uses your credit card without your permission, you pay at most $50, or less if less was spent before you told the card’s issuer. This is the Truth in Lending Act’s Regulation Z, 12 CFR 1026.12(b)(1)(ii).
The law on a payment you report as not yours: The bank has 10 business days to decide, or 20 for a payment in a new account’s first 30 days. If it needs longer, it must put the money back in your account for now within those 10 business days, and it can then take up to 45 days, or 90 for a debit card purchase, a payment made from outside the US or one on a new account. It tells you the result within 3 business days of deciding. This is Regulation E, 12 CFR 1005.11.

RBC Cash Back Mastercard ending 9931, expires December 1, 2028 
Lock It Damaged Lost Stolen Report It 
Sources And Estimates
The bank lists no fee for a new card, so the game charges nothing for one. Cards last 4 years here. That’s estimated: the bank doesn’t say.
RBC cancels a lost or stolen debit card and sends a new one, and blocks a lost or stolen credit card and sends a new card. Your first replacement debit card in a calendar year is free, and each one after that costs C$5.00, unless it’s replaced because of fraud, a name change or a card that arrived damaged. The game charges nothing for any of them.
On a credit card, RBC texts you from short code 722373 or 347722. Reply Y to confirm a payment, or N if you don’t recognize it, and a fraud specialist contacts you. The card agreement says RBC can block the card without telling you when it suspects fraud.
The law on the debit card: You don’t pay for losses you couldn’t control: fraud, theft or being forced to use the card, a technical failure, or use after you reported the card lost or stolen. If you contributed to the loss, say by choosing to share your PIN or by not reporting it in reasonable time, you can be made to pay, but no more than the card’s withdrawal limits, unless an overdraft or a linked credit line is attached. No law sets a dollar limit for debit cards: this is the voluntary Canadian Code of Practice for Consumer Debit Card Services, section 5.
The law on the credit card: If someone uses your credit card without your permission, you pay at most C$50, unless you were grossly negligent (gross fault, in Quebec), and nothing after you report the card lost, stolen or at risk. A payment made with your PIN doesn’t prove gross negligence on its own. For card issuers that aren’t banks but are federally regulated, the FCAC says the limit is C$50 or the amount in your agreement, whichever is less. This is the Bank Act, S.C. 1991, c. 46, s. 627.33.
The law on a payment you report as not yours: Under the Debit Card Code of Practice, the bank must answer your report of a payment you didn’t make as soon as it can, within 10 business days, though it can stop that clock while it waits for a signed statement or affidavit from you. No law sets a deadline to refund a credit card payment.

The card issuer also has to try to get the money back through the card scheme’s chargeback rules (cl. 11.10).
The law on a payment you report as not yours: Within 21 days of your report, the bank must finish investigating or tell you in writing that it needs more time, and it must finish within 45 days unless the circumstances are exceptional. The code sets no separate deadline for a refund (ePayments Code, cl. 18).
