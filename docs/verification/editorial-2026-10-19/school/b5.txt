== The concerns’ sources ==
Feeling Held In: Subtext, the life under the lines (Stanislavski, in the project’s acting research, topic 1); the genre claims are actors’ and critics’ accounts of contained, controlled performances.
Played Straight: Truth and belief, the “magic if” (Stanislavski, topic 1); the genre claims are advice to play sentiment, or absurdity, with complete earnestness.
Played Large: Melodrama’s exaggerated emotion and silent film’s mime (Britannica, topic 10); Lecoq’s melodrama as a dramatic territory with its own structures in the body (topic 10).
Comic Rhythm: Comic timing, the pauses, cadence and pacing that land a joke (the comedy genre’s claim, and Attardo and Pickering 2011 on timing in jokes, topic 10); Stanislavski’s tempo-rhythm (topic 9).
Caught Live: Naturalism, the Moscow Art Theater’s and Stanislavski’s realistic acting (topic 10); the genre claims are non-professional casting, improvised dialogue and withheld scripts.
Dense Text, Exact: Fluency from memory (LAMDA’s criteria, topic 1); the genre claims are dense, fast dialogue mastered by repetition, memorized closing arguments and medical jargon drilled before the shoot.
Another Age’s Manners: Period movement, deportment and speech, assessed by LAMDA and taught by conservatories (topic 10); the genre claims are historical advisers and period voice and movement lessons.
Belief In What Isn’t There: Imagination and the “magic if” (Stanislavski, topics 1 and 9); the science-fiction claim, that playing against things not there takes imagination and specificity.
States At The Limit: Emotional truth under extreme circumstances (topic 1); the genre claims are sustained fear, screaming and real reactions to absent threats, and grief pushed to extremes.
Played Between Two: Ensemble playing in place of the star turn (the realist stage, topic 10); the genre claims are chemistry reads, contrasting leads and a crew in which each actor must establish a distinct role.
Suspense Held: Concentration and the through-line (Stanislavski, topic 1); the genre claims are tension built by silence, close-ups and pacing, with the performance holding it.
The World’s Own Logic: Stylised forms as dramatic territories (Lecoq, topic 10); Beckett’s exact timing and silence; Buñuel’s direction through outward gesture (the genre claims).
Command Of The Frame: Presence, one of the trained skills the acting schools name (topic 1); the genre claims are the gangster film’s tough, physically marked style and the western’s economy of words in close-up.
