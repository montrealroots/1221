The Calendar
Show Everything 
The Calendar panel at the top of Home shows what’s coming: the next thing you need to be at, how long until it, and up to five more after it. When the clock reaches a commitment, it stops and asks what you want to do. Press Go to be there. You can also skip a plan of your own, call off a rehearsal with a classmate, or press Stand Here to wait.
Where To Find It
Home, The Calendar Panel, Open Calendar.
When You Can Use It
From “What’s Coming”: the panel, Go, Skip It and the entries. Play, which moves the clock to a commitment, opens with “The Clock”.
Show Me Where 
What To Press
Read the Calendar panel on Home: the next thing you need to be at, how long until it, its date and time, and under it up to five more things coming, such as a card statement or a birthday.
Press an entry’s name or its time to open it under the panel: what it is, when, how long it runs, and what missing it costs.
When the clock stops at something, a dialog named for it says the time and how long going takes. Press Go to be there.
For a plan of your own, you can press Skip It instead, and for a rehearsal with a classmate, Call It Off. Stand Here closes the dialog and leaves the clock paused at the commitment.
What You Need First
The panel, Go, Skip It and the entries open with “What’s Coming”.
Only a plan you made offers Skip It. A rehearsal with a classmate offers Call It Off, which opens with “Drama School”. Anything else, such as a class or an appointment, has only Go and Stand Here.
To count as there, you need to be awake, out of hospital and in the commitment’s town when it runs. Yale Health’s assessment is the only commitment you can be at from a hospital bed. An injury that lays you up keeps you from everything else except the doctor, therapy, orientation and your own plans. The game never moves you to another town, and a plan is in the town where you made it.
Time And Money
Time
Reading the panel and opening entries doesn’t move the clock. Go spends the commitment’s own length, the figure its dialog gives.
Money
None
What Happens
Straight Away
Go: the commitment runs at once, with you there unless a hospital bed or an injury keeps you from it, and the clock moves through its hours, on to where it was headed if that’s later.
Skip It: your plan is marked missed, a notice says you didn’t go, and the clock carries on to where it was headed.
Call It Off, on a rehearsal with a classmate: it’s called off at short notice, which costs their trust and stays with them, and the clock stays paused where it stopped.
Stand Here: the dialog closes and the clock stays paused at the commitment, which stays at the top of the Calendar panel, marked now. It happens the next time the clock moves, and you’re there if you’re awake, in its town, and not kept away by a hospital bed or an injury.
Any stop pauses the clock, so press Play again afterwards.
Later On
A commitment missed costs what its own kind costs: classes an unexcused absence, a rehearsal call one counted against you, a performance a grave breach, a work-study shift its pay. An entry, opened, says which. The faculty weigh attendance at the end of each term’s classes.
A plan of your own you walked away from costs nothing beyond the plan.
Reading The Result
The time above the next commitment’s name counts down from now: “in 2h 30m” is two and a half hours away.
An entry, opened, says A Commitment or Happens Without You. The second, a card statement or the start of a term, runs whether you’re there or not.
A stop dialog with neither Skip It nor Call It Off is one you can go to or wait at, but not walk away from.
How It Connects
Comes First
The Clock: Play And Pause : A commitment only arrives as the clock moves, and Play opens with the chapter after this one.
Changes Straight Away
Moving Time On : Every move of the clock, Advance and Choose An Event among them, stops here at each commitment.
Counts Toward
Your Standing And Classes : Classes, calls and shifts you went to or missed are counted at the end of each term’s classes.
Worth Doing With It
Your Own Plans : The plans you put on the calendar yourself are the commitments Skip It works on.
Calling In Sick : Calling in sick before a school day begins excuses the commitments you’d otherwise miss.
Rehearsing With A Partner : A rehearsal with a classmate stops the clock like any commitment, and its dialog offers Call It Off, at a cost to their trust.
What Leads Here
The Guide And This Help : “What’s Coming” opens the calendar, and the dialog that asks what to do when a commitment comes up.
The Clock: Play And Pause : The live clock stops and pauses at each commitment on the calendar, and asks.
Moving Time On : Every jump stops at each commitment on the way, and the stop’s dialog asks whether you go.
Your Own Plans : A plan is a commitment: when the clock reaches it, the clock stops and asks Go or Skip It.
Sleep : Anything that needs you during the night happens without you and is missed.
Orientation Week : Each session is on your calendar, and the clock stops at it.
Rehearsing With A Partner : A rehearsal you agree goes on your calendar, and the clock stops at it.
Casting : Each call and performance goes on your calendar, and the clock stops at it.
Cabaret And Summer Cabaret : Joining puts every evening, call and show on your calendar at once.
The Doctor And The Pharmacy : A booked appointment is a commitment on the calendar, 20 minutes at 10:00 AM on a weekday that isn’t a public holiday, and the clock stops at it.
Conditions And Therapy : Begin A Course Of 8 Sessions puts a session on the calendar each week, a commitment the clock stops at.
What To Do Next
Answer each stop as it comes: Go to be there, or Stand Here to wait.
To put a plan of your own on the calendar, press Open Calendar on Home.
If You Can’t
Go, Skip It and the entries show Locked until “What’s Coming” is read; pressing one opens the guide there.
Skip It before the plan starts: “It hasn’t started yet. To drop it, open it on the Calendar and press Call It Off.”
Skip It on something the world booked: “Only a plan you made yourself can be skipped.”
Skip It on something already done or missed: “That’s already over: it was kept, missed or called off.”
Mistakes, And Putting Them Right
Stand Here and then Sleep sleeps through the commitment: it runs while you’re asleep and counts as missed.
Pressing Play after Stand Here skips nothing. The commitment runs at once, and you’re there for it live, hour by hour.
A commitment can’t be undone once it has run. Before it starts, you can call off a plan of your own on the Calendar screen.
What Decides It
What Needs You There
Anything booked for you to attend: classes, labs, rehearsal and performance calls, work-study shifts, orientation, the doctor, therapy, a partner’s rehearsal or lines with a classmate, and your own plans
Whether You’re There
When it runs, the game checks that you’re awake, out of hospital and in its town. In hospital, you were at nothing but Yale Health’s assessment. Asleep, you weren’t there
Where The Clock Was Headed
Go carries on the move the stop broke off, so a jump to the morning still ends in the morning

What comes of it. Registration runs with you there, and the clock carries on through the night to 7:00 AM, where the jump was headed. A night you didn’t choose was slept on the way, so energy stands at 74.

The Clock: Play And Pause
Show Everything 
Press Play at the top of the screen to start the clock, and Pause to stop it. While it runs, a minute passes in the game for every real minute: you get tired and hungry, what’s on your calendar happens, and the clock pauses itself at each commitment. It waits while the guide or this help is open.
Where To Find It
The Top Bar’s Play Or Pause Button.
When You Can Use It
From “The Clock”.
Show Me Where 
What To Press
Press Play in the top bar. The button then reads Pause.
Leave it running, and the day goes on around you a second at a time.
Press Pause to stop it where it is.
When it stops at a commitment, answer the dialog, then press Play again.
What You Need First
A new life begins paused, guided or made with “I Know SLATE”, so the world waits until you’re ready.
Play opens with “The Clock”. Pausing is never refused, and the world pauses itself at every stop.
Time And Money
Time
One world second for each real second, while it runs
Money
None for the button. What the hours bring is charged as they pass, the day’s groceries at midnight among it.
What Happens
Straight Away
Play: the clock runs from now, one to one. Pause: it stops where it is.
The setting is saved with the life, so Continue brings it back running or paused as you left it.
Later On
Each second costs what a waking second costs: energy and food run down, sleep debt builds, and what’s scheduled runs as its moment comes.
Past midnight with the clock running, your character stays up with you: the running clock moves at most 30 minutes at a step, and a night is only slept for you on a move of more than an hour. Press Sleep, or jump more than an hour with Advance, to sleep the night.
Left on Play while the device sleeps, the world keeps counting. Back on screen it catches up through every minute, stopping at the first commitment.
Save & Exit, or closing the tab, stops the world at that moment until you Continue.
Reading The Result
The button says what pressing it does: Play while the clock is stopped, Pause while it runs.
Reading Play when you didn’t pause means the clock stopped itself at a commitment, and the dialog for it names which.
How It Connects
Changes Straight Away
The Calendar : The live clock stops and pauses at each commitment on the calendar, and asks.
How You Are : Every second the clock runs is charged to your energy, food and the rest of your wellbeing as waking time, as it passes.
Worth Doing With It
Moving Time On : For hours you don’t want to wait through, Advance jumps them.
Sleep : A live clock keeps you up past midnight; Sleep is how the night gets slept.
What Leads Here
Home And Where You Are : The local time is the game’s clock, so it moves when the clock does.
The Guide And This Help : With the guide, the clock starts paused, and reading “The Clock” opens Play.
The Calendar : A commitment only arrives as the clock moves, and Play opens with the chapter after this one.
Moving Time On : The hours you’d rather live than skip, the live clock runs one to one.
Practice : Each hour of practice moves the clock 60 minutes and spends its energy.
What To Do Next
Press Play to start the day, and Pause when you step away.
To skip hours you don’t want to wait through, press Advance.
If You Can’t
Play shows Locked until “The Clock” is read, and pressing it opens the guide at that chapter.
A life that has ended keeps its clock where it stopped, and Play moves it no further.
Mistakes, And Putting Them Right
Leaving Play running overnight keeps your character up all night: energy keeps falling and no night is slept. Press Sleep before bed, or Pause.
A dialog you’re reading, such as the Sleep dialog or a plan’s form, doesn’t stop the clock. Only the guide and this help do, so press Pause first if you want time to think.
Pausing doesn’t put a commitment off. It waits at its moment, however long the clock is stopped.
What Decides It
The Wall Clock
Your device’s own clock, so a slow browser tab loses no time
Time Owed
Time that passed while the tab was hidden or the device slept, paid out in steps of up to 30 minutes
What Needs You There
Anything on the calendar you need to be at: the live clock stops there and pauses
Open Help
While the guide or this help is open, the clock waits, and the minutes you spend reading are never counted
A Worked Example
A class day at 8:30 AM, paused, with Classes at nine.
Press Play.
Wait half an hour of real time.
What comes of it. At 9:00 AM the clock stops by itself, the Classes dialog asks, and the button reads Play again. The half hour was charged to your energy and food on the way.

Moving Time On
Show Everything 
Press Advance in the top bar to move the clock ahead when you don’t want to wait: an hour, to this evening or tomorrow morning, to the next event, a week on, or to an event you choose. Everything on the way happens in order, and the clock stops at each commitment to ask what you want to do. A jump of more than an hour through the night means your character sleeps that night on their own.
Where To Find It
The Top Bar’s Advance Menu.
When You Can Use It
From “Moving Time On”.
Show Me Where 
What To Press
Press Advance in the top bar and pick a line: One Hour, This Evening (6:00 PM), Tonight (10:00 PM), Tomorrow Morning (7:00 AM), Next Event, Next Monday (9:00 AM) or One Week. Each says where it lands.
If the move passes a commitment, On The Way lists it first: press Advance to go on, or Cancel.
Or pick Choose An Event… for the next fourteen things on the calendar. Skip To Event shows what each jump crosses; press Skip To and the event’s name.
At each stop, answer the dialog: Go, Stand Here, Skip It on a plan of yours, or Call It Off on a rehearsal with a classmate.
What You Need First
Advance and Choose An Event… open with “Moving Time On”.
Next Event is gray when there’s nothing on the calendar ahead.
Time And Money
Time
The jump’s own length: an hour to a week from the menu, and as far as the event you choose
Money
None for the jump. What falls due on the way is paid as it falls due.
What Happens
Straight Away
The clock moves to the line’s time, or to the first commitment on the way, where it stops, pauses and asks.
Everything due on the way runs in order: the day’s groceries at midnight, a card statement, a payday.
Later On
A night slept on its own is a poorer one than a night you choose. It never lifts energy above 74 or brings stress under 14, it lasts as long as your stress and your sleep allow, and it clears less sleep debt.
A jump stopped by a commitment in the first hour of the night and carried on with Go is still the same jump, and the night comes as it would have.
Reading The Result
The gray detail on each line is where it lands; on Next Event, it names the thing.
In Skip To Event, “3 on the way” means the clock will stop three times before it gets there.
The count of things on your calendar on the way includes ones that don’t need you, such as a card statement.
How It Connects
Changes Straight Away
The Calendar : Every jump stops at each commitment on the way, and the stop’s dialog asks whether you go.
Sleep : A jump of more than an hour through the night sleeps your character from midnight, a night that stops at 74 energy.
Changes Later
Looking After Yourself : Each midnight crossed buys the day’s groceries and tops up nourishment and hygiene.
Worth Doing With It
The Clock: Play And Pause : The hours you’d rather live than skip, the live clock runs one to one.
What Leads Here
The Calendar : Every move of the clock, Advance and Choose An Event among them, stops here at each commitment.
The Clock: Play And Pause : For hours you don’t want to wait through, Advance jumps them.
Sleep : Skipping ahead through the night sleeps for you, but it’s a poorer night that stops at 74 energy.
What To Do Next
Before a long jump through the night, press Sleep instead: a night you choose puts back more.
Answer each stop on the way as it comes.
If You Can’t
Advance shows Locked until “Moving Time On” is read, and pressing it opens the guide there.
Time only runs forwards: no line moves the clock back.
A life that has ended doesn’t move.
Mistakes, And Putting Them Right
After midnight, Tomorrow Morning means seven on the next date, a whole day on. For this morning, use Next Event or One Hour.
One Hour at a time through the night keeps your character up: an hour is short enough to count as you being there. Sleep, or a longer jump, gives the night.
A jump never runs over a commitment. If it stops early, that’s the thing you need to be at; go on from it with Go.
What Decides It
Where It Lands
The line’s time, on your town’s clock
What’s On The Way
Everything on the calendar between now and then, run in order at its moment
How Far You Jump
More than an hour through the night, and your character sleeps from midnight on their own. An hour or less, and they stay up with you
A Worked Example
A weekday at 9:30 PM, with nothing booked until classes at nine the next morning.
Press Advance, then Tomorrow Morning (7:00 AM).
The clock runs through the night with no stop.
What comes of it. At 7:00 AM your character has slept the night on their own from midnight, and stands at 74 energy, or at what they had at midnight if that was more. A Sleep chosen at eleven would have put back more.

What Needs You
Show Everything 
What Needs You, on Home, holds the questions you need to answer and the notices that tell you what happened. A question has its answers as buttons, such as File It or Keep The Account, and Dismiss. A notice says what happened: groceries you couldn’t pay for, a commitment you slept through, a welcome to the school. The number beside Home on the menu counts both.
Where To Find It
Home, The What Needs You Panel.
When You Can Use It
From “What Needs You”.
Show Me Where 
What To Press
Open Home and read What Needs You: questions first, then notices.
Press one of a question’s answers to act on it there. It does what the same button on its own screen does.
Press Go There to open the screen a question or a notice is about.
Press Dismiss to take a question off the list without answering it.
Press Mark N Read, at the top of the panel, to mark every unread notice read at once.
Press Show All N, under the notices, to see every unread one before you do: the panel draws the six most important first, and Show The Six Most Important goes back to them.
Press a question’s or a notice’s title to open it: how urgent or important it is, when it closes, and where it sits in the list.
What You Need First
The panel, Mark Read, the answers and Dismiss open with “What Needs You”.
An answer does what the same button does on its own screen, so that screen’s chapter has to have been read too.
Time And Money
Time
None: reading, dismissing and marking read move no clock
Money
None for the panel. An answer costs what the same button costs on its own screen, a fee or a payment.
What Happens
Straight Away
An answer does what its button does on its own screen. Done, the question closes; if it can’t be done, the question stays open and the reason shows.
Dismiss closes the question unanswered. What it was about stands: a return still falls due, and can still be filed from its screen.
Mark Read marks every unread notice read, and the count beside Home falls by that many.
Later On
Every question has a date. If you don’t answer it, it closes by itself within two days after that date.
You can still open a closed question for 180 days after it closes, and SLATE keeps your latest 500 notices.
The same thing raised again after you dismissed it comes back as a new question.
A notice said again word for word while the last is still unread takes the new time, and isn’t added twice.
Reading The Result
The stripe beside a question turns amber at an urgency of 65 and red at 85.
Overdue, in red, means a question’s date has passed, and it closes within two days.
All Clear means every question is answered and every notice read, nothing is owed past due, the bank and the card are in order, and the calendar has no clash for a month. No Questions Waiting lists what still weighs: money owed, an overdrawn account, a card near its limit or behind, a late return, a clash.
Home shows six questions, with “N more waiting.” for the rest, and the six most important unread notices, with “N more unread.” and Show All N for the rest. A question moves up as those above it clear. Mark N Read marks every unread notice read, shown or not.

Worth Doing With It

Your Own Plans
Show Everything 
To put a plan of your own on the calendar, press Open Calendar on Home, pick a day and press Put Something On It. Give it a name, a start time on the half hour and a length from half an hour to all day. The clock stops at your plan like any commitment, and Call It Off takes it off before it starts. A plan costs no money and changes nothing in your wellbeing beyond the hours it takes.
Where To Find It
The Calendar Screen, From Open Calendar On Home.
When You Can Use It
From “Your Own Plans”. The Calendar screen itself opens with “What’s Coming”.
Show Me Where 
What To Press
Press Open Calendar on Home’s Calendar panel, or C anywhere.
Page the months with ‹ and ›, or Today, and press a day to open it under the month.
Press Put Something On It. Fill in What Is It, When It Starts and How Long It Takes, then press Put It On.
To take a plan off, press it on its day or in Next Thirty Days, then Call It Off.
Press a plan’s name on Home to open it, and from there its hours and the rest of your plans.
What You Need First
A day that hasn’t ended: a past day has no Put Something On It.
A name, up to 80 characters; Put It On stays gray until it has one.
To call it off, a plan of yours whose start is still ahead.
Time And Money
Time
Making or calling off a plan moves no clock. Going to one spends its length.
Money
None
What Happens
Straight Away
Put It On: the plan joins the calendar at once, on its day and in Next Thirty Days, and on Home once it’s among the next few things coming. A clash with anything else you need to be at is marked Clash, and allowed.
Call It Off: it leaves the calendar, and its hours are free again.
Later On
When the clock reaches it, the clock stops and asks: Go spends its length, and Skip It marks it missed, with a notice.
Inside a Sleep you chose, it comes while you’re asleep and is missed, and the morning’s notice names it.
In hospital when it comes, it’s missed even if you press Go, and no notice says so.
Before it begins, it keeps its time free: you can’t start an hour of practice, or anything in Look After Yourself, that would run into it, and the reason says what’s coming and when.
While you’re at it, Sleep and everything in Look After Yourself but Eat A Meal wait until it ends.
Reading The Result
Clash marks two things you need to be at, at the same time; the red box at the top of the screen counts the clashes in the next month.
You, beside an entry in Next Thirty Days, means you need to be there.
A plan’s status reads Pending, Completed, Missed or Canceled.
Your Own Plans counts what’s Waiting, Called Off and Gone By.
A plan’s hours, opened, give the temperature across them and how much of them is dark or wet.

Looking After Yourself : A plan about to begin refuses anything in Look After Yourself that would run into it.

What Needs You : Skipping a plan, or sleeping through one, leaves a notice in What Needs You.

The Weather
Show Everything 
The Weather panel on Home shows the weather in your town: now, today’s high and low, humidity and wind, and the next five days. Press any figure for more: a day opens into its hours, and the sky into the week ahead. The weather changes one thing in the game: whether a workout is a run. It’s worked out from your town’s climate, the season and a little chance, so it’s the same every time you look.
Where To Find It
Home, The Weather Panel.
When You Can Use It
From “Weather”.
Show Me Where 
What To Press
Read the Weather panel on Home: the temperature now and the condition, the high and the low, humidity, wind and the climate, and five days after today.

What Happens
Straight Away
Opening the sky, a day or an hour changes nothing: the weather is calculated as you look, and never stored.
Later On
Work Out checks the weather as its hour ends. Dry, at 0°C (32°F) or warmer and feeling no hotter than 32°C (90°F) with the humidity, it was a run, which builds 10% more fitness than a workout indoors.
The flu comes in the weeks your town’s climate brings it: its coldest weeks where the coldest month averages 18°C or less, its wettest where that month averages 21°C or more, and a blend between. That goes by the climate’s usual year, never the day on the panel.
Reading The Result
Against Usual is how far the day sits from the date’s normal, in the degrees on show.
The high comes around 3 PM and the low around 3 AM. A wet day rains for a spell of three to nine hours and is overcast either side of it.
Fahrenheit and miles an hour in the United States and the few other countries that read Fahrenheit; Celsius and kilometers an hour elsewhere.
The weather is estimated from a climate model of the town, so a day ahead is as fixed as today: what it shows for Thursday is what Thursday brings.

Mistakes, And Putting Them Right
Expecting rain to call off a class or a shift: it changes neither. The one thing it changes is whether a workout is a run.
Judging a run by the sky now: a workout checks the weather as its hour ends, and so does its line in Look After Yourself, which tells you before you press it whether it’ll be a run.
What Decides It
Your Town
Its climate, its latitude and the date’s usual high, low, chance of a wet day and cloud
The Day’s Luck
A random draw, fixed for each town and day in your save: its warmth blended with the two days before it and its wet with the day before, so a cold snap lasts and a wet spell lingers
Thunder In New Haven
About 25 days a year, estimated for a continental climate, where no count is published (estimated) 
A Worked Example
A mild, dry morning in New Haven, with an hour free for Work Out.
Press the temperature on Home to open the sky, then The Rest Of Today.
Check the next hour is dry, and not freezing or sweltering.
Open Look After Yourself and press Work Out.
What comes of it. The hour was a run: 10% more fitness than the same hour indoors, for more calories burned and a run’s higher chance of an injury. The Work Out line had said so before you pressed it, opening “A run in fine weather”.
