Dispositions

Your temperament, not your talent. Each one changes a part of your life:

Emotional Stability: how fast stress builds and clears, and how much Composure you start with.
Conscientiousness: how well you sleep, and how much Discipline you start with.
Ambition: how fast stress builds, from work and from money alike.
Risk Tolerance: above 65, your card’s autopay starts out paying only the minimum, so you carry a balance.
Extraversion: whether performing in front of people, an audience or your class, raises your stress or eases it.
Openness: whether rehearsing new work, a festival’s new play or a Cabaret piece, lifts your morale or lowers it.
Agreeableness: whether a day of rehearsal lifts your morale or lowers it.

Apart from your starting Composure and Discipline, none of these makes you a better or worse actor. Composure is one of the abilities your performances are judged on.

Traits

Pick up to four. Traits come in opposing pairs, and you can’t have both of a pair. Between them, they change:

how fast stress builds and clears;
how well you sleep, and how much a night restores;
how soon you need a shower;
how far your morale swings;
how hard an illness hits;
how sharp you are in the morning or the evening;
which part of preparing a scene goes furthest;
how you warm to classmates, and how much a slight from one hurts;
and, for Frugal, Spender and Generous, what your food costs and how much a meal lifts you.

A trait’s description and cost describe the whole person, but the game uses only the effects above. Once you’re playing, open a trait on your Player Profile to see exactly what it changes.

0 / 4
TEMPERAMENT
Even Tempered
Very little gets through. Bad news lands on you the same way good news does.
People read the calm as not caring, and drift away from it.
Quick Tempered
It comes up fast and it’s gone just as fast, which isn’t much comfort to whoever was standing there.
Stress arrives quicker and relationships wear.
Highly Strung
You feel everything at full volume. It’s exhausting, and it’s where the work comes from.
Stress builds fast and sleep is the first thing to go.
Unshakeable
Whatever happens, you’re the one still thinking clearly twenty minutes later.
You’re slow to notice something is genuinely wrong, including with you.
Sentimental
Birthdays, first drafts, the people who were there at the start. You keep all of it.
Endings go badly, and you hold on well past the point of any use.
Detached
You can put a thing down and walk away from it, including people.
Everything you have drifts unless you tend it: a classmate comes closer more slowly.
HABITS
Early Riser
Up before anybody wants you, and the best hours of your day are over by ten.
An evening call finds you flat: rehearsals and performances get less of you.
Night Owl
The good hours start after midnight, and none of them are before noon.
Every morning class is a fight with yourself, and you sleep badly.
Orderly
Everything has a place and you notice immediately when it isn’t in it.
Disorder costs you more than it costs other people.
Chaotic
The mess is genuinely invisible to you, and it never weighs on you.
It shows on you sooner.
Homebody
Your own bed is where you recover, and it puts more back than most.
A full room does less for you: friends in a cast lift you less.
Restless
Stillness is beyond you, and a room full of people is where you come alive.
You don’t rest properly, and a cast without a friend in it weighs on you.
BODY
Disciplined
The training, the sleeping, the eating: all of it belongs to a person who means to still be working at sixty.
The hours it takes are hours you aren’t spending on anybody.
Indulgent
Late food, late nights, and the firm belief you’ll deal with it later.
You’ll deal with it later, and it will cost more then.
Resilient
Illness, injury, a bad year: all of it clears faster in you than in the people around you.
None. This one is simply luck.

CHAPTER 1 OF 17
Your Life, Hour By Hour

How SLATE works, and how to read Home.

In SLATE you live one life, hour by hour, in a real town with its own time, seasons, weather, prices, money and laws. This guide shows you how, one part at a time, and each chapter you read opens the part of the game it explains.

You start on Home. The line under the greeting shows the time, the town, its time zone, the season and the weather. Where You Are, further down, tells you more about the town.

Underlined words are links: press one to see more. Press the town’s name and its details open just below, with more links inside for its region, its country and its money. The date, the season and the daylight work the same way.

Some words only become links once you’ve read the guide chapter that covers them. A panel you can’t use yet says Locked and names the chapter to read. Press its Read About It button to go straight there.

When you’ve read a chapter, press I’ve Read It at its foot to open what it covers, listed under What It Opens. Then press Next, which names the chapter after it, to read on, or Try It to close the guide and use what just opened. Later closes the guide without opening anything, and the line at the top of the screen keeps a Read button for the next chapter.

Once you’ve read its chapter, you can open each part of your life from Home and from the menu down the left. On a small screen, press Menu to see the menu.

Each panel has a How This Works button that explains what the panel does and how it connects to the rest of the game. Press the ? key anywhere for the help’s contents, or the / key to search it. The clock waits while you read the help or this guide.

Save & Exit, in the top bar, saves your life and takes you back to the save slots. Press Continue there to pick up at the same moment.

A new life starts with the clock paused, so nothing happens until you’re ready. Read “What’s Coming” next to open the Calendar panel.

CHAPTER 4 OF 17
Moving Time On

Jumping ahead, and what happens on the way.

To jump ahead instead of waiting, press Advance in the top bar and choose how far: One Hour, This Evening (6 p.m.), Tonight (10 p.m.), Tomorrow Morning (7 a.m.), Next Event, Next Monday (9 a.m.) or One Week. Choose An Event opens Skip To Event: pick one of the next fourteen things on your calendar and press Skip To with its name.

Everything on the way happens, in order. If the jump would pass any commitments, a dialog called On The Way first says how many: press Advance there to go on, or Cancel to stay where you are. The clock still stops at each commitment to ask what you want to do.

Time only moves forward, so you can’t undo a jump.

Read “What Needs You” next: it’s where you find out what happened.
