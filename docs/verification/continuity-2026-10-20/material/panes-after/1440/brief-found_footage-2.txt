Night Two At Juniper Creek
The Tape For Later

On the last night of 1999, a college sophomore records a message to her future self on her roommate’s camcorder, and the roommate, behind the lens, won’t let her leave out the real news.

THE SCENE
Where And When
A dorm room on New Year’s Eve, 1999, an hour before midnight, the hallway loud with a party. The tape is meant for the campus time capsule, to be opened in twenty-five years, and this is what was found in it. Her roommate holds the camcorder. On your tape the lens stands in for it, and the reader is beside the lens as Dana: talk to the lens for her future self and just beside it for Dana. Yesterday she withdrew from school to go home and look after her father, and she has told no one but Dana.
The Two Of You
Roommates for a year and a half, closer than sisters, who record everything.
You Play
Rosa Cordero, nineteen, a sophomore who meant to be a marine biologist.
The Reader Plays
Dana, her roommate, behind the camcorder, asking questions from off camera.
How They Play It
Steady: playing opposite it asks nothing more than the scene does.
The Genre Asks
Caught Live: Behavior that looks unrehearsed: real listening, the next line taken from what the other person just did. A self-tape in it is judged more on connection and responsiveness, and calls on your listening and responsiveness and adaptability. Rehearsing on your feet: what looks unrehearsed is found by playing it through until nothing is set but the wants.
States At The Limit: Fear, grief or exhaustion at their extreme, sustained, and believed though the threat is often not in the room. A self-tape in it is judged more on believability and execution, and calls on your emotional depth and physical expression. The lines, then rehearsal on your feet: an extreme state has to be found in the body, on lines you no longer think about.
It routinely uses improvisation and naturalistic acting, in fear, often shot by the actors themselves. This comes from the genre’s research, mostly held with low confidence.
What You Want
Make a cheerful tape her future self will enjoy, and keep the withdrawal off it.
What’s In The Way
Dana believes the tape is worthless unless it holds the truth, and she won’t stop recording or asking.
WHERE IT TURNS
She says a goofy hello to the future: predictions, a tour of the room, a little dance to the party through the wall. (Comic)
Dana asks, from behind the camera, what she’s doing next semester, and she dodges with a joke. (Comic and Grave, held at once)
She tells the lens the truth, and asks her future self whether it was the right call. (Lyric and Grave, held at once)

2 changes of tone between beats and 2 beats held two ways. Rehearsal is complete at 50% in a beat, up to 15% more in a beat a change leads into, and 8% more in one held two ways.

WHAT THE TAPE FOR LATER ASKS
Everyday Speech, on camera
Timing: Some
Physical Work: Some
Playing Off The Partner: Built On It. Dana steers every turn from behind the lens, and Rosa plays two eyelines: the lens for the future, and just beside it for her friend.
Freedom To Improvise: Built On It. The brief gives the beats and the objective and leaves every word to you as you tape, the way found footage is made.
Tone: comic to grave to lyric, 2 changes of tone to play. A home video, a question dodged, and the truth told to a listener who doesn’t exist yet.
Techniques It Asks: Improvisation. The tape is improvised: you find every line live inside the beats, in answer to what Dana asks. Talking to the lens is the on-camera work every self-tape asks for.
It Only Takes: Open. With no written lines, the words come fresh from what the reader asks in each take, so it can only be played open, never set and repeated.
