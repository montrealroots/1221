The Steward Of The Cellar

A privateer caught stealing back her ship’s papers from a governor’s wine cellar finds that the elderly steward who corners her there was once the finest sword in the colony.

THE SCENE
Where And When
The wine cellar of the governor’s house in an invented island colony of the seventeenth century, past midnight. The letters that make her a privateer and not a pirate were seized last week and locked in the cellar strongbox; she has them inside her coat. The steward came down for a bottle, found her, and took a rapier down from the wall.
The Two Of You
Strangers: she knows him as the stooped old man who pours the governor’s wine, and he knows her from the wanted bills.
You Play
Captain Ysolde Marrec, thirty, a privateer with a fast ship, a quick tongue and less fencing than her reputation.
The Reader Plays
Benedek, the governor’s steward, seventy, courteous and unhurried, pressing her harder with every pass.
How They Play It
Pressing: playing opposite it, the tape counts responsiveness for more and leans on your adaptability.
The Genre Asks
Command Of The Frame: Status and threat carried in the body and the look more than the words: the person everyone in the room watches. A self-tape in it is judged more on connection and execution, and calls on your presence and physical expression. Rehearsing on your feet: status is found standing, in how little you need to move.
Played Large: Feeling worn on the outside and sized to fill the frame: the voice and the body carry it as much as the face. A self-tape in it is judged more on execution and connection, and calls on your vocal expression, physical expression and presence. Rehearsing on your feet: a size the body and the voice can carry has to be found aloud and standing.
Swordplay and daring heroes, at full theatrical size. This comes from the genre’s research, mostly held with low confidence.
What You Want
Get out of the cellar with the papers before the watch changes at one.
What’s In The Way
He’s better with a sword than she is, and every trick she tries he seems to have seen before.
WHERE IT TURNS
She tries charm, a forged pass and a bribe in his own master’s wine, and he declines each with a bow. (Comic)
They fence, three passes marked solo to his called cues, and on the third she plays her blade knocked from her grip and lowers it to the floor at her side. (Comic and Grave, held at once)
Disarmed and at his point, she asks him why a swordsman like him pours wine, and he tells her. (Grave)
She offers him a place on her ship as master-at-arms, and he unties his apron. (Comic and Lyric, held at once)

3 changes of tone between beats and 2 beats held two ways. Rehearsal is complete at 50% in a beat, up to 15% more in a beat a change leads into, and 8% more in one held two ways.

WHAT THE STEWARD OF THE CELLAR ASKS
Heightened Prose, on camera: The lines are period courtesy sharpened into wit on both sides, and the old man’s formality is a weapon of its own.
Timing: Much. Her jokes and his bows are timed against the passes, and the watch changing at one sets the clock.
Physical Work: Built On It. She fights three passes in a narrow cellar between the racks, plays a disarm on her own side, then holds a duelist’s stance with no blade at all.
Playing Off The Partner: Built On It. He presses her harder with each pass and each courtesy, and she has to read him to find her next trick and, in the end, her offer.
Vocal Demand: Some
Tone: comic to grave to lyric, 3 changes of tone to play. Tricks that fail, a fight she loses, a question that reaches him, then a recruitment where an arrest should be.
Techniques It Asks: Stage Combat, Comedy. The fencing is stage combat: mark three set passes solo with a replica rapier at reduced speed against the reader’s called cues, never pointed at the lens or the reader. Play the disarm with your own hand and lower the replica to the floor at your side, so it repeats safely on every take. Her charm and her bluffs are played for laughs against his patience, a job for comic timing.

YOUR READING
How This Works
The Fight Is Real. The passes are the scene: she fights for her life in a cellar against a man who could kill her. It rests on: Three passes in a narrow cellar end with his point at her and her blade on the floor. Fully prepared, it would come to 53.8 for you now.
All Charm. She never stops performing, and losing the duel is only a cue for her best line. It rests on: She has a quick tongue and tries charm, a forged pass and a bribe first. Fully prepared, it would come to 52.7 for you now.
One Fencer To Another. The moment he beats her she stops playing the rogue, and the offer is one fighter’s respect for a better one. It rests on: He was once the finest sword in the colony, and she asks him why a swordsman pours wine. Fully prepared, it would come to 54.1 for you now.
