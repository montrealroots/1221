The Loading Bay
The Safe Behind The Ring

A night-shift guard who has just been knocked down by an intruder has twenty seconds to get the keys back before the shutter closes on her colleague.

THE SCENE
Where And When
A distribution warehouse at three in the morning. The intruder took the shutter keys off her belt in the struggle; her colleague is pinned behind a fallen pallet on the far side, and the shutter is coming down on its timer.
The Two Of You
Strangers: she has never seen the intruder before, and he wants only to get out with the keys.
You Play
Dana, a security guard in her second month, winded and on her knees.
The Reader Plays
The intruder, backing toward the shutter with the keys.
How They Play It
Steady: playing opposite it asks nothing more than the scene does.
The Genre Asks
Command Of The Frame: Status and threat carried in the body and the look more than the words: the person everyone in the room watches. A self-tape in it is judged more on connection and execution, and calls on your presence and physical expression. Rehearsing on your feet: status is found standing, in how little you need to move.
Suspense Held: A threat kept alive under every line: what’s at stake never dropped, even in the quiet. A self-tape in it is judged more on consistency and interpretation, and calls on your focus and listening and responsiveness. The breakdown, then rehearsal: suspense is held by knowing at every moment what your character fears will happen, and keeping it alive through the scene.
Its chases are stunt work and its fights stage combat; between them, the scenes turn on who holds the frame and what the threat is. The research found nothing on how it’s played, so this is estimated from its conventions.
What You Want
Get the keys back before the shutter reaches the floor.
What’s In The Way
He is bigger, he has the keys, and every second she spends talking the shutter drops another foot.
WHERE IT TURNS
She gets up and tries reason: he can leave, she only needs the keys. (Grave)
He refuses; she closes the distance, and the fight is two moves: his arm goes wide off frame and the keys drop at her feet. (Grave)
Keys in hand, she drops to her knees at the gap under the shutter, calling her colleague’s name as it lands. (Grave and Lyric, held at once)

1 change of tone between beats and 1 beat held two ways. Rehearsal is complete at 50% in a beat, up to 15% more in a beat a change leads into, and 8% more in one held two ways.

WHAT THE LOADING BAY ASKS
Everyday Speech, on camera
Timing: Much. Every line races the falling shutter, so each beat has to land to the second.
Physical Work: Built On It. The scene is a struggle staged to the shutter’s timer: the getting up, the two moves, the drop to the gap.
Playing Off The Partner: Much. What she tries depends on what he does with the keys.
Vocal Demand: Some
Tone: grave to lyric, 1 change of tone to play. Reason, then force, then relief at having the keys, held together with fear as the shutter lands.
Techniques It Asks: Stage Combat. The two moves are a staged fight. Mark them solo at reduced speed against the reader’s called cues from off camera, with no contact and the keys on your side of the frame, so they repeat exactly on every take.
