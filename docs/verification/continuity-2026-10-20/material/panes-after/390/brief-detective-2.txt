Three Cups
Seven Minutes Fast

A small-town clock repairer tells the inspector that a respected man’s alibi rests on the church clock, and that she set it seven minutes fast herself.

THE SCENE
Where And When
A clock repair shop on a small town’s main street, the morning after a farmhand was charged with emptying the feed store’s safe. The store owner’s brother-in-law says he left the tavern as the church clock struck ten, and the safe was opened at five to. She winds and sets the church clock every Friday, and last Friday she set it fast on purpose, so the bell-ringers would stop arriving late.
The Two Of You
They were at school together; he became the town’s inspector and she took over her father’s shop, and he has never once asked her opinion.
You Play
Ottilie Brandt, sixty, clock repairer, with a loupe on a cord and the church clock’s escapement on her bench.
The Reader Plays
Inspector Hollins, sixty, impatient, with the press waiting outside.
How They Play It
Pressing: playing opposite it, the tape counts responsiveness for more and leans on your adaptability.
The Genre Asks
Dense Text, Exact: A lot of words, or difficult ones, delivered fluently and at speed: jargon, argument or deduction as second nature. A self-tape in it is judged more on execution and interpretation, and calls on your memory and focus. Learning the lines: fluency at speed is the lines learned past knowing them.
Suspense Held: A threat kept alive under every line: what’s at stake never dropped, even in the quiet. A self-tape in it is judged more on consistency and interpretation, and calls on your focus and listening and responsiveness. The breakdown, then rehearsal: suspense is held by knowing at every moment what your character fears will happen, and keeping it alive through the scene.
Long-running detectives asked for rapid, exact delivery of deduction speeches. This comes from the genre’s research, mostly held with low confidence.
What You Want
Get him to release the farmhand and look again at the brother-in-law.
What’s In The Way
He has made his arrest and the press is waiting, the brother-in-law is a churchwarden, and her only evidence is a confession that she tampered with the town clock.
WHERE IT TURNS
She explains the clock’s workings with its parts laid out on the bench, in far more detail than he wants. (Comic)
He tries to leave; she walks him through the night minute by minute, correcting his timeline at each step. (Grave)
She admits she set the clock fast herself, for the bell-ringers, and is more embarrassed by that than by anything. (Comic and Grave, held at once)
She tells him what seven minutes are long enough to do, and whose minutes they were. (Grave)

3 changes of tone between beats and 1 beat held two ways. Rehearsal is complete at 50% in a beat, up to 15% more in a beat a change leads into, and 8% more in one held two ways.

WHAT SEVEN MINUTES FAST ASKS
Everyday Speech, on camera
Timing: Built On It. Her case is a timeline told to the minute, and each correction has to land before he can talk over it.
Physical Work: Some. She works with clock parts and a loupe at her bench on her side of the frame, and the parts are how she shows him the minutes.
Playing Off The Partner: Much. He’s trying to leave from the first line, and she has to keep him in the room by finding what will hold him.
Tone: comic to grave, 3 changes of tone to play. A lecture he doesn’t want, the timeline told against his hurry, an embarrassing confession, then the name.
