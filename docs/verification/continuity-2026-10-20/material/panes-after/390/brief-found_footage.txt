Night Two At Juniper Creek
The Tape For Later

A woman filming a camping trip for her mother finds their late father’s fishing lure hanging from the tent zip, and keeps the camera running while she talks her brother into leaving.

THE SCENE
Where And When
A creekside campsite in a state forest at first light. She and her brother came to film their late father’s favorite fishing spot for their mother, who can’t travel. This morning his old red fishing lure was hanging from the outside of the tent zip, and she knows she left it in his tackle box at home. The camera is hers, held at arm’s length or turned on the campsite; her brother stays just out of frame. For the tape, any plain space behind you will do for the campsite: her words supply the creek and the trees.
The Two Of You
Brother and sister, two years apart, who still argue like teenagers.
You Play
Kayla Iverson, twenty-six, who promised her mother the whole trip on tape.
The Reader Plays
Josh, her younger brother, who thinks it’s a prank and wants to stay.
How They Play It
Steady: playing opposite it asks nothing more than the scene does.
The Genre Asks
Caught Live: Behavior that looks unrehearsed: real listening, the next line taken from what the other person just did. A self-tape in it is judged more on connection and responsiveness, and calls on your listening and responsiveness and adaptability. Rehearsing on your feet: what looks unrehearsed is found by playing it through until nothing is set but the wants.
States At The Limit: Fear, grief or exhaustion at their extreme, sustained, and believed though the threat is often not in the room. A self-tape in it is judged more on believability and execution, and calls on your emotional depth and physical expression. The lines, then rehearsal on your feet: an extreme state has to be found in the body, on lines you no longer think about.
It routinely uses improvisation and naturalistic acting, in fear, often shot by the actors themselves. This comes from the genre’s research, mostly held with low confidence.
What You Want
Get him to pack up and leave now, without frightening him or their mother when she watches.
What’s In The Way
He thinks she’s being dramatic for the camera, and she can’t stop filming without breaking her promise.
WHERE IT TURNS
A cheerful good morning to the lens for Mom, panning across the creek. (Comic and Lyric, held at once)
She finds the lure on the zip and describes it calmly, for the tape. (Grave)
She argues with Josh while keeping the camera steady, and doesn’t mention what she heard in the night. (Grave)
She turns the camera on the treeline and keeps talking to fill the quiet. (Grave and Lyric, held at once)

2 changes of tone between beats and 2 beats held two ways. Rehearsal is complete at 50% in a beat, up to 15% more in a beat a change leads into, and 8% more in one held two ways.

WHAT NIGHT TWO AT JUNIPER CREEK ASKS
Everyday Speech, on camera
Timing: Some
Physical Work: Some
Playing Off The Partner: Much. What she says to Josh depends on how far he’s taking it as a joke.
Freedom To Improvise: Built On It. The brief gives the beats and the objective but no lines, so you find every word as you tape.
Tone: comic to lyric to grave, 2 changes of tone to play. A home video, a discovery played down for her mother’s sake, an argument, and a silence she has to fill.
Techniques It Asks: Improvisation. You improvise the lines inside the outline of beats, the way found footage is made, so the scene asks for improvisation. The camera she holds is the on-camera work every self-tape asks for.
It Only Takes: Open. With no written lines, each take’s words come live from what the reader says, so it can only be played open, never fixed and repeated.
