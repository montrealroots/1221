Friday’s Count
Tuesday Night, All Night

After closing, a diner waitress’s ex-husband asks her to tell the police he sat at her counter all last Tuesday night, the night a warehouse was robbed.

THE SCENE
Where And When
A highway diner after closing on a Sunday, half the chairs already up on the tables. Last Tuesday a warehouse two miles away was robbed and its night guard was hurt. Detectives are coming tomorrow to ask about the regulars. He came in the back way.
The Two Of You
Divorced six years, with a son of fourteen who lives with her and worships him. He was never violent, only always somewhere he shouldn’t have been.
You Play
Darlene Voss, forty, the waitress who closes up, putting chairs up on the tables.
The Reader Plays
Cal, her ex-husband, at the counter, who swings from charm to threat and back without warning.
How They Play It
Unpredictable: playing opposite it, the tape counts responsiveness and consistency for more and leans on your adaptability and composure.
The Genre Asks
Suspense Held: A threat kept alive under every line: what’s at stake never dropped, even in the quiet. A self-tape in it is judged more on consistency and interpretation, and calls on your focus and listening and responsiveness. The breakdown, then rehearsal: suspense is held by knowing at every moment what your character fears will happen, and keeping it alive through the scene.
Crime’s motives and consequences: what’s at risk kept alive in the scene. The research found nothing on how it’s played, so this is estimated from its conventions.
What You Want
Find out what he did that night before she agrees to anything.
What’s In The Way
The more he tells her, the more she’s part of it, he keeps bringing their son into it, and she still can’t tell when he’s lying.
WHERE IT TURNS
He jokes about the pie, the lights, the old days, and she keeps putting chairs up. (Comic)
He asks for the alibi plainly: the counter, all night, coffee and the crossword. (Grave)
She asks what he did; he tells her a little, and laughs at the part about the guard. (Grave and Grotesque, held at once)
He says their son would want her to do it, and she sets the chair down. (Grave)
She names her price, and he laughs, and then sees she means it. (Comic and Grave, held at once)

4 changes of tone between beats and 2 beats held two ways. Rehearsal is complete at 50% in a beat, up to 15% more in a beat a change leads into, and 8% more in one held two ways.

WHAT TUESDAY NIGHT, ALL NIGHT ASKS
Everyday Speech, on camera
Timing: Some
Physical Work: Some. She puts chairs up on tables on her side of the frame through the first beats, and the scene turns when she stops.
Playing Off The Partner: Built On It. He changes from charm to threat without warning, and every decision she makes comes from reading which one he’s playing.
Tone: comic to grave to grotesque, 4 changes of tone to play. Old banter, the request, a laugh where there shouldn’t be one, their son used against her, then a price he didn’t expect.
