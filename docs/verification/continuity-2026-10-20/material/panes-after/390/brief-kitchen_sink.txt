Not Going Back
One More Friday

A young mill worker in a northern English town tells his mother at the kitchen table that he walked out of the mill today and isn’t going back.

THE SCENE
Where And When
The back kitchen of a terraced house in a northern English mill town in 1959, on a Friday evening, his father’s tea keeping warm on the stove. He walked off his shift at noon after a row with the foreman and spent the afternoon at the bus station reading the timetables. His father has worked at the same mill for thirty years and will be home at six.
The Two Of You
Mother and son, close in the way of people who never say so; she’s the one he tells things to first.
You Play
Dennis Hartley, twenty, who has never been farther than the coast.
The Reader Plays
His mother, Edna Hartley, fifty, peeling potatoes, who has already heard about the row from a neighbor.
How They Play It
Steady: playing opposite it asks nothing more than the scene does.
The Genre Asks
Caught Live: Behavior that looks unrehearsed: real listening, the next line taken from what the other person just did. A self-tape in it is judged more on connection and responsiveness, and calls on your listening and responsiveness and adaptability. Rehearsing on your feet: what looks unrehearsed is found by playing it through until nothing is set but the wants.
Unknown regional actors with their own accents, breaking with the educated stage voice. This comes from the genre’s research, mostly held with low confidence.
What You Want
Get his mother on his side before his father walks in.
What’s In The Way
She already knows, she’s frightened for him, and the wage he walked away from paid the rent.
WHERE IT TURNS
He comes in breezy, and she lets him lie about his day for a minute. (Comic and Grave, held at once)
She tells him she knows; he says he’s done, he won’t spend his life at a loom. (Grave)
She asks where he’d go; he has the bus timetable in his pocket and reads her the times, and she asks to hear them again. (Grave and Lyric, held at once)
His father’s step in the yard, and she tells him to put the timetable away and say nothing. (Grave)

3 changes of tone between beats and 2 beats held two ways. Rehearsal is complete at 50% in a beat, up to 15% more in a beat a change leads into, and 8% more in one held two ways.

WHAT NOT GOING BACK ASKS
Everyday Speech, on camera
Timing: Some
Playing Off The Partner: Built On It. She knows more than he thinks, and what she lets on decides each of his turns.
Vocal Demand: Much. The speech of the town is in every line, and the feeling of the scene has to come through it.
Tone: comic to grave to lyric, 3 changes of tone to play. A cover story, the truth, a mother who takes his side, and a secret kept from the man coming in.
Techniques It Asks: Accents And Dialects. The lines are written in the regional working-class speech of the town, “Mam” and “our kid” among it. Play the accent as the way Dennis and his mother talk, with the dignity it’s owed, never as color or a joke.

YOUR READING
How This Works
Angry Young Man. Dennis is furious at the life laid out for him, and the scene is his anger finding the one person who’ll hear it. It rests on: He walked off after a row with the foreman and won’t spend his life at a loom. Fully prepared, it would come to 58.2 for you now.
Her Son. Under the anger he needs his mother’s blessing, and every line is a boy asking if he’s allowed to go. It rests on: She’s the one he tells things to first, and she tells him to hide the timetable from his father. Fully prepared, it would come to 58.5 for you now.
