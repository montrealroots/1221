The Household Count
The Approved Lesson

In a state where every classroom is recorded, a history teacher has to answer a student’s question about a date erased from the textbooks without the room’s microphones catching the answer.

THE SCENE
Where And When
A high school classroom at the end of the last period on a Friday, in a state where history is taught from one approved text and every classroom is filmed and recorded. Last week a teacher in the next district was dismissed for an unapproved answer. A student has just asked, in front of the class, what happened on a date the approved text skips.
The Two Of You
Teacher and pupil of two years. He’s the cleverest in the class and has never asked a dangerous question before, so she can’t tell whether he was sent to.
You Play
Ms. Renata Fennimore, forty-eight, history teacher, at the board with a marker.
The Reader Plays
Aurel, fifteen, at the front desk, who has asked his question and waits in silence for the answer.
How They Play It
Withholding: playing opposite it, the tape counts connection for more and leans on your listening and responsiveness.
The Genre Asks
Belief In What Isn’t There: An invented world made concrete: the creature, the machine or the magic played as specifically as a cup of tea. A self-tape in it is judged more on believability and interpretation, and calls on your imagination. The breakdown: the world’s rules have to be worked out before they can be believed.
Feeling Held In: The feeling is there and kept under: stillness, economy, what isn’t said carrying what is. A self-tape in it is judged more on consistency and believability, and calls on your composure and focus. Learning the lines and rehearsing on your feet: control comes from knowing it well enough to hold it still.
An imagined society people are afraid in: its rules made concrete, and fear kept off the face. The research found nothing on how it’s played, so this is estimated from its conventions.
What You Want
Give him the true answer without the microphones recording it.
What’s In The Way
The microphones catch every word, the boy may be a test, and thirty children are watching what she does.
WHERE IT TURNS
She reads the approved passage aloud in the approved cadence, word for word. (Grotesque)
He asks his question again, and the class goes still. (Grave)
She gives the approved answer, word perfect, and he keeps looking at her. (Grave)
She sets a spelling exercise on the board, ten words whose first letters spell what happened, and jokes about the class’s handwriting. (Comic and Grave, held at once)
She looks at him, then at the camera in the corner, and dismisses the class as on any Friday. (Grave and Lyric, held at once)

3 changes of tone between beats and 2 beats held two ways. Rehearsal is complete at 50% in a beat, up to 15% more in a beat a change leads into, and 8% more in one held two ways.

WHAT THE APPROVED LESSON ASKS
Everyday Speech, on camera
Timing: Much. She places the approved cadence and the joke at the board for the microphones, and chooses each pause for what it keeps off the recording.
Physical Work: Some. She writes on a small board on her own side of frame, and the exercise is written, never spoken.
Playing Off The Partner: Much. He gives her a look and little else, so every choice she makes comes from reading whether he’s a student or a test.
Vocal Demand: Some
Tone: grotesque to grave to comic to lyric, 3 changes of tone to play. An approved text in an approved voice, the question again, the approved answer, a lesson hidden in a spelling test, then an ordinary dismissal.
