A practice brief for Eco-Horror: an original scene written for SLATE, taped to a reader off camera.
The Bloom
The Warm Walls

A mold specialist sent to inspect a damp house has to get its owner out tonight, though he has started to feel at home with what’s growing in the walls.

THE SCENE
Where And When
The front room of an old house at the edge of a wetland drained for a housing development, on a humid night in August. The specialist came because the neighbors complained of the smell. The mold has spread across three rooms since June, the walls are warm to the touch, and the owner hasn’t left the house in five weeks.
The Two Of You
Strangers. He didn’t call her, and he’s glad of the company anyway.
You Play
Lucia Marchetti, forty, a mold specialist, in a respirator and gloves with a sample kit.
The Reader Plays
Arthur Penhale, sixty-eight, the owner, in his armchair, calm and a little changed.
How They Play It
Unpredictable: playing opposite it, the tape counts responsiveness and consistency for more and leans on your adaptability and composure.
The Genre Asks
States At The Limit: Fear, grief or exhaustion at their extreme, sustained, and believed though the threat is often not in the room. A self-tape in it is judged more on believability and execution, and calls on your emotional depth and physical expression. The lines, then rehearsal on your feet: an extreme state has to be found in the body, on lines you no longer think about.
Caught Live: Behavior that looks unrehearsed: real listening, the next line taken from what the other person just did. A self-tape in it is judged more on connection and responsiveness, and calls on your listening and responsiveness and adaptability. Rehearsing on your feet: what looks unrehearsed is found by playing it through until nothing is set but the wants.
One production kept its cast from knowing when the creature would attack, for real reactions. This comes from the genre’s research, mostly held with low confidence.
What You Want
Get him out of that house before morning.
What’s In The Way
He doesn’t want to leave, he says the walls are warm and that they listen, and the longer she stays in the house the less she wants to leave either.
WHERE IT TURNS
She explains the readings in her inspector’s voice through the respirator, and he offers her tea. (Grave and Comic, held at once)
He tells her the walls hum at night, contentedly, and asks her to listen. (Grotesque)
She hears it, and notices she has taken off one glove without deciding to. (Grotesque and Grave, held at once)
She takes off the respirator to talk to him plainly, face to face, and asks him to walk out with her. (Grave and Lyric, held at once)

3 changes of tone between beats and 3 beats held two ways. Rehearsal is complete at 50% in a beat, up to 15% more in a beat a change leads into, and 8% more in one held two ways.

WHAT THE WARM WALLS ASKS
Everyday Speech, on camera
Timing: Some
Physical Work: Some. She works in a prop respirator she can speak through and gloves, on her own side of frame, and taking them off is the scene’s turn.
Playing Off The Partner: Much. He’s calm one moment and strange the next, and she has to find, in what he says, whatever is still himself.
Vocal Demand: Some
Tone: grave to comic to grotesque to lyric, 3 changes of tone to play. Procedure and a polite offer, a strange invitation, the moment she feels it too, then her face shown as she asks him to leave with her.

How This Works

Warm To The Touch. The house is working on her as it worked on him, and the horror is how reasonable staying begins to sound. It rests on: The longer she stays the less she wants to leave, and she takes off a glove without deciding to. Fully prepared, it would come to 56.1 for you now.
