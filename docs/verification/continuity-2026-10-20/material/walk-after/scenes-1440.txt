## nonfiler.json, School, Coursework, at 1440: The Impostures of Scapin

The Impostures of Scapin, Molière, tr. Charles Heron Wall (1671), Act Three, Scene Two, the sack scene, in a street in Naples: you play Scapin. Géronte has paid Scapin five hundred crowns to ransom his son from a Turkish galley, and he told his son that Scapin had betrayed him, which put Scapin on his knees under Léandre’s sword confessing his own tricks. Now Scapin has his revenge. He tells the old man that the brother of Octave’s bride and his friends, men of the sword, hold every street to his house, and that the one way home is in a sack on Scapin’s shoulders. Géronte gets in. Scapin plays a Gascon bully who beats the sack for its master’s sake, then a foreigner who beats it when Scapin won’t show what’s inside, then half a dozen soldiers at once, crying out each time as if the blows fell on him and pushing the old man’s head back in whenever it comes out to complain. As the soldiers are about to strike, Géronte looks out quietly, sees the trick, and Scapin runs.

WHAT THE IMPOSTURES OF SCAPIN ASKS
Everyday Speech: Quick, plain prose: the period is in the situation, and what the voices ask is their accents.
Timing: Built On It. Scapin’s asides to the sack in his own voice between a bully’s lines, keeping the old man still and hidden, and each blow cried out as it falls.
Physical Work: Built On It. The sack got into, the stick laid on it again and again, the old man’s head pushed back in twice, and the run at the end.
Playing Off The Partner: Much. Line by line while Géronte is talked into the sack and each time he looks out of it; between, Scapin plays both sides of each encounter to a listener who can’t see him.
Vocal Demand: Built On It. Voices of his own besides his natural tone: a Gascon bully, a foreigner, half a dozen soldiers at once, and his cries as if beaten.
What’s Kept Hidden: Built On It. Géronte hides in a sack, and Scapin plays every enemy who beats it, each in another voice.
A Conflict Out Loud: Some. The bullies threaten and strike out loud, but they’re Scapin’s voices: he never puts his own quarrel, his revenge for the lie, to Géronte’s face before he runs.
Tone: comic, 5 changes of tone to play. Farce throughout: a miser beaten in a sack by the servant who tricked him.
Techniques It Asks: Comedy, Accents And Dialects. The bullies’ accents are written into the lines, “Vat! I shall not hab de pleasure to kill dis Géronte,” and the beating is played for its laughs.
