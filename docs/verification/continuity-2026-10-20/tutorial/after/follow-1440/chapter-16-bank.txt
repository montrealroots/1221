Your money is at a real bank in the country you live in, in accounts with that bank’s own names and with numbers written the way that country writes them. To open the bank, press the button with its name on the Money line in Your Affairs on Home, or choose Money in the menu. Its tabs are Accounts, Cards, Payments And Cash, Borrowing, and Statements. With two accounts or more, Move Money, on Accounts, moves money between them: choose From, To and the Amount, and press Move It. Between two accounts at the same bank, it moves at once. To another bank, Move Money takes the sending bank’s time, or its country’s where the bank doesn’t set one, unless the country’s banks pay instantly. A message says the day it arrives, and until then On Its Way, on Accounts, lists it. That money is pending: it has left one account and isn’t in the other yet, so you can’t spend it. An account that earns interest builds it each day, and the bank pays it at the end of the month, or in the months it pays interest. A checking account with a monthly fee takes the fee at the month’s end, unless that month met one of the bank’s conditions for waiving it, such as enough paid in, enough kept in the account, enough card purchases, or your age. Each account’s panel, on Accounts, says whether this month’s fee is waived so far. Your credit card, on Cards, is the bank’s own, with its own name and terms. Its statement comes on the 20th, or the day you set under Statement Day. Pay each statement in full and you’re charged no interest on it, unless the one before wasn’t paid in full. Autopay pays the whole statement, or only the minimum, from the checking account it’s set on: its button says which, and pressing it switches. Pay Card In Full pays the card off now; when you can’t cover all of it, the button says Pay What You Can and pays what you have. If there isn’t enough to cover autopay, the payment comes back unpaid, and unless you’ve paid the minimum yourself, it counts as missed. Paying less than the minimum is late and costs a late fee. Miss two payments in a row and the card is suspended; miss six and it’s charged off to collections. If your checking accounts and savings together can’t clear the card, press Ask For Help With Payments, on Cards: the bank freezes the interest, sets a payment that clears the balance in four years, and suspends the card until the first statement or due date after it’s paid off. To apply for a card at any bank in the country that issues one, yours or another, choose the Bank under Credit Card, on Cards (Another Card once you hold one), and press Apply For This Card. The bank looks at your credit score and what you have to repay it from. If it says yes, the card comes in about a week. You can hold one card from each bank. The day’s spending goes on your everyday card, and Make It The Everyday Card moves it to another. Statements, on their own tab, show every entry on each account you’ve held, month by month, with the balance after each. The Year adds up the interest, the tax taken off it and every fee. Alerts tell you, in What Needs You, when a checking account ends a day below an amount you set, when a payment is over an amount you set, when a statement is ready, and, unless you turn it off, when an account can’t cover a payment due in the next few days. How You Pay, on Payments And Cash, sets how the day’s spending is paid: by debit card, by credit card or in cash. If that way can’t cover a purchase, it isn’t made, and you owe nothing for it. Under Cash, on the same tab, Take It Out takes money out at a machine or the counter, for the bank’s fee, and Pay It In pays cash back in. Under Your Cards, on Cards, Lock It stops anything new going on a card, and Unlock It undoes that. To stop a card lost, stolen or damaged, choose which and press Report It: it stops working at once, and a new card with a new number follows. If a charge isn’t yours, press Report It As Not Mine beside it in time: the law decides how much of it you pay and how soon the rest comes back. If your bank offers one, Overdraft, on Borrowing, lets your account go below zero, with interest and any fee. Personal Loan, on Borrowing, lends you money repaid in equal monthly payments. To bank somewhere else where you live, choose the Bank under Open An Account At Another Bank, on Accounts, and press Apply. Where the country has a switching service, Switch Bank moves everything there for you. Read “A Whole Life” next: your own page, birthdays and the end. The Details Interest is paid on the last day of the month, or in the months the bank pays it: at each quarter’s end in Germany, in February and August in Japan, and in December on a Livret A. Where the country has banks take tax off interest first, yours does. Where a bank publishes no rate, the rate shown is estimated, and the screen says so. Deutsche Bank, Postbank and Berliner Sparkasse take a checking account’s monthly fee at the quarter’s closing, and ING on the 1st of the month after. A card’s terms are its rate, or the range of rates it sets by credit score; any yearly fee; the minimum payment; the late fee; and the days to the due date, all within the law’s caps. Autopay also takes from easy-access savings at the same bank as its account, if you’ve linked them. If they can’t cover all of it, the card charges a returned-payment fee for the miss, except on a US card, where the late fee covers the same miss. If a statement isn’t paid in full, the interest built up until the day you clear it comes on the next statement. Outside the US, paying less than the minimum also goes on your credit record and lowers your limit; in the US that happens only if the payment is still short at the next due date, thirty days later. Some banks, many in Europe among them, issue a card that takes the whole statement every month and can’t carry a balance. Where a bank’s card terms couldn’t be found, they’re estimated, and the screen says so. Each card has its own statement, due date and minimum, and your credit record looks at how much of all their limits you’ve used together. Move the statement day and the due date moves with it. You can ask for a higher or lower limit, and say whether the bank may raise it without asking. In the US you can opt in to going over the limit, or not. Close a card and nothing new goes on it while what you owe is paid down. Once a statement isn’t paid in full, interest builds each day on what the card owes; paying a statement in full keeps new spending interest-free. Your credit score sets the rate when the card opens, and it stays there. In the US two payments missed in a row end any introductory rate, and where the card has a penalty rate, it applies after 45 days’ notice until six payments in a row are on time. Pay more than you owe and the card holds the credit; Send The Credit To My Account moves it back to you. In Germany, whose banks close an account each quarter, statements run quarter by quarter. A card’s statement says what it asked for and what paying only the minimum would take. Turned on, Savings Covers The Checking Account pays what a checking account can’t from easy-access savings at the same bank, for anything that account pays: the day’s spending from your primary account, a card’s autopay from the account it’s set on, and a loan’s payment from its repayment account. A standing order moves money into savings on a set day each month, and you can set a payment for a date. You can take cash from your bank’s ATM, another bank’s, the counter, or in Britain the Post Office where your bank allows it, each for the bank’s own fee, and from the machines no more in a day than the bank allows; another machine’s owner may add a fee of its own. A cash advance on the card costs its fee and charges its rate from the day you take it, with no grace period, and a payment to the card goes to the more expensive balance first (in the US, only what you pay above the minimum does). You can pay for the day’s groceries in cash. At a German bank that sets a limit, a savings account lets out €2,000 a month without notice, whether as cash, as a move or as a payment, and no more. After you report a card, the new one arrives in the bank’s usual time, for its fee, and a card about to expire has its replacement sent ahead. Someone else may use your card, as often as the country’s official figures say it happens: the bank stops some of it and asks you, and the rest shows up in an alert or on your statement. Where the bank offers it, Move My Account To A New Number gives your checking account a new number and a new debit card. Some cards start with an introductory rate for some months, pay rewards on your spending, lend money into your account as a money transfer, or let you put part of what you owe on purchases into a plan paid off in equal monthly amounts, at the plan’s own fee or rate instead of the card’s interest; at Chase, a plan made during the card’s introductory rate costs nothing. A card you apply for at another bank can take over the balance of one you already hold, for its fee and at its transfer rate, and the old card closes once the balance has moved. A term deposit locks money away at a fixed rate for its term. At the end, the bank does what it does by default unless you choose another option it allows, in time for any notice it needs: renew it for the same term, with any grace days to take it all out; move it into easy-access savings; or pay it out. The bank writes to you before the end. Where the bank allows it, Break It Early costs the bank’s penalty, which you see before you press it, and it comes out of the deposit itself if the interest won’t cover it; some banks need notice first. Some banks let you take the interest during the term, or charge for savings withdrawals past a number of free ones a month. 20 banks move money into savings at the same bank as you spend, each in its own way: rounding up to the next unit you choose, a set amount per purchase, or, at KakaoBank, the leftover of the day’s balance, on the bank’s own day and never into an overdraft. Where the bank leaves a choice to you, such as the unit or the amount, you make it before round-ups start. An account you leave unused goes inactive and then dormant, blocked until you press Reactivate, and after the country’s set number of years its money goes to the state, which you can claim back. Where the country has a cooling-off period, you can cancel a new account or card other than your primary account within it: in Korea only a card, in Canada anything but a card, in Britain anything but a fixed-rate deposit, and in Mexico only one you haven’t used. You can open a savings account, or close it and move its money back. Where Germany’s monthly limit applies and the account holds more than a month lets out, closing it means giving three months’ notice, and it closes then. Past the bank’s free withdrawals in a month, each one out of savings costs its fee, including a payment savings makes for you. An account you kept in the country you lived in before Yale stays at its bank, shown in its own money with the dollar amount beside it. The bank won’t open anything new for you while you’re abroad, it charges its fee for customers abroad and its card’s fee on purchases in dollars, and where the bank closes such accounts, it closes on the day it says. Money moves in or out only as a payment abroad, less its fees and the exchange. If you close it with Close It, a German bank charges this month’s fee only for the days it’s been open, and shows the figure under the button before you press it. Its country takes its tax from the interest. On an F-1 the US doesn’t tax it; a US citizen reports it, with the foreign tax credited, and one whose accounts abroad passed $10,000 is told about the FBAR, and about Form 8938 past its own thresholds, with the tax return. An overdraft is either arranged up to a limit, with interest and any fee, or paid item by item with a fee for each; its interest is charged on the 1st, or when the account closes. A US bank won’t pay an everyday debit card purchase into an overdraft unless you’ve opted in. An account left below zero, past any arranged limit, for sixty days is closed (in France, two months after the bank gives notice). The bank charges off what it can’t recover, with the interest and fees owed to that day; the debt goes to collections and onto your credit record; any standing order from or into the account ends; and your money moves to another bank. In the US the bank reports a closing like that to ChexSystems for five years, and the next bank may turn you away (most do while the debt is unpaid, about half once it’s paid), which leaves a second-chance account with a monthly fee and no overdraft. Pay It, under In Collections, pays the debt down. A French bank writes to you after a month past your overdraft, and after three offers a loan to cover it: Take The Loan accepts it, and once the account is back within its limit, the sixty days start again. If the account can’t cover a personal loan payment in full, the bank takes what’s there, the payment goes on your credit record as missed, with the bank’s fee where it charges one, and the rest stays owed. When you switch to another bank, your card stays with the bank that issued it, as loans and term deposits do. The bank’s name, each account’s name and every line on a statement are links: the bank opens onto the accounts it holds for you and the latest money through them, and a statement line onto the accounts it moved between and the day it went through.
