## 2. Playable Material For Every Genre (Requirement 2)

- **What was written:** `src/engine/content/briefs/`, with 149 briefs, one written for each genre, and 302 readings.
  Each is an original scene written for SLATE, shown as SLATE's own, and names no real work, person, brand or case.
- **Each brief sets out:**
  - circumstances;
  - the relationship;
  - who you play and who the reader plays;
  - objective;
  - obstacle;
  - two to four beats with their tones;
  - medium (144 on camera, 5 at a microphone);
  - the preparation it needs;
  - its demands, with the text's reason for each.
- **Its readings:** two or three, every one supported by the brief.
- **Before committing time:** the player sees all of it, including what each dimension weighs against their levels
  and what each reading would come to fully prepared.
- **Research:** `docs/research/attributes/research-self-tape-briefs.json` covers Stanislavski, Hagen, Spotlight's
  self-tape guidance, a casting director's on adjustments, and the gaps. The acting principles are kept apart from
  SLATE's sizes.
- **Review:** an independent read of all 149 found these, each fixed:
  - names and premises echoing real works, and one real 2021 water-plant intrusion;
  - three stereotype risks;
  - near-duplicate premises across sibling genres;
  - unsupported readings;
  - factual slips;
  - about twenty scenes staging contact with a reader who stands off camera.

  Every brief now says how it's taped alone.

## 3. Choices Connected To Performance (Requirement 3)

- **Where a choice lands:** a reading and a way of playing it reach preparation (the breakdown, the choice's
  rehearsal), the evaluation (leans and abilities, normalized), the feedback (what held it back) and the history (each
  take's reading, playing, purpose and result). Code: `systems/briefs.ts` (`briefContext`, `chooseBrief`, `tapeBrief`)
  and `ui/components/BriefPane.tsx`.
- **No hidden right answer:** a reading never misfits. Only the material's own `allows`, said with its reason, and a
  callback's note given before the take can hold a choice back.
- **Weakness and outcome:** training the input that costs a brief most raises the take, by +2.0 for +20, n = 149.
  An input the brief doesn't read changes nothing, exactly 0 for all 149. `tests/briefs.test.ts` checks this for every
  brief and every ability.
- **No double counting:**
  - knowing the genre is the better of studied and worked in, never the sum, and doesn't also speed preparation;
  - a brief's genres share one session of practice;
  - an applied demand joins once;
  - a way of playing it adds rehearsal, not quality.

## 4. Mixed Genres And Tonal Transitions (Requirement 4)

- **Held and changed:** a beat with two tones held at once is a blend, and asks emotional depth and imagination. A
  change of tones between beats is a transition, and asks adaptability and composure. Each asks more rehearsal before
  it counts complete.
- **Counts:** 144 briefs change tone between beats, and 133 hold two tones at once in some beat.
- **Normalized and deduplicated:**
  - a brief's genres reordered and repeated moved 0 of 149 takes;
  - a repeated tag is practice once;
  - a genre's applied demand is read once;
  - leans move weight and add none.
- **Tests:** order, duplicate tags, overlapping families and contrasting demands are in `tests/briefs.test.ts` and
  `tools/brief-balance.ts` §6.

## 5. Progression (Requirement 5)

- **The contract.** Full relevant abilities, mastered techniques, complete preparation, a sufficient day and a
  fitting choice come to exactly 100.
  - Checked over every brief × reading × fitting way of playing it, more than 900 cases, and over a callback answered.
  - Played to it through the commands in two genres (`docs/verification/briefs/mastery-*.json`).
  - Perfect work stays distinct from casting: a take is read by nothing that casts.
- **Weaknesses addressable:** every ability and technique a brief reads has an exercise or a workout. A brief's
  "What To Work On" names it, with the practice control beside it.
- **Recommendations follow recent limits and move on when a limit improves** (`recommendations`). In the 120-day
  journeys the top recommendation changed two to four times, and was never made for an input at full.
- **Specialist against generalist, the same budget** (`docs/verification/BRIEF-BALANCE.md` §1, §1b):
  - across the School's kinds of work, each specialist is ahead somewhere and behind elsewhere;
  - neither the specialist nor the generalist is ahead everywhere;
  - on camera, the body specialist's deficit falls from −9.0 to −3.2 as a brief is built more on the body and voice.
- **Kept:** the talent and school-difficulty decisions are untouched. No decay or hidden penalty was added.

## 6. Balance Evidence Across The Catalog (Requirement 6)

- **The static analysis:** `tools/brief-balance.ts` covers all 149 briefs, every reading and every fitting way of
  playing it, for eight actors, with n, mean, standard deviation and range for each measure.
  - It covers preparation, readings, transfer, a weakness trained and tags.
  - Two specialists' best readings differ on 42 to 133 of the 149 briefs.
- **The played comparison:** `tools/brief-strategies.ts` spends 24 hours five ways on seeds `bs-0` to `bs-2` and the
  held-out `bs-10` to `bs-12`, n = 3 a cell. They agree.

| Way | Mean Take (matched / held-out) | Genre Practice Per Take | Abilities Gained |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepare | 46.6 / 46.6 | 10 | −0.9 |
| Train between | 47.2 / 46.3 | 10 | +1.6 |
| Tape only | 21.1 / 21.0 | 2.2 | −1.4 |
| Breadth | 21.1 / 21.0 | 10 | −1.0 |
| Repeat one | 58.3 / 58.1 | 2.8 | −1.4 |

- **No strategy dominates without a tradeoff.** Repeat-one's takes are best on its one brief, because its own takes
  build that genre's knowledge. On the 20 held-out briefs every way is within 0.3. Breadth earns the most genre
  practice, at takes of 21.
- **Exploits checked:**
  - repetition: the same take is worth half as practice, on any day;
  - tags: reordered or repeated change nothing;
  - reloads: the same state tapes to the same result;
  - duplicated rewards: an action sent twice is lived once.
- **What each balance change was for** is in `docs/ENGINE-MODEL.md` §8. None was tuned to make a chosen strategy win;
  each answered a measured problem with the gameplay it was meant to serve.

## 7. Variety And Continuity (Requirement 7)

- **What's remembered.** Each brief's record keeps:
  - material met;
  - readings tried;
  - preparation;
  - each take's playing, result, what held it back and its purpose;
  - a callback's note.
- **What reads it:**
  - the suggestions: a callback waiting, the brief leaning most on a named weakness, a reading not yet tried;
  - the take's purpose;
  - the screen.
- **Purposeful repetition.** A take is first, a callback, another reading, another way of playing it, improved, or
  the same again (worth half as practice, not forbidden). A Strong take earns one callback a round.
- **The journeys** (`tools/brief-journeys.ts`, 120 days of term, beginner, developing, specialist and mastered, on
  seeds `bj-0` and `bj-10`, in `docs/verification/briefs/`):
  - no take was the same again in any run;
  - takes were first takes, other readings and callbacks;
  - quality rose through the term, 52.7 to 56.1 for the beginner and 53.9 to 57.3 for the developing student;
  - the specialist's readings stayed apart (1.2 to 1.3 points);
  - at mastery, callbacks and preparation are what remain;
  - 0 breaches and 0 unbalanced entries.
- **Content limits, honestly:** 149 briefs and 302 readings. Nothing claims more.

## 9. Tests, Mutation And Defects (Requirement 9)

**New tests:**
- `tests/briefs-content.test.ts`: every brief whole and honest, every genre with its brief;
- `tests/briefs.test.ts`: 25 tests covering the guarantee, reads, tones, tags, played commands, the record, and each
  bug found;
- updates to the engagement, genre-coverage, history, looks and attributes-complete tests for the brief route, their
  assertions kept.

**Mutation:** twenty mutations of the new rules, each run alone. All twenty were killed. Four first survived, each a
weak or missing test, and each now has one.

**Defects found and fixed,** each reproduced, its cause found, and given a test that fails without the fix:

1. **Material decided little:** a level-3 feature hardly decided who showed best. The feature effects were doubled.
2. **Readings were near-inconsequential.** Their strength was raised.
3. **Knowing a genre was a grind:** about 16 hours of study per genre. It became the better of studied and worked in,
   complete at 30.
4. **Preparation was too heavy:** about 14 hours for a first brief. The steps were lightened.
5. **Callbacks looped:** 84 takes of one brief. Now one callback a round.
6. **A late choice could never be rehearsed in:** the step was refused once rehearsal was complete. This was in
   coursework too, and both are fixed.
7. **Unprepared repetition was rewarded:** it earned more practice than preparing. The same again is now worth half.
8. **An injury never reached a taped brief built on the body.** It now does, on execution and consistency.
9. **The reading toast was wrong:** it said the breakdown was halved on a first choice. It now reports the command's
   own result.
10. **The acceptance tool:** a plan step and a heading match corrected.

## 11. Limitations

- **The content is finite:** one brief written for each genre, 149, with 302 readings.
- **On camera a taped brief favors the feeling abilities.** The body's and the voice's specialists are rewarded by the
  School's movement, voice and clown work, and by briefs built on them. They still trail an even actor on every taped
  brief.
- **The sizes are SLATE's design,** each said where it lives: the pitch, the steps, the completes, the reading strength,
  the callback's lift, the same-again worth, the doubled features. The research supports shapes and directions, not
  sizes.
- **No bug-free claim.** These checks passing doesn't prove there are none.
