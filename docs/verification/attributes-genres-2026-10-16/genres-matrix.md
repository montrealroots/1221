## What A Genre Is In The Engine

The code has 149 genres, each with its families, conventions and researched demands. Each has exactly two briefs written for it (298 in all), and no brief is listed under a second genre. A genre reaches play in four ways:

1. **Its briefs.** They are its scenes, and everything a take of the genre asks comes from them: features, language, beats, staged techniques, the reader's way of playing, stakes, size and readings (`systems/briefs.ts:307-339`).
2. **Knowing the genre.** This is a preparation part of interpretation on every taped brief. Its value is the better of familiarity and practice, over 100, and it is complete at 0.3. The rule is the same for all 149 genres (`briefs.ts:94, 287`).
3. **Speed.** A class scene from a play of the kind, and a part's lines in one, are prepared up to a sixth faster at 100. The rule is the same for all 149 (`engagement.ts:101-105`).
4. **Families.** Their leans are read only for a night or a casting read of a play with no material profile (`school.ts:2265, 2512`). Every play in the catalog has one, so only a festival's or a cabaret's new play reaches this.

The researched demands are read by no evaluation: 51 genres are applied, 41 of them with 52 tags. They act because `tests/briefs-content.test.ts` holds every brief of the genre to stage them.
So apart from its two briefs, every genre is mechanically the same as every other.

## Distinctness, Measured

A brief's profile has 18 numbers. Six are its dimension shares (×10). Twelve are each ability's effect when the even actor (every ability 50) gains +10 on it. A genre's profile is the mean of its two briefs' profiles. Distance is L1 between profiles.

- All 11026 genre pairs: 5th percentile 1.664, median 3.448, 95th 6.749.
- Between a genre's own two briefs: mean 3.25, 0.76 to 7.27.
- A genre's nearest other genre: mean 1.17, 0.72 to 2.29.
- **143 of 149 genres have another genre nearer to them than their own two briefs are to each other.** What a take asks is decided by the brief, and the genre label predicts it no better than a neighbour's would.
- Seven pairs of briefs from different genres are mechanically identical apart from their readings. They match on medium, size, stakes, the reader, all five features, language, staged techniques and beat tones:
  - biker = coming_of_age
  - childrens = science_fantasy
  - christmas = holiday
  - dystopian = medical_drama
  - espionage = neorealism
  - family = steampunk
  - nordic_noir = social_realism
- Which equal-budget actor comes out best on a genre's briefs (13 actors: the even one and a specialist in each ability; baseline §2): emotionalDepth 139, physical 3, vocal 3, listening 3, adaptability 1. One ability wins 139 of 149 genres.
- How far a brief's best and worst readings are apart:
  - the even actor: mean 0.18, over a point on 1 of 298;
  - the emotional depth specialist: 1.57, over a point on 204;
  - the listening specialist: 0.6, over a point on 63;
  - the physical specialist: 0.49, over a point on 54.
  - The best reading differs between the feeling and the listening specialist on 146 of 298 briefs.

## Flags

1. **Genre mechanics are label-only beyond the briefs.**
   - Knowing the genre, its speed, its practice credit and its saturation at 0.3 are one rule for all 149 genres.
   - No genre asks anything of a take that its two briefs don't, and no genre has a mastery effect past 30 known (`briefs.ts:94, 287`; baseline §5).
2. **Genres are not distinct as genres.**
   - 143 of 149 have a nearer neighbour than their own second brief.
   - The closest pairs, each nearer than its own briefs: drama ~ social_realism 0.72; exploitation ~ splatter 0.77; christmas ~ crime 0.77; gangster ~ drama 0.81; gothic_horror ~ historical_fiction 0.84; black_comedy ~ legal_drama 0.87; biker ~ drama 0.89; jidaigeki ~ western 0.90; historical_drama ~ psychological_thriller 0.91; folk_horror ~ prison_film 0.92; yakuza ~ gangster 0.93; fantasy ~ legal_drama 0.93; alternate_history ~ fairy_tale 0.94.
3. **Seven cross-genre brief pairs are identical in every number the engine reads, readings aside.** They differ in text only (list above).
4. **One kind of actor wins almost every genre.** Emotional depth is best on 139 of 149, because every taped brief's heaviest inputs are believability (emotional depth 3) or execution (`tasks.ts:168, 171`). The material changes the margin, not the winner.
5. **The genre gate dominates early feedback.**
   - A mastered actor with the genre unknown tapes 77.1 on average. At 0.15 known it is 88.6, and at 0.3 it is 100 (baseline §5).
   - In the 120-day journeys, "Knowing the genre wasn't complete, which cost the interpretation 26 points" is the chief limit of many first takes (`played-journey-*.json`).
   - It takes four or five hours to answer, after which it is read by nothing.
6. **Families are near-label-only.** `familyLean` (`tasks.ts:261-284`) reaches only a play with no profile. No brief, scene or catalogued play reads it.
7. **Shallow technique content per genre.**
   - 115 of 298 briefs stage a technique beyond the medium's.
   - A +10 on any staged technique moves a take by at most about 0.1 (baseline §1b: comedy, combat, voiceover).
   - Two briefs a genre is the whole variety. Repeating a genre means repeating one of two scenes.
8. **Briefs concentrate on two dimensions.** Execution is the heaviest of 156 and believability of 140. Responsiveness leads 2, and connection, consistency and interpretation never lead.
9. **Reader behaviour varies on 95 of 263 camera briefs** (pressing 34, unpredictable 32, withholding 29). The rest are steady, and a voice brief has no reader.
10. **Readings matter only to an uneven actor.**
    - For the even actor, best and worst are 0.18 apart on average.
    - That is by design (a reading moves weight), but it means a reading changes the outcome only as far as the actor is lopsided.

## Tests That Demonstrate Differences

- `tests/briefs-situations.test.ts:40`: a genre's two briefs meet four actors differently, by at least the floor.
- `tests/briefs.test.ts:109`: readings change what the work leans on.
- `tests/briefs.test.ts:160`: tags in any order change nothing.
- `tests/briefs-content.test.ts`: every applied demand is staged.
- `tests/material.test.ts:156`: two comedies ask different things (scenes, not briefs).
- `tests/engagement.test.ts:55, 71, 99, 126`.
- No test compares one genre against another genre.
