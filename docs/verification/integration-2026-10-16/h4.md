## What Holds A Specialist Back (`h4/landscape-probe.ts.txt`)

On the pieces where its ability reads most, each specialist's difference from the even actor was taken apart: the gain
from its one ability at 85 alone, and the loss from the other eleven at 46.8 alone.

1. **A little of everything.** Since phase 2b every piece reads "a little of every ability its dimensions concern"
   before its own demands (`BASE_INPUTS`, `content/tasks.ts`), and the techniques at 50 take their share of every
   dimension. On its best work an ability carries 12% to 17% of what the piece's abilities move, so +35 on it gains
   about 2.7 points and −3.2 on eleven others loses about 2.45. A coin toss: ahead on about half.
2. **The weakest dimension.** The whole is three quarters the weighted mean and a quarter the weakest dimension. For an
   even performer the weakest is a three-way tie between connection, consistency and responsiveness, the three
   dimensions with no technique term, and the piece weighs it at a median 8.7% (4.6% at the tenth percentile), while it
   takes a quarter of the whole. A specialist's lowered abilities lower that dimension, and its one strength rarely
   reaches it, so a quarter of the whole can only lose. Without the term (measured, not adopted) H4 would be 9 of 12.
3. **Presence, discipline and stamina have no work of their own in the catalog.** Presence reads most in front of a
   house and with no partner, and 298 of the 431 pieces are taped briefs, most with a reader. Discipline reads in
   consistency and a long score, stamina only as a drain on the later segments. Discipline's and stamina's specialists
   are behind on every whole night (0 of 23 to 30) under every configuration measured; presence's is ahead on 5 to 11
   of 30 nights and 15 to 22 of 30 castings where the configurations leave it any, and on 1 of about 100 briefs.

### 1. Length: More Parts By A Scene's Action, Not By Its Length

**What the sources support.** Stanislavski divides a role into bits by its action: in Sharon Marie Carnicke's glossary, "a
new 'bit' begins whenever an action or counteraction shifts in manner of execution or strategy" (Stanislavsky in Focus,
2009, p. 214, as Wikipedia's "Unit of action" cites it, read 2026-10-16; until a check later that day this account and
the code's comment put the words to the glossary of An Actor Prepares), and the house already reads a longer score of such units,
set in rehearsal and kept the same through every change, as discipline's (`DEMAND_EFFECT.beats`, after Sawoski's summary
in research-acting-engine.json). A profile's `turns` are those shifts beyond its changes of tone. So a scene with more
shifts of action is played in more units, and holding them is discipline's: that much has a source, and it's
implemented as `evaluation-10`.

**What they don't.** None of them divides a piece by its length. Stanislavski's bits are found in what the actor is
doing: his example is a walk home from a friend's, two hundred bits counted at first, then four, going home, the
window-shopping that stopped it, undressing and the mind wandering in bed, each "a new, independent Bit" because the
doing changed, whatever time it took. He has the actor go "from the largest to the medium-size, from the medium-size to
the small, from the small to the tiniest Bits so that you can once more combine them and return to the largest", and
in performance play the large ones, "maximal in size and minimal in number" (An Actor's Work, tr. Jean Benedetti,
Routledge 2008, chapter 7, "Bits and Tasks", pp. 139 to 141). So the same scene holds more or fewer bits by how finely
it's worked, and no number of them follows from minutes or lines. The work on sustained attention that does run on time
(the vigilance decrement: detection falling over minutes on a monitoring task) measures attention, which is focus in
the house's terms and not discipline, and nothing found measures a rehearsed performer's precision falling with a
scene's length. So a scene's length (`Scene.size`, which sets how long its lines take to learn) isn't read as parts, and
sustaining precision is read where the length is made of shifts of action, a profile's turns, the large bits a scene
is played through.

**The rule** (`unitSegments`, `demandsOf`, `systems/piece.ts` and `systems/material.ts`; `RULESET` `evaluation-10`; its row in

reads discipline's long score; a segment's place in the piece is read by the running before it (`SegmentSpec.length`), so a
unit falls where it does in its section and a brief's heart, which weighs more in the whole, takes no longer. Placing by
weight was tried first and moved every beat after a brief's heart later: on the designed briefs the even actor's

### 2. The Load: Why It Stays At 1

**Not implemented.** A piece's load (`PIECE.load`, `loadOf`) is the share of the drain a piece applies, 0 to 1: a night's
0.6 and 0.12 a level of the body's and the voice's work it stages, clamped at 1, and at 1 the drain on its last
segment is the whole of `PIECE.drain`, 0.3 of what each tiring dimension would be, at stamina nought. Eight plays reach 1
(their body's and voice's levels come to four or more). Letting a heavier play go past 1 is the same as making the drain
larger for it, and the drain's size is SLATE's estimate with no figure behind it (`content/piece.ts`): nothing found
measures how far a rehearsed actor's work falls across a running time by its length or its physical score. The sport
science of fatigue measures power and pace over exercise, not the quality of acting, and the 2024 Compendium gives what
an hour on stage costs the body (3.0 METs for an actor, 10.3 for musical theater's singing and dancing), not what it costs
the performance. With no account of the size, a heavier load is a weight to be argued, and wasn't added.

What the reading found points the other way. The load reads a night's body and voice and not its length, so the four
one-acts among the eight at 1, The Bear, A Marriage Proposal, Riders To The Sea and Salomé, each a play in one act, are
loaded as whole evenings of two or three hours. A night's running time isn't in the catalog (`content/plays.ts` keeps no length),
so correcting them is reading each play's length from its text and a rule for it: for the owner, recorded here and in the
