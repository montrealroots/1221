## 2. What A Pair Is, As The Engine Reads It

Matching sides alone aren't the same work, so each genre's two briefs were read through the engine's own functions in
the six parts the owner named, by `tools/genre-pairs.ts` (committed `ea2c5fe2`, before any brief changed). Each part is
the same or not by a rule fixed then:

| Part | What the engine reads | The same where |
|---|---|---|
| Circumstances | the medium, the language, the stakes (`briefMaterial`, `BRIEF.base`, `BRIEF.stakes`) | the medium and the language are, the stakes within a level |
| Objectives and obstacles | how the reader plays the other part (`READER_PLAYS`), the changes of tone and the beats held two ways (`turnsOf`) | the reader's behavior is, the turns and blends within one together |
| Technique demands | the task it's judged as (`taskOf`, on its feet at physical 2), the techniques it stages, the five features | the task and the techniques are, the features within 2 in all |
| Preparation | how long its lines take (`stepMinutes`), what rehearsing it trains (`rehearseTrains`), what speeds it (`rehearseSpeed`) | all three are |
| Valid interpretations | each reading's lean and draws (`briefContext`) | two or more readings of one lean first and draw most where one of the other's does |
| Feedback and consequences | the specialist a take rewards most (+35 on one ability, as `tools/genre-signature.ts` builds them) and the dimension a half-prepared developing actor's take names as its chief limit (`chiefLimit`) | both are |

A pair the same in three of the six or more, half of what the engine reads of it, is substantively repetitive. A brief
compared with itself renamed is the same in all six (`tests/remaining-genres.test.ts`, "a genre’s two briefs, read as
the engine reads them > a brief against itself renamed is the same in all six parts: a renamed description is caught,
never passed").

Measured on the 55 before any change: 7 the same in none of the six, 15 in one, 19 in two, 9 in three, 3 in four, 2 in
five. **Fourteen were substantively repetitive**: animated, christmas, conspiracy_thriller, farce, film_noir,
gothic_horror, jukebox_musical, new_wave, psychological_thriller, samurai, silent_film, sword_and_sorcery, tragicomedy
and yakuza. What they shared was nearly always the catalog's template for a reading (consistency by composure, belief by
emotional depth), copied into both briefs to the number, with the same medium, length and reader around it.

## 3. What Changed

Each of the fourteen kept its first brief and got a new second situation, written for SLATE, that asks something else of
the actor within the genre's register (unchanged: `registerOf` is the genre's on both) and its applied demands (staged:
`tests/briefs-content.test.ts`). The retired scene's id isn't reused: the new one is `brief-<genre>-3`, put in the old
one's place in the list so the designed, held-out and fresh samples keep their positions. A save that prepared a retired
brief keeps its record untouched, nothing offers it again, every command on it is refused as for any brief that doesn't
exist, and the new scene starts from nothing; `RETIRED_BRIEFS` (`content/briefs/index.ts`) keeps each one's title and
genres, so the genre's look still lists a take of it (`briefKinds`). The retired scenes' text is in `d7127819`.

Three of the new scenes were corrected after the first measurement, each so that what the engine reads says what its
text does, never to move a figure: farce-3's reader went from unpredictable to pressing (its doors open on set cues, and
the guests press him; it took the structure back to set), new_wave-3's language from heightened to everyday (its
narration is written prose, which the catalog counts as voiceover, as film noir's is), and tragicomedy-3's improvisation
from 1 to 0 (the fence bit is their oldest routine). The suite's first run found two more (`tests/briefs.test.ts`, "a
feature at 3, the material built on it, makes its ability a real share of its dimension", which every reading of the 30
briefs at physical 3 had met): farce-3's ledger reading drew memory into its execution and its believer reading nothing
of the body, so the body fell under a quarter of it; the ledger now reads the stories kept straight as consistency and
the runs between the doors as the body, and the believer the body behind each lie. And jukebox_musical-3's best man, who
can sing a bit and dance less, carries his scene with its songs: physical 2, not 3, its steps still staged. Neither
moved the parts its genre is the same in (`488fe999`); every measurement in §5 is of the tree after them.

Where a new scene's sides differ from its first brief's, it's because that scene asks it: yakuza-3's ceremony is set
where The Last Lesson is open; silent_film-3's take is found to a director's calls, so open; christmas-3's Santa is
answered live, so driven; psychological_thriller-3's word-for-word telling against a pressing detective, driven;
samurai-3's comic haggle, driven and open (and expansive by 0.01, a tie by any reading); sword_and_sorcery-3's oath to a
hall, expansive; tragicomedy-3's one-voice double act, expansive; film_noir-3's pawnshop standoff played off Danny,
open; new_wave-3's walk beside a man who withholds, measured (and expansive by 0.01). Five keep their genre's sides: in
conspiracy_thriller, farce and gothic_horror the register decides at least one axis (with its concerns set aside the
briefs would part on pace or scale), and animated's and jukebox_musical's scenes agree on all three without it, on their
comic beats, their timing and their voice work. They ask other things: §4.
