`tools/brief-journeys.ts -- --trace <file>` (added on 2026-10-16) keeps each take's own record and judges it again
three ways: with no draw, at the actor's levels from the start of the run, and at their levels at its end.

Each cause the brief names, read from the trace:

- **Progression.** Every one of the 17 takes comes out higher at the end's levels than at the start's, by 3.0 to 4.2
  points. The student improved on every piece of work, whatever it was.
- **Material demands.** Six of the last month's 14 takes are callbacks: a harder pitch earned by a Strong take
  (difficulty +0.1 against a first take's −0.2, near a professional's), with a note on how to play it. They average
  60.4; the eight first takes and other readings average 66.8, against 65.5 in the first month. The first month had no
  callback in it, because nothing had earned one yet.
- **Readiness and fatigue.** 71 in the first month and 71 in the last; no take was held back by the day's condition
  (every take's chief limit is an ability).
- **Preparation and deadlines.** On every take of both journeys the breakdown, the lines and rehearsal were complete;
  knowing the genre wasn't, on any take: 12% to 27% of the 90% that completes it, since the policy studies a brief's
  genre at most twice and lets taping build the rest. It was 12% on the first month's takes and 12% to 27% on the last
  month's, so it rose, if anything, and doesn't explain a fall. Briefs carry no deadline.
- **Workload.** The first term's coursework left free hours for two takes in the first month and none in the second.
- **Stamina.** The policy never works out, so fitness fell to 35 by the end, as it does for anyone who doesn't train it.
  It's in the "end's levels" above, which still rose 3 to 4 points.

**So nothing deteriorated.** The last month's mean is lower because it holds the harder work the specialist earned by
doing well, which the first month couldn't. The beginner earned no callback and meets L3. The game says a callback is
harder where it's offered ("It’s pitched harder than a first take", the brief's pane; the guide's How Hard It Is gives
its difficulty), so a lower callback isn't left unexplained.

No change was made. Removing callbacks from the count would discard the hard cases, and easing callbacks would remove a
legitimate consequence of earning one.

## L3 As Written: Not Met

As written, L3 measures what was taped as much as how well, and a student who earns callbacks fails it. It isn't counted
as met.

## The Corrected Criterion, Proposed

L3′, for the owner: **the same work rises.** Every take of the term, judged again at the actor's levels at the end of
the term, comes out at or over the same take at their levels at its start (prepared, played and pitched the same). And
the first month's takes, judged at the last month's levels, are over their first-month results. Measured: the
specialist 17 of 17 (+3.0 to +4.2) and the first month's takes 65.4 → 68.6; the beginner 19 of 19 (+2.2 to +3.6) and
54.6 → 57.7. Every take is in it, callbacks included.
