# The Material's Words

For `docs/GLOSSARY.md`, a section of its own after Core: "In-World Material: Briefs, Readings And Class Scenes". Written
2026-10-17 by brief 14's material area, from the briefs and scenes as they now read and the screens that show them
(BriefPane, PlayingChooser's MaterialDemands, CourseworkPane's table work). Terms other areas share are listed at the
end for the coordinator, not written into Core.

## In-World Material: Briefs, Readings And Class Scenes

| Say | What It Means | Instead Of |
|---|---|---|
| **practice brief**, **brief** | An original scene written for SLATE for a self-tape in a genre, set out as a casting breakdown (`BRIEFS`, `content/briefs/`). Every genre has one or two. | excerpt, sides, "adapted from", "based on the film" (tests/briefs-content.test.ts fails a brief that says so) |
| **an original scene written for SLATE** | The credit line of every original class scene and every brief (`BY`, `scenes-original.ts`), word for word. | |
| **Where And When**, **The Two Of You**, **You Play**, **Reader Plays**, **What You Want**, **What's In The Way** | The brief's breakdown, as the pane heads it: its circumstances, the relationship, your part, the reader's, the objective and the obstacle. Written in the scene's own terms, never the game's. | |
| **the beats** | The scene's turns in order, each with its tones, which the rehearsal sentence under them counts. | segments, units |
| **the reader** | Whoever reads the other part off camera for a self-tape, or the voice on the track for a recording. The briefs' staging notes say where the reader is (off camera, at the eyeline beside the lens) and what the reader calls. | partner, for a self-tape's reader (Playing Off The Partner stays the feature's label) |
| **take** | One recording of a self-tape. The industry's word, kept in the briefs (a self-tape is the one place Core allows it), and in-world where a scene is about film (a single unbroken take, forty feet of hand-cranked film). The engine-speak ratchet counts each one; the status says why it's kept. | the take, for a class piece or a night on stage |
| **a reading**, **Your Reading** | One way of understanding what the brief's scene is about, said in a sentence ("She plays it as ..."), with what in the brief it rests on. | an interpretation id, a lean, what it draws on |
| **It rests on** | The line of the brief a reading stands on, said as a sentence of the scene. | evidence, support |
| **It Only Takes: <Set, Open, Measured, Driven, Restrained, Expansive>** | The one way the scene can be played where its text forces it (`allows`), with its reason after it. The reason says so in the same words: "It can only be played set, the same way every time." | "the structure is set", "can’t be found anew on the take", "the structure has to stay open" |
| **played set** / **played open**, **measured** / **driven**, **restrained** / **expansive** | Core's "how you play it", said in lower case inside a sentence. | a side, an approach, a structure |
| **Techniques It Asks** and the techniques' names | What a brief asks of the craft, named as the School screen names the skills: Stage Combat (stage combat), Improvisation, Voice-Over (voice-over work), On Camera (on-camera work), Classical Text (classical text work), Clown And Mask (clowning), Comedy (comic timing), Character Work, Intimacy (staged intimacy), Singing, Dance, Accents And Dialects, Speech. | combat, improv, improv work, voiceover, voice-led work, screen work, "Classical for", "clown for", a bracketed "(stage combat)" |
| **staged intimacy**, **intimacy coordinator**, **staged fight**, **marked solo**, **at reduced speed**, **called cues**, **replica**, **eyeline**, **off camera**, **on her side of the frame** | How a brief's contact, intimacy or fight is staged safely for a solo self-tape. Industry words, kept, and said in every brief that asks for them. | |
| **class scene** | A scene from a play, public domain to 1930, or an original scene written for SLATE, set for a course's table work (`SCENES`). | excerpt |
| **table work** and its questions: **Given Circumstances**, **The Relationship**, **Your Objective**, **The Obstacle**, **A Choice To Play** | Answering what the scene's text says before it's played, one question at a time (`QUESTIONS`). | |
| **What it rests on** / **What cuts against it** | The text's reason an answer holds, or doesn't, shown at the table once the scene is shown. A reason that cuts against is the teacher's note word for word: "<question>: <reason> As <role> in <play>." Each is a whole sentence of the play's facts. | "Nothing of the kind", a fragment, "she isn’t in this part" |

## For The Coordinator

- **take** is Core's exception ("except a self-tape or an audition"), and 74 of the area's 120 engine-speak hits are it,
  every one a self-tape's take or a film scene's. The ratchet can't tell them from a take for any judged work, so the
  area's rewrites had to say "every time" in new sentences where "every take" was the right word. Core might say the
  ratchet's "a take" pattern leaves the briefs out, or count them apart.
- **reader** is shared with school: the feature label Playing Off The Partner (school's, in PlayingChooser) heads a note
  that, in the briefs, is always about the reader. Kept, since the label is school's.
- **reading** is a brief's interpretation, and Core's "how you play it" is the axes. The two are different choices on
  one pane (Your Reading, and the way of playing it); Core might say so, so no area uses one for the other.
