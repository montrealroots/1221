Do not equate finishing classes with automatic conferral or automatic work authorization.

PART 3 — MATERIALS AND REPRESENTATION

7. Professional Materials

A role or representative requires materials
→ the player reviews what is missing or outdated
→ prepares or updates appropriate materials
→ spends applicable time and resources
→ the engine evaluates relevant quality
→ materials become available for submission
→ submissions reference a specific version.

New work or training:
→ new legitimate credits or samples become available
→ the player updates materials
→ future submissions use updated versions
→ earlier submissions and historical records remain intact.

Do not invent credits or make every update automatically improve every audition.

8. Approaching An Individual Agent

Player finds a suitable individual representative
→ reviews verified agency affiliation, relevant professional scope, and the game’s approach route
→ checks prerequisites
→ submits materials or receives a referral/invitation
→ the approach becomes pending
→ a response, request for more information, meeting, rejection, or closure follows.

Meeting:
→ schedule and prepare
→ attend or reschedule where permitted
→ respond to the representative’s questions
→ suitability is evaluated
→ an offer or refusal is issued.

Agreement:
→ review scope, term, commission, exclusivity, and obligations
→ negotiate where supported
→ accept, decline, or allow expiry
→ accepted representation becomes active
→ access, submissions, communication, and financial obligations change.

No response must not create an eternal unresolved action. Use a supported closure or follow-up rule.

9. Active Representation And Ending Representation

Active representative monitors suitable opportunities
→ submits or contacts the player
→ communicates casting responses
→ negotiates appropriate offers
→ tracks agreements and commissionable income
→ relationship history updates.

Ending:
→ player or representative initiates a permitted ending
→ notice and agreement terms are checked
→ new submissions stop at the correct point
→ existing submissions and contracts are handled according to their terms
→ legitimate continuing commissions or obligations remain
→ records are archived
→ the player can pursue other representation without erasing history.

Differentiate agents from managers, lawyers, and publicists wherever those roles are implemented.

PART 4 — THE INDUSTRY’S PROJECT LIFECYCLE

10. Project Development And Opportunity Supply

A project enters the simulation
→ establish format, market, companies, development status, and plausible resources
→ financing or commissioning progresses
→ production may be approved, delayed, revised, or abandoned
→ roles become available at the appropriate stage
→ casting proceeds
→ production begins only when its prerequisites are met.

Not every project reaches production.

An attached performer may be affected by:
- Project cancellation.
- Financing failure.
- Schedule changes.
- Conditional offers.
- Expired holds.
- Changed role requirements.

Generate continuing new projects over time. Preserve old projects as history rather than recycling their identities.

For real properties, distinguish verified starting facts from fictional simulated developments.

11. Discovering And Evaluating A Role

Opportunity is posted, submitted by an agent, or directly offered
→ player sees an understandable summary
→ drill-down reveals role, project, collaborators, dates, location, compensation status, eligibility, and requirements
→ conflicts and uncertainties are identified
→ player chooses to pursue, decline, save for later if supported, or request information.

Availability and eligibility are checked again when circumstances change. Viewing an opportunity does not reserve it.

A deadline:
→ expires once
→ closes the relevant action
→ preserves the record
→ does not leave a live submission button behind.

12. Submission

Player selects permitted materials
→ submission requirements are validated
→ materials and availability are recorded
→ submission is sent in the simulation
→ status becomes pending
→ casting reviews it
→ rejection, audition invitation, further information, hold, or direct offer follows as appropriate.

Repeated presses must not create multiple submissions or charges.

A project withdrawing the role:
→ closes pending submissions
→ explains the change
→ resolves related notices and provisional calendar entries.

PART 5 — AUDITION AND SELECTION LOOPS

13. Self-Tape

Invitation arrives
→ player reviews instructions, material, deadline, and restrictions
→ schedules preparation
→ prepares interpretation, lines, accent, movement, or other relevant requirements
→ records a take
→ reviews or retries within available time and rules
→ selects and submits the take
→ receives confirmation
→ casting evaluates the submitted performance
→ callback, further request, offer, or rejection follows.

Track preparation and performance separately. More attempts must not create unlimited free progression or guaranteed improvement.

Missing the deadline:
→ prevents or flags late submission according to the simulated process
→ closes or changes the opportunity
→ records the actual reason.

14. In-Person Audition

Invitation
→ availability and location check
→ schedule confirmation
→ travel and preparation
→ arrival
→ audition performance
→ response to direction where applicable
→ completion
→ casting decision.

Support justified rescheduling and withdrawal.

Late arrival, absence, document problems, or conflicts must produce appropriate outcomes, not a universal arbitrary penalty.

15. Callback, Chemistry Read, Or Screen Test

Casting requests another stage
→ explain its purpose and requirements
→ identify relevant collaborators
→ schedule preparation and attendance
→ evaluate the applicable performance dimensions
→ update the casting decision
→ offer, further stage, hold, or rejection.

A chemistry read must depend on the interaction being tested, not simply repeat the first audition score.

16. Direct Offer

Offer arrives without an audition
→ establish why the player is a plausible candidate
→ disclose project, role, terms, and conditions
→ check availability and eligibility
→ player reviews and negotiates
→ accepts, declines, or allows expiry.

Do not insert an audition merely because the generic workflow expects one.

17. Rejection And Recovery

Decision occurs
→ communicate the outcome and supported explanation
→ release provisional commitments
→ preserve the submission history
→ apply only justified relationship or reputation effects
→ make legitimate next opportunities available.

Do not treat every rejection as lost skill, damaged reputation, or a player failure. Some outcomes reflect competition or project needs.

PART 6 — AGREEMENTS AND ENGAGEMENT

18. Negotiation

Initial terms
→ player and representative identify negotiable items
→ propose changes
→ other party responds
→ terms may improve, remain unchanged, trade off, expire, or be withdrawn
→ player reviews the latest coherent version
→ signs or declines.

Version the agreement. Do not let screens display one version while the engine executes another.

Negotiation is not unlimited rerolling for better pay.

19. Contract Signing And Booking

Final terms accepted
→ signatures/confirmation complete in the simulation
→ validate conditions precedent
→ establish the engagement
→ reserve definite dates and distinguish them from holds
→ create preparation and production obligations
→ schedule contractual payments
→ update representation, papers, calendar, and career history.

If the agreement is conditional:
→ keep that condition visible
→ do not treat uncertain work as guaranteed income
→ resolve the condition at the appropriate time.

20. Holds, Options, And Conflicts

A hold or option begins
→ record its scope, dates, and terms
→ show how it affects availability
→ a conflicting opportunity arrives
→ evaluate permitted responses
→ release, exercise, negotiate, or decline according to the arrangement.

Expiry:
→ removes restrictions at the correct time
→ notifies the player where useful
→ preserves the history.

Do not treat every hold as a signed paid engagement.

PART 7 — PREPARATION AND PRODUCTION

21. Role Preparation

Booking identifies preparation needs
→ player reviews material and requirements
→ chooses relevant preparation activities
→ spends time and resources
→ preparation changes the dimensions it actually trains
→ readiness feeds the appropriate work tasks.

Include applicable line learning, script analysis, character work, movement, accent, voice, specialist training, fittings, and rehearsal.

Preparation must interact with existing condition and scheduling systems. Avoid making one generic action optimal for every role.

22. Work Travel And Arrival

Confirmed call requires another location
→ establish travel responsibility and arrangements
→ verify documents and work permission
→ calculate travel time and local dates
→ resolve conflicts
→ depart
→ arrive
→ check in to appropriate production accommodation if provided
→ become available for the scheduled call.

Delays or missing authorization:
→ affect arrival and work eligibility
→ notify production
→ create appropriate options and consequences.

Returning home:
→ occurs only after the relevant work or travel arrangement
→ respects travel time
→ updates future availability.

Do not restore unrelated lifestyle-travel engines.

23. Live-Action Production Day

Call sheet arrives or updates
→ player reviews scenes, location, time, preparation, and relevant requirements
→ travels and arrives
→ performs applicable preparation
→ participates in blocking or rehearsal
→ performs scheduled work
→ responds to direction and additional takes
→ handles justified delays or changes
→ wraps
→ work performed is logged
→ pay, condition, relationships, performance, and progress update.

Production does not have to shoot scenes in narrative order.

Do not equate more takes with bad acting automatically. Technical and creative reasons may also require them.

24. Multi-Camera Or Audience-Based Production

Establish the actual format
→ rehearsal and blocking cycle
→ revisions
→ technical preparation
→ recording/taping arrangement
→ audience interaction where applicable
→ pickups
→ wrap and evaluation.

Research format-specific workflows rather than copying the feature-film day.

25. Animation And Voice Work

Material and direction arrive
→ vocal preparation
→ recording session
→ individual or ensemble work as appropriate
→ alternate readings
→ pickups
→ session completion
→ payment and credit conditions update
→ later recording requests can return the engagement to an appropriate active stage.

Do not create physical location shoots, costume continuity, or screen chemistry requirements where the project does not use them.

26. Performance Capture

Specialist requirements
→ preparation and safety checks
→ applicable calibration and rehearsal
→ capture sessions
→ movement/voice collaboration
→ technical or performance retakes
→ wrap
→ later pickups where required.

Evaluate the actual skills used by the work.

27. Documentary-Related Performer Work

Identify whether the engagement is narration, reenactment, hosting, or another legitimate performer role
→ apply the correct preparation and production process
→ record the contribution accurately
→ apply the relevant contract and credit.

Do not confuse documentary participation with playing a fictional scripted character.

28. Changes, Absence, And Replacement

A schedule change, illness, conflict, absence, or performance issue occurs
→ determine its cause and relevant contractual rules
→ communicate the issue
→ offer justified remedies
→ reschedule, excuse, warn, replace, suspend, or end work where applicable
→ update calendar, pay, condition, permissions, and history.

Do not impose replacement automatically after one universal number of mistakes unless a specific established game arrangement supports it.

29. Wrap And Post-Production Obligations

Principal work ends
→ record actual services completed
→ release applicable dates
→ retain genuine continuing obligations
→ finalize outstanding work records
→ calculate owed compensation
→ establish provisional or confirmed credit status.

ADR, pickups, or reshoots:
→ request arrives
→ check contract and availability
→ schedule and complete the work
→ update compensation and project status.

Wrap does not necessarily mean release, final payment, or a guaranteed public credit.

PART 8 — FINANCIAL LOOPS

30. Compensation

A contractual payment trigger occurs
→ calculate the amount due
→ apply applicable deductions and commissions
→ create the payable record
→ payment arrives or becomes overdue
→ deposit into the correct account
→ update statements, net worth, taxes where supported, and history.

A retry or reload must not pay twice.

Expenses:
→ incur an eligible expense
→ submit or record it through the supported process
→ approve, question, or reject
→ reimburse under the applicable arrangement
→ avoid counting reimbursement as earnings incorrectly.

31. Residuals Or Contingent Compensation

The agreement contains an applicable provision
→ qualifying exhibition or revenue event occurs
→ determine entitlement using the supported rules
→ calculate and record the amount
→ pay on the applicable schedule
→ update finances and history.

If required data is unavailable, use an explicit simulation model rather than invented real-world figures.

No provision or qualifying trigger means no automatic payment.

32. Work Ending And Money Still Owed

Cancellation, release, termination, or completion
→ determine work performed and any applicable guarantees
→ remove obligations that no longer apply
→ retain legitimate amounts owed
→ cancel unearned payments correctly
→ settle remaining balances
→ archive the engagement.

Do not delete earned pay when cancelling future work.

PART 9 — RELEASE AND MARKET LOOPS

33. Distribution And Release

Project approaches completion
→ release pathway is established
→ rights and territories are represented appropriately
→ dates and strategy become confirmed or remain provisional
→ publicity obligations are scheduled
→ project releases
→ exhibition and performance reporting begin.

Possible alternatives:
- Delayed release.
- Different distributor.
- Limited release.
- Direct-to-streaming release.
- Shelving.
- No public release.

Do not force every completed project to succeed or release immediately.

34. Publicity

Applicable invitation or obligation
→ check agreement and availability
→ prepare
→ attend or respond
→ complete the activity
→ apply justified visibility, relationship, or contractual consequences
→ record the event.

Publicity should influence exposure where appropriate, not directly manufacture artistic merit or guarantee awards.

35. Weekly Box Office

Release and screen availability establish the opening conditions
→ weekly reporting period closes
→ calculate simulated performance or retrieve the applicable verified historical figure
→ update weekly and cumulative totals
→ rank comparable releases
→ adjust future exhibition where justified
→ continue until the run ends.

Prevent mixing currencies, territories, weekend totals, and full-week totals.

Project success depends on multiple project-level factors, including but not limited to the player’s performance.

36. Weekly Television And Streaming Performance

An episode or batch releases
→ identify the correct reporting window and metric
→ calculate or ingest supported performance
→ update the relevant chart and project history
→ assess sustained performance through the project’s actual model
→ feed renewal or strategy decisions where justified.

Distinguish:
- Hours watched.
- Views.
- Audience estimates.
- Reach.
- Completion.
- Retention.
- Chart rank.

Do not invent private metrics as published facts.

37. Long-Term Project History

Initial release ends or changes phase
→ preserve credits, reviews, metrics, earnings, and awards
→ support legitimate later exhibition or rights events
→ trigger only the obligations actually associated with those events
→ retain the project as part of the player’s permanent professional record.

A later platform appearance must not automatically restart every award-eligibility period.

PART 10 — SERIES CONTINUITY

38. Renewal Decision

A series reaches a decision point
→ evaluate the project’s simulated commercial, creative, contractual, and strategic circumstances
→ renew, delay, conclude, cancel, or leave the decision pending
→ communicate the decision
→ update project plans.

Do not reduce every renewal decision to one viewer threshold.

39. Returning Player

Series renewal or new installment
→ determine whether the player’s character returns
→ inspect options and prior agreements
→ issue an option exercise, negotiation, or new offer where appropriate
→ resolve availability and eligibility
→ agree new terms
→ book the next engagement
→ preserve character and career continuity.

Show renewal does not equal guaranteed player employment.

40. New Character On An Existing Series

A simulated season requires a new role
→ establish its creative and production context
→ define episode commitment and engagement type
→ identify plausible candidates
→ audition or directly offer
→ contract and production
→ release
→ audience response and future character decisions.

Use real-series starting facts accurately. Generated roles and future events must be clearly simulated.

41. Cancellation And Possible Revival

Cancellation
→ close future production plans
→ settle contracts and outstanding pay
→ release applicable holds
→ update representation and availability
→ preserve credits and project history
→ allow the career to continue elsewhere.

A later revival:
→ requires a plausible new project event
→ checks elapsed time, agreements, availability, and character plans
→ creates a new engagement rather than silently restoring the old one.

Rights holder or authorized party considers a festival

→ submit
→ await selection
→ reject, withdraw, or accept selection
→ resolve screening and attendance commitments
→ screen
→ receive applicable festival outcomes
→ update release strategy and history.

43. Festival Attendance

Invitation or obligation
→ check schedule and work logistics
→ confirm attendance
→ travel
→ participate in appropriate screenings, press, or events
→ receive any relevant opportunities or feedback
→ return
→ settle expenses and preserve history.

44. Award Eligibility And Submission

Project’s qualifying activity occurs
→ select the applicable award edition
→ evaluate current rules and category placement
→ confirm authority and submission route
→ submit or enter through the organization’s actual process
→ close eligibility at the appropriate deadline.

Explain ineligibility clearly.

45. Nomination And Voting

Eligible field established
→ apply the researched organization-specific stages
→ produce shortlists where applicable
→ conduct the applicable nomination process
→ notify nominees
→ conduct final voting where applicable
→ lock results for the ceremony.

Do not use a generic public popularity vote for every award.

Nomination or invitation
→ attendance decision
→ schedule and preparation
→ ceremony occurs on the appropriate date
→ category is announced
→ player wins or loses

→ award history updates

For the current scope:

47. Post-Award Career Effects

Result becomes part of the record
→ materials and industry reference update
→ appropriate representatives and casting processes can consider it
→ relevant opportunities or negotiation circumstances may change
→ effects persist without making future work automatic.

Avoid endlessly stacking reputation rewards for the same award or repeatedly announcing it as new.

PART 12 — THE INDUSTRY REFERENCE AND COMMUNICATION LOOPS

48. Interactive Industry Reference

Player opens a project, person, company, season, or episode
→ sees verified information and clearly marked simulation developments
→ follows relationships to related records
→ sees the player’s involvement and relevant history
→ reaches legitimate available actions where appropriate
→ returns without losing navigation context.

Keep public information separate from confidential casting access.

Use original presentation and permitted sources. Do not claim a complete copy of another commercial database.

49. Actionable Mail And Notices

Engine event creates a message
→ message identifies source, context, deadline, and available action
→ player opens it
→ follows the relevant action
→ validation runs
→ successful action resolves or updates the message
→ refusal leaves an accurate explanation
→ expiry closes the action once
→ history remains readable.

Prioritize urgent actionable decisions separately from general updates.

Reading a message must not automatically accept an offer.

50. Ongoing Opportunity And Relationship Loop

Completed work, auditions, professional conduct, and interactions create history
→ relevant systems consult that history
→ new opportunities and responses reflect it where justified
→ player makes new decisions
→ additional history develops.

Avoid universal relationship meters that make every person react identically.

Do not invent private opinions attributed to real people. Generated interactions belong to the simulation.

PART 13 — GLOBAL RULES FOR ALL LOOPS

51. Persistent State And Event Order

Every event needs:
- Stable identity.
- Clear owning system.
- Valid lifecycle state.
- Timestamp and relevant time zone.
- Dependencies.
- Cancellation behavior.
- Duplicate-execution protection.
- Save/load continuity.
- Appropriate history.

Prevent impossible transitions and circular chains that trigger themselves indefinitely.

52. Meaningful Variation

Differentiate experiences through actual project circumstances:
- Format.
- Role.
- Genre combination.
- Collaborators.
- Territory.
- Contract.
- Production scale.
- Schedule.
- Preparation requirements.
- Release route.
- Player history.
- Outcomes.

Do not disguise identical gameplay by swapping names.

Recurring routines are legitimate; accidental duplicate content and history-blind scenarios are not.

53. Transparency And Player Understanding

For every consequential action, the player should understand:
- What they are doing.
- What is required.
- What is known and uncertain.
- What it costs.
- How long it takes.
- What can happen next.
- Why the actual outcome occurred.
- What they can do afterward.

Use natural language, established Title Case rules, summary-first presentation, and deep drill-down access.

Do not expose unnecessary engine jargon in player-facing screens.

54. Research And Accuracy

Research the rules for each supported lifecycle.

Do not claim all markets follow one agreement, timetable, award calendar, or casting process.

Use verified historical facts for real history and explicit simulation models for generated futures.

Record uncertainties rather than guessing real facts.

55. Coverage And Verification

Create a lifecycle matrix mapping every supported loop to:
- Code.
- Entry screen.
- Decisions.
- States and transitions.
- Immediate effects.
- Downstream consumers.
- Timed events.
- Exit and recovery paths.
- Save/load behavior.
- Tests and interactive evidence.

Run representative complete journeys across different formats and circumstances, not only the happy path.

Include:
- Rejection.
- Withdrawal.
- Expiry.
- Cancellation.
- Delay.
- Ineligibility.
- Conflict.
- Partial completion.
- Late payment where supported.
- Interrupted saves.
- Repeated inputs.
- Long careers beyond the original fixed catalog.

Verify that changing relevant abilities or circumstances changes the appropriate outcomes without changing unrelated behavior arbitrarily.

56. Execution And Completion

Implement these loops through complete playable milestones.

Use ONE agent and conserve unnecessary token use through persistent checkpoints, focused file reads, reused valid research, and proportionate testing.

Do not stop at a design document or data catalog.

Continue authorized implementation and fixes until the defined scope is accounted for with evidence, or an actual blocker prevents further work. Continue independent work around blockers.

Do not claim universal perfection or zero possible bugs. Report completed behavior, test results, remaining gaps, and external verification honestly.

Preserve existing scope exclusions and deferred work unless the preceding command explicitly authorizes the relevant functionality.

Keep changes reviewable. Do not merge to main or deploy to production without my explicit instruction.

Begin by mapping these lifecycle requirements against the previous command and existing implementation, then build the missing connected behavior.
