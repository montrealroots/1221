1. READ THIS FIRST

The goal is a text simulation where an acting career takes place in a persistent, geographically coherent world. Accepting a shoot in Australia must eventually put the player at the actual production location, with elapsed travel time, local dates, valid work eligibility, scheduled obligations, accommodation, payment obligations, and consequences carried forward.

This dossier contains 110 selected source records, researched findings, implementation requirements, a coverage matrix, 100 acceptance scenarios, and an efficient handoff brief. Section 27 adds the latest role-matching, weekly-ranking and anonymous-awards requirements. Sources are primarily government departments, unions, awards institutions, festivals, studios, and broadcasters. Some complete rulebooks are linked but have only been inspected in relevant excerpts. They are not all fully transcribed or validated clause by clause.

This is not a certification of 100% worldwide legal or industry accuracy. A source found today may describe an older season, an earlier agreement, or last year's competition. Private negotiations, private ballots, future casting, and unpublished audience data cannot be recovered as public facts. Unsupported rules and values must remain explicit gaps. That is a necessary part of accuracy, not permission to fill them with invented facts.

Use three distinct kinds of content:

- Verified fact: a dated, scoped statement backed by the cited source.
- Proposed simulation behavior: the way SLATE should model an activity or consequence.
- Unresolved rule: required research or interpretation that is not yet sufficiently established.

All uncited implementation behavior below is a design proposal, not a statement of universal industry law.

Strong supporting work in an unsuccessful film can improve professional reputation.

An inexperienced player must not receive a universal zero chance.

Variation must follow context, not arbitrary daily rerolls.
