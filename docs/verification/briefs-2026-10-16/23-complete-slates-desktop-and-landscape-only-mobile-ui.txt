Complete SLATE’s Desktop And Landscape-Only Mobile UI

Redesign and polish the existing interface so it is aligned, organized, readable, and usable throughout the game.

Preserve SLATE’s dark palette, gold accents, established typography direction, and desktop sidebar. Keep all existing gameplay functionality and downstream effects. This is a presentation and interaction pass, not a career expansion or engine rewrite.

Work primarily in one agent/session. Avoid redundant research, repeated full-file reads, and unnecessary full-suite runs. Preserve detailed findings and screenshots in the repository.

1. Establish The Visual Baseline

Start from the latest main branch. Read the handoff and repository instructions.

Inspect every PC and mobile capture under:
docs/verification/career-genre-2026-10-22/captures/

Open the actual images, including the larger Profile and help captures. Then inspect the current built application. Screenshots show an earlier build and must not override newer behavior.

Inventory every current screen, tab, drawer, dialog, dropdown, tooltip, notification, and drill-down panel. Record layout issues and the acceptance checks for each.

2. Mobile Gameplay Must Be Landscape-Only

This is my explicit design decision: do not build a portrait gameplay layout.

When a phone is upright:

* Display “Rotate Your Phone To Play.”
* Explain briefly that SLATE is designed for landscape play.
* Keep gameplay controls behind the orientation screen inaccessible.
* Pause simulation time while the orientation screen is displayed.
* Preserve the current destination, open details, scroll position where practical, unsaved form input, and game progress.
* On returning to landscape, restore the interface and leave the game paused until the player explicitly resumes.
* Do not apply elapsed wall-clock time as a catch-up jump.

Use orientation locking only where supported. Do not require it to work, repeatedly request fullscreen, or assume a browser can force rotation.

Distinguish phones from desktop windows carefully. A narrow desktop window must not automatically become an unusable “rotate your phone” screen. Document the detection approach and provide a usable fallback where device detection is uncertain.

Test rotation during a dialog, a form, an open menu, saving, and paused/running gameplay. Opening the keyboard must not incorrectly trigger the rotation screen.

3. Preserve Desktop, Adapt Mobile Landscape

Desktop:

* Keep the full sidebar and the overall visual character I already like.
* Use consistent page margins, content widths, card alignment, spacing, and section hierarchy.
* Use multiple columns where they improve scanning.
* Constrain long prose to readable widths.

Phone landscape:

* Design for limited vertical space.
* Use a compact, collapsible navigation rail or drawer.
* Let content use the available width when navigation is closed.
* Use two columns where both remain readable; use one within a section when necessary.
* Do not shrink the desktop page, reduce text to fit, or require pinch-zoom.
* Keep essential controls reachable without a tall header consuming the screen.
* Account for notches, safe areas, browser chrome, and changing viewport height.

4. Remove “Your Affairs” From Home

Remove the duplicated “Your Affairs” section from Home on desktop and mobile.

Keep its destinations available through navigation. Preserve actionable notices on Home that lead directly to Money, School, Papers, or another relevant destination.

Do not delete the underlying systems or their information.

5. Organize Home Around The Current Day

Use this information hierarchy:

* Today: greeting, date, time, location, and compact weather.
* Needs Your Decision: genuine decisions, deadlines, and urgent actions.
* Up Next: the next commitment, followed by a concise schedule preview.
* How You Are: condition and access to Look After Yourself.
* Recent Updates: concise summaries with access to the complete history.

Desktop may arrange these in aligned columns. Phone landscape should use its width deliberately.

Avoid repeating the same date, time, location, weather, and destinations across several large cards unless there is a clear purpose.

Keep self-care actions accessible and prioritize relevant needs using existing game state. Do not invent new health logic for the UI.

6. Make Notices Manageable Without Losing History

The captures show long notices and hundreds of unread items dominating Home.

Create a clear distinction between:

* Items requiring a decision or action.
* Informational updates.
* Historical records.

Use concise summaries with sender/context, date, status, and an appropriate action. Open complete messages on demand.

Keep all existing history accessible through a clearly named inbox or updates view. Support suitable filtering and read/unread management.

Do not silently delete notices, mark decisions completed, or dismiss obligations merely to clean up the screen. “Read” and “Resolved” must remain distinct.

Group related messages when their existing data supports it. Avoid repeating the same information in several Home cards.

7. Refine Navigation And Time Controls

Create a deliberate header layout with:

* Navigation and current context.
* Date/time and running/paused status.
* Primary time and self-care controls.
* Secondary actions such as help and saving in suitable locations.

Do not allow controls to wrap into arbitrary crowded rows.

Make the current destination obvious. Keep navigation labels consistent between desktop and mobile.

For tabs:

* Keep the active tab visible.
* Do not leave labels half-cut without an indication that more tabs exist.
* Use clearly scrollable tabs with overflow cues or an accessible section selector where necessary.
* Preserve keyboard and touch access.

If any controls are sticky or fixed, reserve space for them so they never cover content, actions, or focused fields.

8. Make Dense Pages Easy To Explore

Career offers and engagements:

* Lead with project/role, current status, dates, compensation summary, deadline, and next action.
* Use expandable sections for requirements, agreement details, schedule, payment records, and history.
* Keep critical warnings visible before acceptance.
* Show payment schedules as structured records rather than repeated paragraphs where possible.
* Preserve access to every existing detail.

Eligibility:

* Clearly distinguish current permission from historical assessments.
* Label an older assessment “Eligibility When Accepted” or equivalent.
* Put a current restriction or release reason ahead of an earlier approval.

Calendar:

* Preserve individual events and their actions.
* Let selecting a date reveal its agenda.
* Group recurring rehearsals or calls into expandable schedules.
* Keep paydays, conflicts, and important deadlines easy to identify.
* Avoid forcing the player through a long repeated list to find one event.

Profile, Money, Papers, School, and help:

* Apply the same summary-to-detail approach where appropriate.
* Do not hide essential actions inside unclear nested sections.
* Help should open the relevant topic first; full reference content remains available.
* Preserve meaningful drill-down links and clear return navigation.

9. Apply A Consistent Layout System

Standardize:

* Page gutters and maximum widths.
* Spacing between and within sections.
* Heading hierarchy and text sizes.
* Label/value alignment.
* Button heights, padding, and action grouping.
* Card borders, corners, and backgrounds.
* Icon size and alignment.
* Form layout and validation messages.
* Empty, loading, disabled, error, and expanded states.

No:

* Text or controls bleeding outside containers.
* Names and balances squeezed into vertical stacks.
* Accidental horizontal page scrolling.
* Distorted icons or stretched controls.
* Overlapping sticky elements.
* Clipped dialogs or unreachable action buttons.
* Arbitrary fixed heights that cut off content.

Use component-specific overflow handling for genuinely wide tables. Do not hide defects with global overflow clipping.

Preserve visible keyboard focus indicators. Do not mistake a focus outline for a malformed border.

10. Accessibility And Content Integrity

Keep body text readable and controls comfortably usable by touch. Aim for at least 44-by-44 CSS-pixel touch targets for primary interactive controls.

Verify keyboard navigation, focus order, dialog focus management, accessible names, selected states, and contrast.

Support browser zoom and larger text without losing actions or information. Do not disable zoom.

Use natural, concise wording and established Title Case conventions. Do not rewrite accurate gameplay rules merely to shorten a card.
