SLATE — Professional Career Foundation And The First Individual Agent

Build the next chapter of SLATE on the current game: the transition from drama student to professional actor.

Start with ONE fully developed individual talent agent and a complete playable career loop. Depth, continuity, and meaningful consequences come before adding more agents or companies.

This is an implementation request. Research, build, test, debug, and provide evidence.

1. START FROM THE CURRENT REPOSITORY

Read the repository instructions, latest main, handoff, paused Brief 18, and existing school, attributes, genres, evaluation, banking, papers, calendar, relationships, and writing systems.

Confirm the actual current state before editing.

Keep Brief 18 paused except for specific dependencies required to make this career phase function correctly. Identify any such fixes and why they are necessary.

Preserve existing saves and work. Do not restore the deleted industry engine wholesale or bring back unrelated personal-life systems.

2. RESEARCH THE CAREER TRANSITION

Research current authoritative information about:

- Yale’s acting program, degree requirements, graduation timing, showcase, and professional-work restrictions.

- Whether early graduation, accelerated completion, or another officially permitted route exists.

- Representation submissions, referrals, meetings, agreements, and the chosen agent’s professional role.

- Relevant work authorization, representation requirements, and payment rules for the location and work being simulated.

Use official school publications, agency pages, applicable government sources, and relevant professional organizations.

Record sources, dates, and uncertainties. Verify facts rather than copying assumptions from previous prompts.

Separate:

- Verified real-world information.

- Explicit gameplay assumptions.

- Fictional conversations and simulated decisions.

Never invent an unpublished policy and present it as a real school’s or agent’s rule.

3. SUPPORT DISTINCT SCHOOL-TO-CAREER PATHWAYS

Build a transition that recognizes:

A. Completing the degree normally.

B. Seeking representation while still enrolled, where permitted.

C. Pursuing professional work while enrolled, subject to applicable school rules, scheduling, and work authorization.

D. Leaving school before graduating.

E. Graduating early, ONLY if the school’s verified rules permit it.

Early graduation must not be awarded simply because attributes are high. Check coursework, required terms, attendance, standing, and all applicable requirements.

If Yale does not permit early graduation, clearly explain that in the game. Do not manufacture an accelerated Yale degree. Keep legitimate career alternatives available without mislabeling them as graduation.

If the published rules do not settle the question, record the uncertainty and leave that route unavailable pending verification while completing the other routes.

Seeking representation must not automatically withdraw the player from school. Signing with an agent must not automatically count as employment or permission to work.

4. PRESERVE THE PLAYER’S EXISTING LIFE

Carry forward:

- Identity and background.

- Acting attributes and techniques.

- Genre familiarity and experience.

- School standing and completed coursework.

- Production history and actual roles performed.

- Relationships and relevant feedback.

- Money, accounts, obligations, and documents.

- Health, readiness, calendar, and existing commitments.

Do not reset progression when the career begins.

Differentiate school credits, practice exercises, self-tapes, professional credits, and unreleased work. A practice brief must not become a professional screen credit.

5. BUILD A DURABLE PROFESSIONAL PROFILE

Create an actor profile and portfolio that can support submissions and representation.

Include, where applicable:

- Professional name and relevant contact information within the simulation.

- Training and truthful education status.

- School and professional credits, clearly distinguished.

- Relevant skills.

- Headshots.

- Résumé.

- Reel or selected performance material.

- Availability and location.

- Work eligibility.

Every component must have a purpose and an effect.

Materials must have provenance: where they came from, when they were created, what they contain, and whether the player can actually use them. Do not create footage automatically from a performance that was never recorded.

Make creating or updating necessary materials playable, with clearly stated time and costs where modeled.

Keep permanent credits and important portfolio assets separate from the rolling detailed evaluation history. They must survive long careers and save/reload without making saves grow uncontrollably.

Give the player practical feedback about missing or unsuitable materials.

6. CONNECT THE SCHOOL SHOWCASE

Research and implement the appropriate school showcase or professional introduction process.

Connect it to:

- Eligibility and timing.

- Material selection.

- Preparation.

- Scheduling.

- Performance.

- Recorded feedback.

- Follow-up opportunities.

Use existing attributes, genre knowledge, preparation, and condition systems to evaluate the work.

Do not guarantee that the chosen agent attends or offers representation. If attendance cannot be verified for a real person, treat their participation as a clearly fictional event in the simulation.

A strong showcase can improve opportunity without guaranteeing a contract.

7. IMPLEMENT ONE INDIVIDUAL AGENT IN DEPTH

Do not populate a large agency directory or introduce several shallow agents.

Use an individual agent already selected by the owner if one exists. Otherwise, research and select one real, currently active agent appropriate to this starting career path, explaining the choice.

Verify their current:

- Name.

- Agency affiliation.

- Office or market.

- Department and professional focus.

- Public submission or contact policies, where available.

Represent the agency only as deeply as needed to support this individual’s actual function.

If suitable information is unavailable, choose another appropriate agent or document a clearly fictional simulation assumption. Do not invent a real person’s client list, private policies, personality, or contract terms.

All outreach happens inside the game. Do not contact real agents, submit real materials, or send real messages.

8. BUILD THE COMPLETE RELATIONSHIP

Implement the relevant stages:

Discovery → eligibility and materials check → submission or introduction → waiting and follow-up → response → meeting → decision → agreement → active representation.

Allow meaningful outcomes:

- No response.

- A request for more material.

- A rejection with only as much explanation as the simulation supports.

- A meeting.

- An offer.

- The player declining an offer.

- Representation ending.

Do not turn persistence into an exploit where repeated submissions guarantee acceptance.

Track genuine relationship history:

- Prior contact.

- What was requested.

- What the player supplied.

- Meetings kept or missed.

- Promises.

- Offers and decisions.

- Opportunities handled.

- Relevant successes and problems.

Stable event identities must prevent resolved incidents and completed requests from reappearing as new.

9. MAKE REPRESENTATION DECISIONS EXPLAINABLE

An agent’s interest should consider supported factors such as:

- Quality and relevance of submitted work.

- Training and experience.

- Fit with the agent’s professional focus.

- Availability.

- Existing roster needs, explicitly modeled as simulation data.

- Reliability demonstrated through actual events.

Avoid a single universal “agent likes you” score deciding everything.

High attributes should produce high-quality work under the existing mastery rules when preparation and conditions are appropriate. They must not guarantee representation or booking.

Distinguish:

- Performance quality.

- Suitability for a particular role.

- Representation fit.

- Availability and eligibility.

- The final competitive decision.

10. IMPLEMENT REVIEWABLE AGREEMENTS

Before acceptance, clearly present the agreement’s:

- Representation scope and territory.

- Covered work.

- Exclusivity, where applicable.

- Commission and what earnings it applies to.

- Duration.

- Responsibilities.

- Termination provisions.

- Any relevant continuing commission obligations.

Use verified requirements and explicitly identified simulation terms. Do not claim to reproduce the real agent’s private agreement.

The player must be able to inspect, accept, or decline.

Accepting must change actual representation state and affect later opportunities and payments. A contract must not be decorative text.

11. COMPLETE ONE PROFESSIONAL WORK LOOP

Build enough supporting industry structure for this sequence to work:

Opportunity → submission → audition or self-tape → optional callback → offer → contract decision → preparation → scheduled work → performance evaluation → payment → commission where owed → lasting career record.

Do not require every opportunity to use every stage.

Each opportunity needs:

- A clearly identified project and role.

- Requirements.

- Relevant genre and performance demands.

- Dates and location.

- Submission or audition deadline.

- Compensation terms.

- Eligibility restrictions.

- A meaningful outcome.

Use clearly fictional projects for initial playable opportunities unless a real project’s details are verified and its fictional casting scenario is clearly identified.

Do not imply that a real production is actually hiring or that a real person made an offer.

The first agent must actively participate in this loop: finding or receiving suitable opportunities, submitting the player where appropriate, communicating outcomes, and handling offers within their role.

12. KEEP AN UNSIGNED PATH AVAILABLE

The player must be able to pursue appropriate opportunities without representation where the opportunity permits it.

Representation should change access, support, and obligations—not unlock every possible acting action.

Do not make an agent responsible for every career task. Distinguish the agent’s role from casting, production, school, and the player.

13. CONNECT EXISTING ENGINES

Use the existing systems rather than building disconnected duplicates.

- Calendar: deadlines, meetings, auditions, rehearsals, and work.

- School: permission, attendance, leave, withdrawal, and graduation.

- Attributes and genres: preparation and work quality.

- Health and readiness: relevant performance and scheduling consequences.

- Banking: receivables, actual payments, commissions, and balances.

- Papers: work eligibility and supported tax treatment.

- Relationships: professional history and reliability.

- Journey/profile: credits, milestones, and development.

Every interactive field and action must affect something identifiable, provide necessary information, or navigate to a functioning feature. Explain connections through useful drill-down views.

14. FIX REQUIRED DEPENDENCIES

Before career appointments rely on the clock, resolve relevant time-jump and dialog problems from Brief 18.

Before career earnings are paid, verify that payment and commission accounting:

- Uses the correct accounts and currency.

- Distinguishes promised money from received money.

- Applies deductions only when due.

- Prevents duplicate payments through reloads and time jumps.

- Handles delayed or insufficient payments where included.

- Connects to applicable reporting without treating all income identically.

Before affected characters accept work, connect actual work eligibility to the new employment record. Remove any obsolete assumption that no professional work exists after school.

Fix only dependencies required for this phase; keep unrelated backlog items clearly tracked.

15. USE THE NEW WRITING STANDARD

Preserve natural language, concise summaries, optional detail, and established Title Case rules.

Give the agent a consistent fictionalized game voice without claiming to imitate the real person.

Messages must reflect actual events, requests, decisions, and history.

Avoid repeated congratulations, generic rejection loops, invented performance observations, and fake novelty. Keep control names stable.

Provide contextual tutorials for each new feature, explaining what to do, why it matters, and what it affects.

16. BUILD AN EXTENSIBLE FOUNDATION

Use reusable structures for agents, agreements, opportunities, auditions, engagements, payments, and credits.

Fully populate and verify ONE agent first.

Future agents should be addable with distinct data and supported behavior, without copying the whole engine. Do not populate empty profiles or advertise unavailable relationships as playable.

17. VERIFY THROUGH COMPLETE PLAYTHROUGHS

Write acceptance criteria before implementation, then verify:

- A graduating student carrying their history into the career.

- An enrolled student pursuing a permitted opportunity.

- A player leaving without a degree.

- Early graduation correctly allowed, refused, or unavailable according to verified policy.

- An incomplete submission and its remedy.

- Rejection and a valid later attempt.

- A meeting missed or rescheduled.

- An offer accepted and declined.

- Representation ending according to its terms.

- An unsigned opportunity.

- An audition, callback where applicable, booking, completed work, and payment.

- Scheduling conflicts.

- Relevant work-authorization restrictions.

- Commission accounting.

- Save/reload at critical transitions.

- Migration of an existing graduate’s save.

Ensure high skill improves performance without guaranteeing casting, and that suitable opportunities remain possible for developing actors.

Check desktop, mobile, keyboard navigation, accessible labels, and tutorial accuracy.

Reuse existing regression tests. Add meaningful tests for new mechanics and defects. Run the repository’s required full verification gate after the changes stabilize.

18. DELIVER EVIDENCE

Provide:

- The selected agent and why they fit.

- Research sources, dates, and explicit simulation assumptions.

- The school-policy findings, including early graduation.

- A coverage matrix linking actions to state changes and downstream effects.

- The exact tested commit.

- Actual player-facing examples.

- Complete playthrough results.

- Tests run, failures found, and fixes made.

- Any remaining limitations.

- Accurate merge and deployment status.

Continue through implementation, debugging, and verification. Do not stop after adding an agent card, writing a plan, or producing sample dialogue.

The milestone is one complete, coherent professional beginning: my existing student becomes an emerging actor, develops a real in-game relationship with one individual agent, pursues work, experiences consequences, and carries every meaningful outcome forward.

Do not claim perfection from test counts. Finish the defined scope and show that it works.
