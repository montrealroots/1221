How This Works 
The play asks for Scene Study, Emotional Depth, Character Work and Script Analysis, as its kinds of play do (Drama, Domestic Drama). The chair reads every student for it the same way: their fit for the play, with a fifth of it scaled by how much of themselves they delivered in their runs since the last casting, and then a balance for who had the big parts in the School’s last productions. The best read gets the biggest part written for them.
Your 100% delivered comes from The Seagull, Yale Rep, 2 nights (100%, counted as 2 nights); Carlotta Festival Of New Plays, a School production, 4 nights (100%, counted as 4 nights). A Cabaret or Summer run counts half as much as a School production, each run up to eight nights, and a night missed when you weren’t in hospital counts as nothing delivered. How the nights were judged doesn’t count here: that’s in your work.

Sources And Estimates Each way of playing shifts what counts most in your work toward what it is, and uses the abilities shown. It moves the balance without adding to it, so at full ability every choice that fits reaches 100; below that, a choice that leans on what you do well shows you at your best. A choice the material or the director doesn’t take holds the qualities it leans on to 85% of themselves. A choice that fits can still suit the scene or go against it, by what the scene is (its genre, its reader, its tones and turns, its features and language, its circumstances, the medium and the reading you’ve taken): one that suits it is easier to make work and needs less rehearsal, one that goes against it is harder and needs more, and at full ability either reaches 100.
