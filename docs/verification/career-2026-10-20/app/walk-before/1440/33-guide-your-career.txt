CHAPTER 13 OF 20
Your Career

Your professional profile, the headshots, résumé and reel a submission sends, and your work’s pay and unions.

Career, in the menu under Your Work, shows where you stand as a professional actor. Its Overview says what training you have, when you’re free for work, whether you can work in the US, and what your portfolio is missing.

The Portfolio tab holds what a submission sends: your profile, headshots, a résumé and a reel. Each says where it came from, when it was made and what’s in it. Each button says its time and cost before you press it.

Your Profile shows the name you work under. To change it, type a name under Professional Name and press Use This Name. Leave it blank and press it to go back to your own name. Your résumé uses the name you choose.

Book A Headshot Session takes four hours and costs about $700.00, both estimated. Photographers work by day, so a session starts between 9 AM and 4 PM. Headshots more than two years old read as out of date.

Make Your Résumé takes an hour. It lists your name, training, unions, credits and special skills from your record, with School credits and professional credits apart. Practice, practice briefs and self-tapes are never credits.

Once something new is on your record, such as a part played or a skill grown, your résumé reads as behind. Press Update Your Résumé to bring it up to date.

A reel is cut only from recorded work: self-tapes of practice briefs, the School’s filmed monologues once they’re posted at the end of your last term, and screen work once it’s released. Choose up to four pieces under Recorded Work To Cut From and press Cut A Reel. It takes two hours and about $250.00, both estimated.

A night on stage and a class piece aren’t recorded, so they can’t go on a reel. A voice brief is a sound recording, so it can’t either.

The Work tab lists work you’ve booked: its dates, its calls on your calendar and its pay. Pay is promised when you accept the work, and paid the Thursday after each week of it with tax withheld. If you’re represented, it goes through your agency, which takes its commission and passes the rest on within three business days. Each payment says what was taken and what reached you, and Money shows it land.

Your permission to work is read for each date of the work when you book it, and signing with an agency changes none of it. On an F-1 while you’re a student, outside acting work isn’t open to you. On OPT after your degree, each day of work counts as a day worked.

Your degree makes you a member of Actors’ Equity, and $600.00 of its initiation fee is taken then. A SAG-AFTRA role on a TV show or a theatrical or Low Budget film lets you join SAG-AFTRA. From the 30th day after that work began, SAG-AFTRA work needs you to be a member: press Join SAG-AFTRA on the Work tab first.

If a button is grayed out, the line under it says why: you’re busy then, your bank can’t cover it, or your résumé already has everything on your record.

Read “Representation” next. It opens the agent’s panel on the Career screen: how to reach him, and the agreement.

The Details

A technique goes on your résumé as a special skill once it reaches 60: accents and dialects, stage combat, singing, dance, improvisation, clowning, voice-over work, classical text work, comic timing, intimacy work and on-camera work. Once it’s mastered, the résumé says so.

When you graduate, the degree makes you a member of Actors’ Equity, as the School’s Bulletin says, and your profile and résumé show it from that day.

Your record keeps the last six of each kind of material you make, and every credit for good.

Commission is taken only on work you contracted while represented: 10% at most, 5% on an Equity contract paying $525.00 a week or less, and on SAG-AFTRA work only what’s paid over scale. A contract made while an agreement ran still carries it after the agreement ends.

Equity’s initiation fee is $1,800.00, paid within three years, and its basic dues $88.00 each May and November. Joining SAG-AFTRA costs about $3,244.07, read from a search engine’s copy of its pages. Each union takes working dues from union pay.
