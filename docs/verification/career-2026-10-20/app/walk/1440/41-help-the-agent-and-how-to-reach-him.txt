The Agent And How To Reach Him
Show Less

The Representation tab holds the game’s one agent, Jordan Parente, an agent in Stewart Talent’s New York legit department. He’s a real person, and everything he says and decides in the game is invented: his panel says so first, before anything else.

Stewart Talent’s page for New York legit submissions says “We do not accept unsolicited submissions at this time.”, and the game keeps to it. His office takes your materials only through the School’s follow-up after the showcase, or a referral from someone you’ve worked with. He never gets in touch first.

Where To Find It
Career, Representation.
When You Can Use It
From the guide’s chapter “Representation”.
Show Me Where
What To Press
Open Career, then the Representation tab.
Read the notice at the top of his panel, then press I’ve Read This.
Under Your Materials For Him, press Check Your Materials. If something’s missing, the line under it says what, and where to make it.
Under How To Reach Him, press Ask For A Referral beside a finished job, or wait for the School’s follow-up after the showcase.
Press Send Your Materials.
What You Need First
The notice read.
Headshots and a résumé: a check refuses without them.
For a referral, a finished professional job whose work was judged Strong or better, once for each job.
For a send, an introduction: the School’s follow-up or a referral.
TIME AND MONEY
Time
None
Money
None
What Happens
STRAIGHT AWAY
Sending moves Where You Stand With Him to Materials Sent, and his office’s answer is on the way.
LATER ON
His office answers in about two weeks: a request for more, a meeting or a pass, each as a notice. Or no answer comes, and after six weeks that counts as no.
After a pass, his office looks again only after a year; after no answer or a declined offer, six months. Then it looks only at something new: a new credit, a better reel or a referral.
READING THE RESULT
Where You Stand With Him names the step you’re at, and What’s Next says what to do about it.
A referral comes from the casting office of a job you’ve finished, never from him, and he never writes to you first.
How It Connects
COMES FIRST
Headshots: A check refuses an approach with no headshots, since every submission starts with one.
Your Résumé: A check refuses an approach with no résumé, and a résumé behind your record is one thing his office can ask for.
CHANGES LATER
His Office’s Answers And The Meeting: Sending your materials puts his office’s answer on the way, about two weeks on.
WHAT LEADS HERE
Why It Went This Way: Each answer his office gives keeps its reasons here.
The Agreement: Accepting, declining and the agreement’s end each go in History With His Office.
What To Do Next
Keep your materials current while you wait: his office may ask for more.
If You Can’t
Stewart Talent’s page for New York legit submissions says: “We do not accept unsolicited submissions at this time.”
Check your materials first: a referral passes them on.
A referral needs work judged Strong or better in the game.
In the game a new approach needs something new for it to consider: a new credit, a better reel or a referral.
MISTAKES, AND PUTTING THEM RIGHT
Sending again too soon after a pass. The refusal says the date his office looks again from, and what it would look at.
What Decides It
Your Materials For Him
Your headshots, résumé and reel, checked as a submission would be
A Referral
A finished job’s work, judged Strong or better (estimated)
An Answer
Usually within two weeks; no answer in six weeks counts as no (estimated)
A Worked Example

A graduate with headshots and a résumé, and a finished job judged Strong.

Press I’ve Read This, then Check Your Materials.
Press Ask For A Referral beside the job.
Press Send Your Materials.

What comes of it. The job’s casting office refers you, your materials go to his office, and its answer comes about two weeks later.
